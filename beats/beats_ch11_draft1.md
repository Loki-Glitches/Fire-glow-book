# Beat Plan — Ch.11 "The Roof" (Elizabeth POV, Calm unlocks)

POV: Elizabeth, third-limited. No access to Lucifer's interior, no telepathic Azrael exchange (none needed — Azrael is off-page per the unit plan's own scene list). Setting: Elizabeth's house, evening into sunset, presumably an evening soon after Ch.10's dinner (per the from-09-to-10 handoff note: "the small shift is already sitting between them, unremarked" — no new goodbye-exchange callback needed, just continuity of routine).

## Double-layer check
- **Surface job:** Lucifer keeps showing up and lingering; Elizabeth calls it out; she needles him about his powers; he shows her strength (twice); they watch a sunset from the roof.
- **Real job:** The mission is quietly finished being used as his cover for being here — this chapter is where presence stops needing a justification. Calm arrives as the visible proof: for the first time he is somewhere with nothing left to extract from it, and it makes him still instead of watchful.

## Sequence

1. **Opening — porch, early evening, routine established.** Cold open into an ordinary visit, no fanfare about it being different from Ch.10 (per handoff note). Small talk/action beat to open (world/action texture since this is Elizabeth's chapter and small talk is allowed for her). Establish he's been coming by "for reasons that got thinner each time" is Lucifer's own Ch.10 line — mirror it from her side without repeating the phrase: she's noticed the visits don't seem to serve whatever errand he says he's running.

2. **She names the shift.** Not accusatory — plain, observational, consistent with her "notice, don't chase" pattern but this time she says it aloud instead of filing it away (a small escalation of her own, matching his new pattern of saying true things). She says something like: he's not just asking about her, he's building a picture of her whole life — the questions have changed shape.

3. **He presses her — new behavior.** He asks what she means, what she noticed, specifically. (New: pre-Calm he never asked for elaboration on an observation about himself — Ch.8's forehead-bump question and Ch.10's "you do that a lot" were him making observations, not soliciting them.) She gives a partial answer — mentions she found him interesting early, doesn't go deeper. Consistent with "Elizabeth is not calculating," open but not oversharing.

4. **Turnabout — her question.** She trades: he gets to dig, she gets one back. "What can you actually do? You're an angel." Play this as genuinely curious, not a gotcha — she's earned the right to ask directly (style guide Exception 1) and she's wanted to ask this since Ch.8's disclosure.
   - He answers sincerely: no real power. He believes it completely (Bond Tier Awareness — he's never tried).
   - She doesn't accept it, keeps pushing: "Then what CAN you do?" — genuine, a little exasperated/amused, not hostile.

5. **The first attempt (private uncertainty, legible only externally).** He looks for the path of least resistance to get her off the subject — picks something physically modest on the porch (a subject to choose carefully: something heavy enough to prove a point but small/contained enough that a visible failure would be recoverable and not embarrassing — e.g., a big planter, a stack of paving stones, a porch bench, a section of railing). Externally-visible tell of uncertainty: a beat of stillness/stillness-before-moving that reads oddly precise for something this small, maybe he takes a second longer than the task should need, or tests his grip before committing weight to it — something Elizabeth can actually observe and register as slightly off without being able to name it. Then he does it — moves/lifts it with no visible effort. It works.
   - His reaction: unremarkable, immediate return to normal, doesn't linger on it, moves the conversation past it himself.
   - Her reaction: audibly/visibly startled — more than he is. This is the chapter's first hard evidence for her that "angel" isn't just a word.
   - Keep dialogue in this beat spare — he doesn't explain the mechanism (doesn't understand it fully himself, and wouldn't over-explain even if he did).

6. **Sunset noticed.** Natural transition — light changing, one of them clocks it. She wants to see it properly, says something like the porch roof blocks the view / she's always wanted to see it from up top. This should read as an offhand idea of hers, not a staged setup — consistent with "Scene Completeness," the destination (the roof) needs an actual reason to be reached, not just narrated into being.

7. **Getting up — second use, casual.** He gets them both onto the roof via a boost/lift, framed as offhand now that the first attempt already worked. NOT a jump, NOT flight — leverage/boost/climb-assist only. Concretely: he could brace himself against the porch post/railing and lift her up to the roof edge first, then follow himself in a single controlled vault up the porch post — a strength-assisted climb, feet leaving the ground only as far as an athletic person clearing a ledge, not an aerial motion. Keep the verbs grounded: "boosted," "caught," "pulled up," "climbed" — avoid anything that reads as soaring or gliding.

8. **On the roof — the sunset.** Lucifer is visibly mesmerized — this is Elizabeth's external observation only: stillness, attention fixed on the horizon, none of his usual scanning. She's never seen him like this and it registers to her as new, funny even — she giggles at him, at the sight of him being caught by something this ordinary.

9. **He leans back, closes his eyes.** Not sleep — rest. A slight, real smile surfaces (FIRST unguarded smile of the book — do not name it as significant, do not have her analyze it). She studies him for a beat.
   - **Calm unlock beat:** one quiet internal note from her, then move on immediately per the writer restriction. E.g., something like noticing he isn't watching anything, isn't listening for anything, isn't waiting to be needed — just resting — and that this is the first time she's seen him do nothing at all. Keep it to a sentence or two, not a paragraph, and do not let her land on a word like "calm" or "different" that reads as an explicit label — let the image (eyes closed, smile, stillness) carry it.

10. **Down from the roof.** Mirror the up-trip briefly (he helps her down — quick, doesn't need to be elaborate) — Scene Completeness requires this beat exist, not skip straight to the porch/goodbye.

11. **Goodbye — subtly different.** Neither names anything. Something in how the goodbye plays differs from prior chapters' goodbyes — maybe less transactional, a beat that lingers half a second longer, something exchanged in tone rather than word choice. Keep it small and let the reader feel the shift more than see it stated.

## Craft constraints checklist going in
- 1,000-word floor — content plan above should clear it comfortably through genuine scene beats (two strength-use scenes, dialogue, sunset, roof descent, goodbye) without padding.
- No explicit physical description (outfit/expression only).
- No hedge phrases in narration ("as if," "as though," "in a way," a stand-in "like X").
- No mechanism similes.
- No overwritten posture/expression/action — state beats plainly.
- Attribution: action-beat tags preferred; bare dialogue-verb tags sparingly only.
- Comma vs. question mark before tags: match clause's own mood.
- No wings, no flight, nothing that reads as flight — strength/leverage verbs only for both uses of the ability.
- Lucifer is not all-knowing — he only knows what's been established (does not know or assert bond tier facts to himself in a way Elizabeth could perceive — moot anyway since we have no interior access to him this chapter).
- Mission-fade: no reference to tracking Eve, scanning, or the mission framing anywhere in this chapter — from this chapter on it's gone. Do not reintroduce it even as a contrast/callback.
- Sam is not present in this chapter per the unit plan's scene list (porch/roof, just the two of them) — no need to force him in; a brief, incidental mention is fine if natural (e.g., he's inside, or out) but not a scene requirement.
- No smile from Lucifer before this chapter — this is the first one, written once, simply, on the roof.
- Every chapter changes/introduces a character AND advances plot: Calm's unlock (character change) + Power Bond becoming real/first physical evidence for Elizabeth + mission-fade beginning (plot advance).
