# Beat Plan — liz-part1-ch1 (draft 1)

Narrator: Elizabeth. First person, present tense. Structural mirror of "Hell": long ordinary stretch, sudden disorienting break, end shaken-but-resolved (not scared off).

## Part A — Ordinary morning (runs long, genuinely mundane)

1. Elizabeth up before Sam, doing a small chore (coffee, dishes left from last night, feeding something/watering something) — establishes house routine without narrating it as routine. Thinks a stray practical thought, not a weighty one.
2. Sam comes through — warm, teasing exchange, caretaker dynamic (he checks on her lightly, she deflects, doesn't burden him with whatever she's carrying). Keep dialogue short, no standalone tags. A beat that shows she carries something unnamed — a flicker only, not dwelt on, no label for it.
3. She makes a list / decides on an errand for the day (pharmacy pickup — something small and plausible, like a headache/concussion-follow-up item, tied lightly to her established bump-on-forehead baseline, kept casual not medical-drama). Sam offers to come, she waves him off — establishes she's about to be alone.
4. She heads out. Ordinary town texture: heat of late summer, the square's layout (ring road, crosswalks, fountain, benches), maybe a small normal interaction with someone incidental (a shop person, a neighbor) — nothing supernatural, nothing portentous. Let this breathe — this is the "boring on purpose" stretch.
5. Pharmacy stop — brief, mundane, a small practical thought or mild impatience. She finishes and decides to cut back through a quieter route (alley/lot behind the storefronts) instead of the square — her own choice, not pushed by anything ominous.

## Part B — The crisis (sudden, disorienting)

6. Alone on the quiet cut-through. Something off in her peripheral vision down the alley/lot — pulls her attention before she can name why. She stops.
7. She sees, at a distance: one man against several others, already mid-fight. No weapon she can identify in his hand. It doesn't look staged — too fast, too real, bodies hitting things wrong. She can't make out faces or hear words, just impact and breathing distance away.
8. He's outnumbered badly and yet not losing — and that's the part that snags wrong in her head, more than the violence itself. She doesn't move closer. A beat of her own fear/instinct arguing with her curiosity (her "drawn to broken things" habit flickering even here, but she doesn't act on it).
9. The fight ends — ambiguous: she loses clear sight of exactly how for a second (flinch, looking away, bad angle) and when she looks back he's the only one standing, unbothered, and the others are... down, or gone — she can't actually confirm which, and doesn't get closer to check.
10. She backs away, quiet, doesn't run until she's around the corner / far enough. Unsure whether he saw her — no eye contact she can swear to, no dialogue exchanged, no name for him in her head because she has nothing to call him.

## Part C — Aftermath, shaken but resolved

11. Walk home faster than normal, replaying fragments, nothing coheres into an explanation. Sam or house is near at hand but she doesn't blurt it out immediately — deliberate withholding consistent with "doesn't burden him with her own weight," not explained/labeled on the page, just shown.
12. Ends on resolve, not fear: something stubborn in her — she's not about to let one bad, unexplainable thing rearrange her own town around her. A small concrete action showing this (e.g., she still does something ordinary/defiant — finishes unpacking the pharmacy bag, goes back out to the porch, whatever's smallest and truest) rather than a stated moral. No decision to investigate further — that's reserved for later chapters.

## Notes for voice compliance
- Keep reflections 1–3 sentences, triggered by scene specifics.
- Vary "I" sentence openers; no more than two in a row.
- No elapsed-time markers.
- No name for the fighting man anywhere on the page.
- Watch figure-of-speech budget: max 3 for the whole chapter, from Elizabeth's own idiom (plain, town/domestic, not literary).
- Sam appears only in Part A/C, not in the crisis itself.
