# Beat Plan — Ch.12 "The Dress" (Lucifer POV, Beauty/Desire flickers)

POV: Lucifer, third-limited. Narration uses real names — **Lucifer** and **Azrael** — per the Identity Checker rule (narration always matches the POV character's own knowledge, regardless of who's present; confirmed precedent in Ch.4/Ch.8/Ch.10, e.g. Ch.10's opening paragraph narrates "Azrael was always waiting..."). Spoken dialogue aloud in front of James, Claire, or the shop manager uses the aliases **Adrian** / **Tristan**. Real names aloud only if the brothers are alone together with zero unaware humans in earshot (the opening beat, before James/Claire arrive).

Setting: the town, daytime, the morning/day after Ch.11's roof scene. No Elizabeth on the page at all — hard restriction.

## Double-layer check
- **Surface job:** Lucifer reports back vaguely, gets pulled into wedding-dress duty with Claire while Azrael gets pulled into venue-scouting with James; Lucifer horse-trades Azrael into going; dress shopping happens; a price gets negotiated down.
- **Real job:** Beauty is entering him for the first time as something disconnected from utility — not through Elizabeth, through an object, a piece of craftsmanship, so it can't be mistaken for romantic feeling yet. The scene also quietly cements Lucifer's new fluency with truth-twisting as a light, almost enjoyable tool rather than a defensive one — a small piece of Calm's aftermath showing up as ease rather than survival.

## Sequence

1. **Opening — Lucifer and Azrael alone, day after the roof.** A public spot in town but empty enough that they're effectively alone (a bench, a corner near the hardware store, wherever Azrael has taken to waiting). Real names spoken aloud. Azrael asks how last night went; Lucifer gives the shape of it, not the substance — vague, short, doesn't elaborate on the roof, the smile, or Calm. Azrael doesn't press for detail but clocks something different in how Lucifer is standing/talking — a new looseness, unhurried. Neither names it. Keep this brief — a handful of lines, not a scene.

2. **James and Claire arrive.** Warm, easy, curious about the brothers (per unit plan) — genuine town-familiarity texture (style guide § "the town dynamic" — Adrian/Tristan are still strangers who get noticed, welcomed, asked about). Switch to aliases the instant they're in earshot. This becomes a 4-person scene — apply the 3+ Speaker Rule (anchor speaker at least every other line via name/action beat).

3. **The asks land in the same conversation.** Claire invites Lucifer to come help her try on dresses (she trusts a blunt opinion over Claire's own indecision or James's diplomatic one — light, in-character reason for picking him specifically). James asks Azrael/Tristan to help scout a reception venue. Azrael's reluctance shows here — dry, brief, physical more than verbal (flat voice, a look that says he has better things to do) — he finds ordinary errands beneath his function. Lucifer has to talk him into it; some real effort — not banter exactly, but the door on real back-and-forth is technically open post-Calm (style guide: "once Calm unlocks, real back-and-forth between them can start to show up") — keep it restrained, dry, one exchange, not a repartee scene. Something practical works as leverage: Azrael going keeps James/Claire's trust intact, or simply "you're going," settled without much argument once Lucifer makes clear he's not budging.

4. **James's ring problem — plant only, don't resolve.** While this is being arranged, James mentions — to Azrael/Tristan specifically, since it's tied to the venue-scouting favor-trading — that he's short on money for a wedding ring after what the engagement ring cost him, and could use help finding one down the line. Azrael takes note (a beat, nothing verbal needed from him beyond acknowledging it) — Lucifer witnesses this as the POV character; he doesn't offer to solve it here (that's Ch.24). Keep this compact, two or three lines, embedded naturally in the group conversation, not a separate scene.

5. **Split.** Claire + Lucifer head to the dress shop; James + Azrael/Tristan head off toward wherever the venue options are. Brief, clean transition.

6. **Dress shop — precision only.** Claire tries on multiple dresses (imply at least two or three tried, per Scene Completeness — don't skip straight to "the one"). Lucifer gives blunt, short verdicts: "that one" / "not that one," each with one brief, concrete reason (cut, color, how it moves, a seam, proportion — a craft reason, not a compliment). No warmth, no charm, no performance — precision as the whole register. Claire responds to this well — she says as much or shows it (relief at getting an honest answer for once, maybe a dry line about James being useless for this). 3+ speaker rule active once the shop manager is also present and talking — anchor speakers clearly.

7. **The elegant dress — the beauty flicker.** Claire comes out in one particular dress — described through craftsmanship/construction (the actual stitching, the cut, the weight of the fabric, how the light sits on it — object-level, not body-level, per the no-explicit-physical-description rule, which extends to not describing Claire's body in it). Something in Lucifer registers, genuinely, for the first time in the book — not evaluation, not utility, just the dress being beautiful and him perceiving it as such. One quiet beat: a held moment, a single line of noticing, no interior analysis, no naming it as new or strange to him. Immediately move past it into the next practical problem (does it fit right, what does it cost) — he must not become self-aware about the beat. This is the single most carefully-controlled moment in the chapter — do not let it run past one paragraph, and do not let Lucifer's internal narration comment on the fact that something happened.

8. **Price negotiation — the truth-twisting set piece.** Once Claire has settled on (or is leaning toward) the dress, the shop manager names a price that's clearly a stretch for her/James's budget (consistent with James's money troubles just established in beat 4 — nice quiet reinforcement, not spelled out as connected). Lucifer works the manager down using technically-true, misleading-by-omission framing — precise, almost effortless, no lie at any point (per Divine Purity — he cannot lie, only frame). Claire watches this happen half delighted, half alarmed at how easily/casually he does it — a reaction beat from her, not from him. This is the first extended, deliberate scene built around the device (Ch.11's "you didn't specify rules" was one reflexive line; this is a whole scene) — per the unit-plan's own correction, don't call it a literal first on the page. 3-person scene (Claire, Lucifer, manager) — anchor attribution per the 3+ rule.

9. **Walking back.** Claire and Lucifer walk back toward wherever James and Azrael are supposed to meet them. Light, low-key conversation — nothing that escalates the beauty flicker into anything Claire would notice or comment on (she's focused on the dress win, not on him). Maybe a small callback line to the price negotiation, keeping Claire's half-delighted-half-alarmed reaction breathing a bit further.

10. **Reunion and clean ending.** James and Azrael/Tristan are visible ahead — James looks like venue-scouting with Azrael was its own small ordeal (a light, wordless tell only, per POV limits — Lucifer only gets to see, not know, what happened on Azrael's outing). Claire peels off to James — the couple reunites and walks off together, warm, easy, done for the day. Lucifer walks the remaining distance toward Azrael. End the chapter here, on the two of them together, with no acknowledgment of anything else across the street or nearby — per the hard restriction, Elizabeth is never rendered from Lucifer's side in this chapter. A short closing exchange between the brothers (Azrael asking how the shop went, Lucifer giving the same kind of shape-not-substance answer he gave in beat 1, mirroring the chapter's opening) is a clean, natural way to close without needing a new beat.

## Craft constraints checklist going in
- 1,000-word floor — content plan above should clear it easily (opening report, 4-person street scene, dress shop scene with multiple try-ons, price negotiation, walk back, closing exchange).
- No explicit physical description of Claire's body — dress craftsmanship/construction only, plus facial expression/outfit as usual.
- No hedge phrases in narration.
- **No similes in narration at all** (new locked rule) — watch this specifically in the dress-shop and sunset-adjacent-craftsmanship description beats, where the old instinct would reach for a comparison.
- No overwritten posture/expression/action.
- Attribution: action-beat tags preferred; 3+ Speaker Rule enforced in both the street scene (4 people) and the dress shop (3 people once the manager speaks).
- Comma vs. question mark before tags: match clause's own grammatical mood.
- Divine/telepathic speech: italics, no quotes — likely unused this chapter unless a brief telepathic aside between the brothers is useful, but the plan above keeps their communication aloud/real-names since they're alone at the relevant beats; no telepathic exchange is required by the unit plan for this chapter, so default to none unless a natural moment demands it.
- Lucifer is not all-knowing — do not have him diagnose the shop manager's true price flexibility, James's exact financial situation beyond what's said aloud, or anything not directly observed/stated on-page.
- No smile overuse — stay grounded; the beauty flicker is not a smile, it's a perceptual/attentional beat, not a facial one necessarily (a flicker of attention/stillness is safer than reaching for another smile so soon after Ch.11's).
- Azrael/Lucifer dynamic: restrained, dry, mostly one-sided still — the door is technically open for real back-and-forth post-Calm, but don't invent an escalated banter dynamic without basis; one real, brief exchange to get him to agree to venue-scouting is enough.
- Elizabeth: zero appearances, zero mentions, zero glances toward her. Not even an incidental "the coffee shop" reference that could imply her presence.
- Every chapter changes/introduces a character AND advances plot: Beauty/Desire's flicker (character change, quiet) + James's ring-request plant + the dress/venue logistics moving the wedding forward (plot advance) + Claire getting real screen time on her own terms (character texture).
