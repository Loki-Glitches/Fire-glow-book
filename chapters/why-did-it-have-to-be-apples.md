# Why Did It Have to Be Apples — Lucifer

The portal spat him out sideways.

There was no grace to it, no landing he could claim credit for — one second the dark pulled at every part of him at once, and the next he was airborne over green ground with no sense of up until the ground reminded him. He still had the apple. He didn't know why. Somewhere between Hell's throne room and whatever this was, his fist had closed around it and refused to let go, and now it dug into his palm hard enough to hurt as he went down.

Azrael was already there. Standing off to the side, arms loose at his sides, not a hair out of place, watching Lucifer come through the tear in the world without moving to help and without any particular hurry about it at all.

Lucifer had exactly enough time to register the injustice of that before he hit a tree.

The trunk didn't bend. It went — all at once, bark and pale wood flying apart in every direction, the whole thing coming down in pieces around him while a hundred small red shapes rained out of the branches and thudded into the grass. He rolled through the wreckage, momentum still owed, and put a shoulder into a second tree hard enough to strip half its fruit loose before his boots finally caught the ground and held.

He stood there a second, breathing, apples still settling around his feet, thudding soft into the grass one after another.

He looked up. Rows of them. Trees in careful lines as far as the property ran, heavy and green-leafed, every one of them hung with the same fruit he'd just beaten off two of their neighbors.

Something in him went very still, and then very sharp.

He tipped his head back and glared straight up past the leaves, past the sky, at who he knew had arranged for an apple orchard to be sitting directly under the one spot he happened to land in, on the one day in several thousand years he was on Earth hunting the woman most associated with the best apple anyone had ever eaten.

"Really?" he shouted, to the open air, to whatever was or wasn't listening. "Apples? Out of all the places on this entire planet — apples?"

Nothing answered him. Just the orchard, settling back into quiet, the last few apples tricking down through the leaves around him. He stood there a second longer than he needed to and got exactly what he got every other time he tried this: silence, complete and entirely unbothered.

"Apples," he said, to no one now, quieter. "Of course it's apples. Why did it have to be apples."

"You'll want to lower your voice," Azrael said, not moving from where he stood. "You've only been here five minutes."

Lucifer looked down at his own fruit. It was smaller and darker than the ones on the ground, its skin gone slightly translucent. He turned it over once, bit into it instead of answering, and wiped the juice from his palm hard on his thigh.

"You could have caught me," he said.

"You didn't need catching. You need to slow down Lucy" Azrael finally moved, closing the distance, eyes going once over the mess of splintered wood and fallen fruit, flat and patient, already tallying it as bad news before he'd finished counting. "What a mess you've made."

"It's an orchard. Things fall in orchards."

"Trees don't."

"These two did." Lucifer straightened his jacket, found it torn at one shoulder, and left it.

He bit into the fruit again, unhurried, and let Azrael sit with that.

Azrael's jaw worked. "You remember the terms."

"I remember them fine. I argued with every one of them individually, and lost." Lucifer kicked an apple out of his path; it rolled a lazy arc and stopped against a fallen branch. "No divinity revealed to a human eye. Every commandment still holds, on Earth, the same as anywhere else, apparently, because God gave me more rules than you. And then there's you, glued to my side the entire time, so someone is watching to make sure I don't 'make a mess again.'" He said the last part in the exact cadence God had used, which he knew would land.

"I'm aware of the terms too." Azrael said.

"Then you're aware of what happens if someone puts a direct question to me and I can't lie my way clear of it. I don't get to shade it. I don't get to leave a gap and let them fill it in wrong. If it's put to me plainly, I answer plainly, and whatever that costs us, it isn't going to cost me anything I haven't already paid — it's going to cost you the part where you have to stand next to me afterward and explain what I just said." He spread his hands outward. "So don't lecture me about lowering my voice. Worry about what you're going to say when I can't lower it enough."

Something moved behind Azrael's eyes, brief and hard, gone before it could be called an expression. "You say that like I asked for the seat next to you."

"You didn't have to ask. It's the only seat you're ever offered."

"That's not — " Azrael stopped himself, visibly, jaw tight. Started again, quieter. "Six minutes ago I was walking souls out of hospital rooms and battlefields, doing the one thing I've done since I was reassigned because of your mistake. Now, one of our siblings is doing that right now. They are standing at those bedsides because I've been reassigned to stand at yours instead — not to evaluate anything, not this time, just to keep a hand on the back of your neck so you don't do to Earth what you very nearly did in that paradise. I didn't choose that. I was handed it. Same as I was handed the last one, and the one before it, every single time the job changes shape to whatever you need it to be." He exhaled through his nose. "So no. I don't need you to remind me what it costs me to stand next to you. I have a very current knowledge."

For a second neither of them said anything.

"For the record I didn't ask you to come," Lucifer said, quieter than before, which was as close as he was going to get to conceding the point.

"No," Azrael agreed. "You never do."

The sound of voices cut through before either of them could decide whether to let that sit or pick it back up, and a man and a woman came around the tree line at a jog, the woman a step ahead, both of them slowing hard when the orchard came into view.

"Oh my God!" The woman — hair pulled back, dirt still ground into the hem of her sundress from kneeling in the orchard earlier that morning — stopped dead at the edge of the wreckage. "What happened to the trees?"

"My dad's going to lose it," the man beside her said, already moving closer, eyes going from the shattered trunk to Lucifer's torn jacket and back, waiting for someone to connect the two out loud. "Did — did somebody drive through here?"

Lucifer opened his mouth. Beside him, without any visible signal passing between them, Azrael opened his at the exact same moment.

"We don't know," Azrael said, in a flat, final register, and decided not to elaborate.

The man looked between them for a second, clearly unsatisfied, then let it go — decided, visibly, that the stranger in front of him wasn't worth the argument. "Right. Well — are either of you hurt?"

"No," Azrael said, before Lucifer could answer that one on his own terms.

"Well then, I'm James," the man said, sticking a hand out. He was sunburned across the nose, built from actual physical work rather than vanity. "This is Claire. We work the orchard — well, my dad's orchard, but the same difference most days." Claire lifted a hand in a small wave, still eyeing the debris unhappily.

Azrael took the offered hand. "Tristan."

James looked at Lucifer, waiting.

"Call me Adrian," Lucifer said plainly.

"You two aren't from around here," Claire said. Not a question, more an observation offered to fill space while she crouched to pick up one of the fallen apples, turning it in her hand, checking it for bruising out of what looked like pure habit.

"No," Azrael said again, easy, unbothered, the answer clearly settled on well before the question came. "Passing through. We are currently between places."

Lucifer glanced sideways at him. Between places? Not a lie, technically — certainly vague enough to survive scrutiny, and yet specific enough to pass for an answer.

James was still frowning at the trees. "You said you don't know what happened, but you were standing right here."

"We have, but we haven't been here for very long," Lucifer said, taking another bite of his apple.

A beat. Then James, apparently deciding that was as much as he was going to get and finding he didn't much care, let it go. His problems tended to run small and solvable, and he assumed everyone else's did too, so he moved past this one just as fast. "Well — you look like you've had quite a morning. We were just about to have lunch, actually, up past the rows there, if you want to sit a minute before you go… wherever you're going."

"That's kind," Azrael said, "but we've got somewhere to be."

"You've got nowhere to be," Lucifer said. "We don't even know where we are yet."

Azrael's look could have stripped paint. Lucifer ignored him.

"Come on," James said, already turning, clearly used to being the one who insisted and clearly used to winning. "It isn't a bother. Besides, Claire packed enough for six. I'll feel bad eating it in front of you."

Azrael's exhale was audible "Fine," he said. "Briefly."

---

The blanket was spread in the shade past the last row of standing trees, a basket beside it with sandwiches wrapped in wax paper and a jug of something pale yellow sweating in the heat. Lucifer sat because sitting was easier than arguing about it, and because some low, curious part of him wanted to watch how this would go. He didn't touch the sandwiches. He had his own fruit for that, and took another bite of it now, unhurried, while everyone else ate.

"So," Claire said, handing out the sandwiches, entirely at ease with two strangers on a blanket at noon, "where are you two from, if not around here?"

"East, originally," Azrael said. "Both of us. Family's spread out now — we've been moving around more than staying anywhere, the last while." He took a bite and let that stand as the whole answer.

"What do you do?"

"This and that. Whatever needs doing where we land." Azrael smiled, small and practiced. Lucifer watched him do it and thought, not for the first time, that his brother had gotten better at lying than he ever let on.

"And you two are — brothers? You've got the same look about you," James said, glancing between them.

"Yes," Azrael confirmed.

"So what brings you all the way out here, then?" Claire again, easier with strangers than James, or maybe just more curious. "You said you don't know where you are. That's a strange way to travel."

"We're looking for someone," Azrael said, smooth, already angling the sentence toward something vague and forgettable.

"Oh yeah? Who?"

Lucifer felt the question land on him specifically, though no one had asked him specifically, and felt the old familiar tightening that meant the truth was already climbing up his throat whether he wanted it there or not.

"Eve," he said, before Azrael could get another sanded-down non-answer in ahead of him.

Azrael's sandwich stopped halfway to his mouth.

"Eve," Claire repeated, turning the name over once. "Ex? Sister? Runaway kid?"

"Something like that," Azrael said quickly, reclaiming the thread. "It's a long story. Family thing."

Claire made a small, sympathetic sound and let it drop, apparently satisfied that "family thing" covered whatever awkwardness was sitting under the name. James nodded along, incurious — nothing in his life so far had given him a reason to suspect a stranger of being anything other than exactly what he said he was.

Lucifer said nothing else. He'd told the truth. He hadn't been made to explain it. He counted that as a win and reached for some lemonade.

They'd gotten maybe ten more minutes into it — Claire asking whether they'd eaten today, James describing, at length, the correct way to tell a good apple from a bruised one by weight alone, Azrael nodding along with an attentiveness that anyone who didn't know him would have read as genuine interest — when James set his sandwich down, he wiped his hand on his jeans, and stood up.

"Claire," he said.

Something in his voice changed shape. Claire looked up at him, sandwich halfway to her mouth, and went very still.

James got down on one knee in the grass, in front of everyone, in the middle of an orchard and produced a ring from his jeans pocket — small, thin-banded, the stone in it catching the sun's light thin and flat, obviously glass cut to mimic something costlier.

"I've been carrying this around for two weeks trying to find the right moment," he said, "and I don't think there's going to be a right one, so —"

"That's not real gold," Lucifer said.

The silence that followed had a texture to it.

"Luc… Adrian," Azrael corrected, very quietly, and then his fist connected with Lucifer's jaw hard enough to snap his head sideways.

It didn't hurt exactly — nothing on this side of the portal had found a way to really hurt him yet — but it was enough to make his point, which Lucifer understood was the actual purpose of it. He worked his jaw once, testing it, and found himself, infuriatingly, already opening his mouth again.

"I'm not wrong, though," he said, rubbing his jaw with two fingers, undeterred, apparently unable to help himself even with James still frozen on one knee and Claire staring somewhere between horrified and furious. "Look at it. That's glass set in tin, dressed up to catch light from an angle. You can tell from here." He tilted his head, studying the ring with the same flat, critical eye he'd have given a lazily assembled report. "It's an insult, honestly, to propose to someone with a lie on your hand."

"Adrian," Azrael said, and it came out as a warning more than a name.

Lucifer's eyes cut to him, flat and pointed, and held there just long enough to land.

"I'm not finished." He got to his feet, and something about the way he did it — unhurried, almost theatrical — pulled every eye on the blanket toward him whether they wanted it there or not. He lifted the fruit to his mouth and bit down hard, anchoring it between his teeth, both hands free now. He held up his left hand.

"Adrian—"

He closed two fingers of his right hand around the base of his middle finger and drew them slowly down and off it, the unmistakable motion of a man sliding a ring free, milking the pause for every second it was worth — pinched an object in his fingers.

A ring. Gold, not tin. A stone set deep enough not to snag, cut with more facets than the eye wanted to count at once, throwing light back in a dozen directions even in the shade of the trees.

He took the fruit from his mouth with his free hand and held the ring out to James with the other.

"That one isn't a fake," he said.

Nobody moved for a second. Then James, still on one knee, looked at the ring in Lucifer's hand and then at his own, the cheap one still pinched between his fingers, forgotten.

"How did you — " Claire started, and didn't finish, because there wasn't an ending to that sentence that made any sense out here in the grass.

"It's nothing for you two to worry about," Lucifer said, and smiled, and meant it more than he'd meant to. There was something genuinely satisfying in the sleight of it, in watching three faces try and fail, one after another, to work out how he'd done it. "Your first one was fine, for what it was. This one is just better"

James took it. He didn't argue, didn't ask again where it had come from, just turned back to Claire with something on his face that had nothing left in it interrogating, and held it out to her instead of the one he'd started with.

"Yes!" Claire exclaimed before he'd finished getting the words out a second time.

James's amazement didn't last long — he decided not to look too hard at a good thing, and let it go. Claire ran in the same direction, laughing, turning the ring in the light to watch it throw color across the blanket. Azrael's face wore something else entirely — flat, careful, the particular stillness Lucifer recognized from every other time his brother had a great deal to say and had decided not to say any of it at all.

"Where," Claire said, still turning the ring, "did that come from, though. Really."

"A very reasonable question," Lucifer said. "I collect things. Old habit."

That was enough for both of them. They'd just gotten something good out of a stranger, and neither wanted to look too closely and risk finding a reason it shouldn't be theirs.

Azrael waited until James and Claire had drifted a few steps off, arms around each other, examining the ring in better light, before he leaned in close enough that his voice wouldn't carry.

"You did it," he said. Low, tight. "In front of them? You know exactly what the terms said."

"The terms said no revealing my divinity where a human could witness it," Lucifer stated. "They watched a magic trick. A very good one. Ask them what they think happened and they'll tell you a man with a ring up his sleeve, not an angel pulling gold out of nowhere. I kept the letter of it."

"That's not the same as keeping the spirit of it, and you know it."

"I do know it," Lucifer agreed, unbothered. "I also know we need them."

Azrael's eyes narrowed. "No."

"They know this town. Every face in it, probably, given how fast that one" — a nod toward James — "was ready to propose in front of two strangers he met twenty minutes ago. People like that talk. People like that notice things. We've been here less than an hour and I don't have the first idea what year it is on this side of the tear, let alone where to start looking."

"We don't need to involve humans."

"We're sitting on their blanket eating their bread. We're already involved with them." Lucifer's voice dropped, harder. "I am not spending the next however-long stumbling around a town I've never seen, guessing, while she gets further ahead of us. We use what's in front of us."

Azrael held his gaze a long moment. Whatever calculation ran behind his eyes, it didn't show — but eventually his jaw unclenched half a degree, which was as close to surrender as his brother ever gave.

"Fine," Azrael said. "Carefully."

They came back to where James and Claire stood, still bright with the last few minutes, and Lucifer let Azrael take the lead on it, because his brother's version of careful was worth something even when it grated.

"Can I ask you both something," Azrael said. "That woman we mentioned. Eve. We need to find her, and we don't know how long she's been here — could be days, could be longer. We won't know until we see her. But she'll look… Out of place. Dressed wrong, talk wrong, like she walked out of a different time entirely and hasn't caught up."

Lucifer nudged him for his exactness.

James and Claire traded a look.

"That's oddly specific," Claire said.

"It's a family thing," Azrael said again, but this time it did the less work it had the first time.

"We haven't seen anyone like that," James said, "but — you know who would? Elizabeth. Or Liz. She's lived here longer than pretty much anybody her age, knows everyone, notices everything — if someone strange came through town, she'd have clocked it before anyone else did."

"Where can we find her," Lucifer said, too fast, and felt Azrael glance at him.

"She's around town most days," Claire said. "You'll know her if you see her — she's hard to miss, honestly. Ask anyone."

They talked a little longer after that — Claire wanting to know if the ring needed sizing, James already planning who to tell first, both of them clearly still riding the high of the last twenty minutes far enough that the wrecked trees at the tree line had stopped registering as a problem at all. Lucifer sat through most of it without much to add, turning the name over once, quietly, where no one could hear him do it.

Elizabeth.

Eventually the light started shifting toward afternoon, and Azrael stood, and Lucifer stood with him, and they thanked the couple for the food, flat and correct.

"Good luck finding her," James called after them. "Eve, I mean."

"Thank you," Lucifer said, as close to kind as he had any intention of being to this town on his first day in it.

They walked back through the row of trees, apples still scattered thick across the grass. Lucifer stopped at the shattered trunk long enough to set it right and checked for prying eyes — the splintered wood drawing back together, the trunk standing whole again in the time it took to register— and kept walking without checking whether anyone behind them had noticed. Azrael noticed. He didn't say anything about it, which was its own kind of answer.

Lucifer took another bite of the fruit still in his hand, smaller now than it had been that morning in the throne room, and said nothing about that either. Neither of them said anything else until the orchard was well behind them and the shape of a town had started to come up over the next rise, all rooftops and one thin church spire and, somewhere in it, a girl neither of them had met yet.

"Elizabeth," Azrael said finally, careful with it, testing the shape of the name before he committed to it.

"Elizabeth," Lucifer agreed, and didn't look back at the orchard again.
