# Hell

The iron doors at the far end of the throne room are forty feet tall and have no handles, because no one who isn't Lucifer has ever been allowed to open them himself. He's been on the throne long enough today that sitting up for visitors stopped being worth the effort around the fourth sector report, and he doesn't bother standing up for this one either. A flick of two fingers, barely more than a twitch, and they swing inward on silence, admitting Sebastian, who has been his sector administrator for longer than most of Sebastian's own memory stretches back and who still, every single time, pauses half a step over the threshold like the room might change its mind about him.

"My lord." Sebastian's bow is precise. His voice is not.

Lucifer doesn't answer right away. He's eating.

The tree at the center of the room has no business existing at the scale it exists at — it goes up past where the ceiling should be, if the room had a ceiling that meant anything, and its branches hang heavy with fruit the color of something that has no good word in any human language, dark red going toward black at the edges. He doesn't reach for the one he wants. He never has to. It drops when he looks at it, and bends off its fall in the last foot of air, curving neatly into his palm.

He bites. Juice runs over his thumb. He doesn't wipe it.

"Report," he says, around the mouthful.

Sebastian's clipboard — he still insists on a clipboard, after six thousand years of better options, and Lucifer has never had the energy to find that endearing — rattles very slightly in his hands. "Sectors two through nine, stable. No incidents. Sector six had a — a minor collapse in the eastern gallery, nothing structural, already — already repaired."

"You said that fine."

"Yes, my lord."

"It's the next one you're going to have trouble with."

Sebastian's throat moves. "Sector — the, ah, the f-first—"

"There it is."

"The first sector remains—" Sebastian stops. Starts again. "Remains at standard containment. No change in—"

"You stammered on *first*." Lucifer sets the apple core down on the arm of the throne, where it rots instantly to nothing, flakes of ash drifting off it like the room is bored of pretending the fruit was ever really there. The fire in the wall seams, which had been banked low and lazy while he ate, comes up a shade. Not his doing, not exactly. It just knows. "You've given me nine sectors in a row without a hitch and the only one you can't say clean is the one with her in it."

"It's a hard word, my lord, I only—"

"Don't." He's on his feet. He hadn't decided to be, and that's the part that should have warned Sebastian more than it did. "Don't tell me it's the word. You've said *sector* nine times in the last two minutes without your voice doing anything. You say *first* and it falls apart. You want to try that sentence again?"

"My lord, I didn't mean—"

"I didn't ask what you meant." Lucifer crosses the floor without seeming to hurry and is somehow across it anyway, close enough that Sebastian has to tip his head back to keep eye contact, close enough that Sebastian's clipboard has gone still because his hands have stopped being able to do anything at all. The fire in the walls isn't a lazy orange anymore. It's climbed to something nearer white, throwing Lucifer's shadow huge and wrong-edged across the stone behind Sebastian, and for one full second there's nothing warm left in the room, nothing bored, nothing that looks like a man who was eating fruit off a tree a minute ago. "You stammer on her name because some part of you still thinks she's going to get out, and you think that, because you're not watching that wall closely enough to know better. Am I right."

"No, my lord — I mean, yes, I watch it, I watch it every—"

"Then why," Lucifer says, very quietly, which is worse than the shouting would have been, "does your voice do that."

Sebastian doesn't answer. He can't. His mouth is open and nothing is in it.

Lucifer holds the look a moment longer than he needs to, long enough that the fire starts creeping back down the walls on its own. Then he turns away, already bored of his own temper, and the doors behind Sebastian — the same doors, forty feet of unmarked iron — groan open another half-inch, unasked.

That's when the second set opens on their own, hard, and the runner comes through at a dead sprint.

"My lord — the wall, first sector, the containment's been failing, it's been failing an hour, nobody caught it until the stone started—"

"An hour."

"The wall's coming apart from the inside, my lord, it's nearly—"

Lucifer doesn't let him finish the sentence. He also doesn't walk anywhere.

Every flame in every wall seam in the throne room freezes mid-flicker, a held photograph of fire, and Sebastian's half-raised hand stops being half-raised, suspended, a fly in glass. Somewhere past these walls, in sectors Lucifer isn't looking at, the same thing is happening to every clock, every drip of water, every damned soul mid-scream. The room doesn't get quiet. It gets stopped.

He's the only thing still moving.

The floor opens under him without him touching it, a straight clean split in the stone, fast as a drawn blade, and he goes down into it rather than falling, dropping through sector after sector, down past the stable ones and the quiet ones, until sector one's ceiling is above him and closing and he's already running before his feet have properly agreed to be on the ground.

The cell is at the end of a corridor he could walk blind, has walked blind, more times than he's bothered to count. He doesn't need to count the steps this time either, because he can see the wall from fifty feet out — the wrong kind of light coming through it, a seam of white-gold that isn't his fire and isn't anyone's, cut into the black stone like a wound that opened the wrong way.

Eve is most of the way through it.

He sees her hand first, through the opening, fingers spread against nothing. Then her shoulder, turned sideways to fit, her whole body angled into the gap like she's being swallowed by it headfirst. And then, impossibly, she turns her head back toward the corridor. Toward him.

She reaches.

Not away. Not bracing against the wall to push herself the rest of the way through — she turns and her arm comes back out of the light, fingers open, reaching down the corridor straight at him. Whether she wants pulled back or wants him pulled in after her, he can't tell. Not from twenty feet. Not with that light behind her washing out everything in her face that would have told him.

He doesn't have time to decide which. He throws himself the last twenty feet, hand out, and the portal closes on the empty place where hers was a half-second before his isn't there yet, and the wall he hits instead is just wall, old and black and immovable, and the sound his body makes against it is the sound of stone cracking, not giving — cratering around the shape of him in a shallow bowl that will be there for the next thousand years.

He doesn't get up right away.

When he does, he goes to the opening — closed now, sealed, nothing but a hairline seam where it had been — and runs his hand along the stone around it, and understands.

It isn't new damage. The edges are soft and rounded — nothing about a fresh break looks like this — the black gone pale and chalky in a shape that could only be made one way: fingers, or claws, it hardly matters which, working at the same 12 inches of wall for a millenia. Not an hour. Years. The stone had been worn down to the color of itself before it ever let anything through — the containment hadn't failed tonight. It had been failing since long before anyone was watching for it, and tonight was just the night it finished.

"My lord."

Sebastian and Lilith are both already there when he turns — Sebastian breathless, having run the whole way down through the ravine Lucifer tore and hasn't bothered sealing yet; Lilith not breathless at all, arms folded, taking in the crater in the wall and the state of him with the same flat, unhurried attention she gives everything.

"She's out," he says. "Or she's gone somewhere. Either way she's not in this cell and I don't currently care which."

"Out where," Lilith says.

"I'm going to find out." He looks at Sebastian. "Every cell in this sector gets reinforced. Not inspected — reinforced. I want new containment on every wall by the next watch, and I want it done by someone other than whoever's been checking this one, because whoever's been checking this one is either blind or lying, and I don't have time to work out which tonight."

"Yes, my lord."

He looks at Lilith. "You're running Hell while I'm gone."

If that surprises her, nothing in her face bothers to show it. "For how long."

"However long it takes."

"And if something needs you specifically."

"Then it waits." He's already walking back toward the ravine, toward the long climb up through nine sectors of stopped time. "Everything in this place has waited longer than that for less."

He climbs. Behind him, the walls of the ravine ease shut as he goes, floor by floor, sealing from the bottom up, until by the time he steps back out into the throne room there's no sign the floor was ever anything but floor.

Time starts again around him with no fanfare — fire resuming its flicker, Sebastian's clipboard rattling faintly, somewhere far off the first scream of the evening picking back up exactly where it left off.

He picks another fruit off the tree without reaching for it and eats standing, looking up at nothing, because there isn't a ceiling worth the name and there hasn't been a sky in this place in six thousand years.

"I need to go up there," he says. "After her."

No voice answers out of the air, not exactly, and no light changes, not exactly, but the warmth that comes into the room has nothing to do with the fire in the walls, and it has always meant the same thing.

*You may go.*

"Good." He's already turning the fruit over, already thinking about which sector gets his attention first on the way out. "I'll need—"

*You'll have nothing.*

He stops.

*No power. Not a flicker of it. Your body up there will be exactly what theirs are — it will bruise the way theirs bruise, break the way theirs break, and heal on their schedule, not yours. For as long as you're on that ground, you're not what you are down here. You're just a man.*

"That's absurd." He hears his own voice climb and doesn't bother reeling it back in; there's no one here he's trying to look composed for. "I'm hunting someone that's been clawing through my walls for *forever* without me noticing, and You want to send me after it with nothing. No fire. No speed. Not even the small things — not even this." He gestures at the fruit in his hand, at the doors, at all of it.

*Yes.*

"Why."

*You know why you don't need to ask that.*

"I don't. Enlighten me."

Nothing enlightens him. That's the part he's never been able to argue past, in six thousand years of trying — not a wall, not a raised voice, just a silence that isn't cold and isn't going anywhere, patient enough that shouting into it starts to feel pointless. He opens his mouth to try a different angle and finds he already knows how this ends, same as every time before. He's not going to win this by arguing longer. He's never once won this by arguing longer.

The portal opens on its own, off to his left, without him reaching for it — a seam of ordinary daylight cut into the black stone, warm and plain and nothing like the wound Eve tore in her own wall an hour ago.

*Better get going, Luc.*

He's still holding the fruit. He's still, in fact, chewing it, mouth half full, which feels like an undignified way to be dragged off the throne of Hell to go play human for however long this takes — but the ground is already tilting under him, pulling him sideways and up, and he only gets the one look back: the tree, impossibly tall, fruit catching what light there is like a spill of dropped coins across the dark.

Then the portal closes behind him, and it doesn't make any sound at all.
