# Chapter 2 — Lucifer

The cycle's figures were the same as the last cycle's figures.

He read them anyway. The ledger lay open across the arm of the throne, its pages the gray of ash pressed flat. Column one: souls received. Column two: souls in active treatment. Column three: souls released.

Column three was empty as usual. It had been always been empty.

"Fourteen thousand and some in this cycle," Sebastian offered from the foot of the dais. He had his own tally folded in one hand and did not consult it. "Down slightly. The patrol thinks it's a plague season ending somewhere. Corridors nine through forty are at capacity. Forty-one is close."

Fewer deaths. Same corridors, still full.

"Then open forty-two."

"Lillith's already reinforcing it." Sebastian shifted his weight. He had been a patroller for eleven centuries and he still stood at attention. "The demons in twenty-two have been pushing harder. Full reliving, four rotations instead of three. They wanted it noted."

Four passes through the same guilt instead of three. More of a cure that had never once worked.

"Note it."

"It's noted." A pause. "It isn't working, though. Twenty-two, I mean. Four rotations, same as three. Same as two."

"Their diligence is not in question."

"No. It isn't." Sebastian folded the tally into a smaller square and put it away.

The throne room was cold. It was always cold. At the center of it stood the Tree of Knowledge, gold to the root, its branches holding the ruby weight of fruit that had not been touched in an age, and Lucifer sat with his back to it. Beyond the doors, past the fortress and down the flank of the mountain, Hell went on in every direction — dark, close, endless, and lit only where the cell doors leaked. Suffering was the realm's sole illumination. It had been designed that way. It had been designed by him.

He closed the ledger.

"Anything else."

"One thing. Probably nothing." Sebastian tipped his head toward the doors. "Dust in the low corridor. Brimstone, powdered fine, about a handspan deep across ten yards. Patrol swept it last cycle and it was already back before this one closed. They've flagged it twice."

"Which corridor."

"The one under the mountain. The old ones."

---

The spiral staircase ran down through the mountain's body for a long way. Lucifer took it at an ordinary pace. There was no urgency attached to dust.

The cavern at the base was older than most of the realm, and the corridor that opened off it was the oldest thing in Hell that was not the Tree. The cells here held the first arrivals — the ones who had come down when the ground was still settling, whose weight had brought them to the floor of the world before anyone else had thought to die. Their doors glowed low and steady. Patient light.

The dust began forty feet in. It was not spread. It was fanned, thrown outward from a single point. He followed the fan to its origin, and the ordinary pace stopped being ordinary.

She was still there when he rounded the last turn. On her knees in the wreck of the doorway, one hand still flat against the door she had already finished with. Centuries had done what centuries do. She looked up, and something in her face opened before it had any right to.

"Lucifer?"

He crossed the distance in two strides. He was still one stride short when the corridor went empty again — the same force that had freed her taking her a second time, up and gone, faster than he could close the space between them. The dust resettled where she had been kneeling.

The cell door had not been opened. It had been ended. The frame was intact; the seam where the frame met the brimstone was intact; the lock, such as a lock meant anything here, was intact and still engaged. The door itself had come out of its housing in a single piece and lay eight feet down the corridor with its inner face toward the ceiling. That face was scored. Not scratched — scored, in layers, thousands of passes over the same four inches of the same lower corner, until the metal had gone thin as a leaf there and the whole plate had finally let go at once.

Lucifer crouched and set two fingers against the thinned place. It was smooth. It had the polish of long work.

He stood in the doorway of the cell itself. Six feet by eight, unlit now that the door was gone. It still held the shape of her in it. On the back wall, cut into the brimstone at the height of a seated person, were marks. Rows of them. Not a count of days — a count of something else, arranged in a pattern he did not care to decode.

He did not need the register to know the name. He checked it anyway, two cells down, where the patrol kept it. Cell one. First corridor. Occupant: Eve.

He closed it.

He would have to tell God about this. That was the whole of what the moment cost him — not her name, not the centuries attached to it, only the shape of that conversation and how badly it would go.

Through the bond, once, flat: *First corridor. Now.*

She was there before the thought had finished settling.

Lillith crouched at the door where it lay and set two fingers against the same worn corner.

"Not a break." She turned her hand over and looked at nothing in it. "A wearing. She did it with her hands. There is blood in the corner and it has been there for centuries."

"Continuous?"

"Continuous. Every moment I was not in the room with her." Something moved at the edge of her mouth and did not become anything. "She had a great deal of time and only the one idea in it."

"Confirm the direction."

"Up. The door agrees with what you saw — it left through force, not release, and force only had the one direction open to it. Nothing else." She stood. "This has not happened before. I have a wearing pattern and old blood. I do not have a destination beyond that, and I will not invent one for you."

"No one asked you to." He looked once more at the scored plate lying in the corridor and then stopped looking at it. "Reinforce the neighboring cells to the eighth corridor. Every door in this section comes off its housing and gets inspected on the inner face. If any of them are thin, replace them. If you find another with polish on it, you contact me before you touch it."

"Understood."

"Have the patrol log the section on every round instead of once a cycle. Sebastian will want to argue about staffing. Let him argue and then set it on every round."

"Understood."

He was already climbing.

---

Permission was not a thing one applied for in writing. There was no office and no queue. He went up through the fortress to the highest room, where the ceiling opened onto nothing at all, and he asked.

*A containment failure in the first corridor. The occupant escaped through force. I saw it happen. I am requesting leave of the realm to pursue it on Earth.*

The answer did not arrive as sound. It never had. It settled into the room with the weight of something already decided, waiting only to be asked for.

*Granted.*

Lucifer inclined his head and turned to go.

*Two conditions.*

He stopped.

*Azrael will meet you there.*

He considered that. Not for long, and not with any feeling attached to it, but it was a departure from procedure and departures from procedure were recorded. Retrieval was a Hell function. Hell functions did not require oversight. He filed it under *new* and did not pursue it.

"Very well," he said aloud, since aloud cost nothing. "And the second?"

*You will not go down there as yourselves.* A pause that was not hesitation. *You are Adrian. He is Tristan. Those are the names you answer to, both of you, in every room that has a human in it. No exceptions, no explanations, no clever framing of the truth. They are not to know what is standing next to them.*

"Adrian."

*Adrian.*

"I suppose it is no worse than the others."

There was no reply to that, which was itself a kind of reply.

---

He found Lillith in the ninth corridor with her hand flat against a door frame, feeding brimstone back into a hairline seam.

"I am leaving the realm. You have it until I return."

She took that without pause. "Terms."

"Full regency inside the usual limits. Reinforce, command, maintain — nothing beyond. No possession. If a soul goes wrong in a way that cannot be held any other way, you may take one, and you will tell me the moment you do."

"And if I cannot reach you?"

"You will always be able to reach me. That is not a condition, it is a fact. Use it when something is genuinely wrong and not before." He watched the seam close under her palm. "No leave for anyone. Not the demons, not the patrol, not you. Every hand on the floor for as long as I am gone. If a demon complains about rotations, they get another rotation."

"They will complain."

"Then Hell will be well staffed." He turned. "The first corridor is your priority. If it happens twice it is not an anomaly, it is a design flaw, and I would rather hear that from you than discover it."

"You will hear it from me."

She had already gone back to the seam before he was out of the corridor. He had never once had to tell her a thing twice, and it had never once occurred to him to say so.

---

The throne room was empty when he came up. It would have surprised him to find it otherwise. Azrael did not come down here — could not, though nothing in Hell's architecture forbade it. Whatever kept him tethered to his own work kept him tethered to Earth entirely — the dying did not pause for anyone's containment breach, and there was no version of the job that let him leave them mid-thought to stand in a room with a tree in it. Lucifer knew this about him the way he knew most things about Azrael — through the bond, passively, without needing to ask.

He crossed to the dais anyway and took the ash-gray ledger from the arm of the throne, setting it squarely in the centre of the seat where Sebastian would find it. The band on his left hand caught what little light the room had and gave none of it back.

Through the bond, faint and already irritated: coordinates, a location, and underneath both of them the particular flavor of resentment Azrael did not bother to disguise when he thought no one was reading it closely. *Do not make this interesting,* it said, without words, same as always.

He had no intention of making it interesting.

The doors of the fortress stood open on the mountain and the dark beneath it, fourteen thousand new cells that cycle, none released, the powdered brimstone still lying several cycles deep in the lowest corridor where nobody had yet swept it a third time.

Lucifer did not look back at any of it. He went up, alone, toward wherever the coordinates put him — and toward whatever was already waiting for him there.
