# Chapter 2 — Lucifer

The cycle's figures were the same as the last cycle's figures.

He read them anyway. The ledger lay open across the arm of the throne, its pages the gray of ash pressed flat. Column one: souls received. Column two: souls in active treatment. Column three: souls released.

Column three was empty as usual. It had always been empty.

"Fourteen thousand and some in this cycle," Sebastian offered from the foot of the dais. He had his own tally folded in one hand and did not consult it. "Down slightly. The patrol thinks it's a plague season ending somewhere. Corridors nine through forty are at capacity. Forty-one is close."

Fewer deaths. Same corridors, still full.

"Then open forty-two."

"Lillith's already reinforcing it." Sebastian shifted his weight. He had been a patroller for eleven centuries and he still stood at attention. "The demons in twenty-two have been pushing harder. Full reliving, four rotations instead of three. They wanted it noted."

Four passes through the same guilt instead of three. More of a cure that had never once worked.

"Note it."

"It's noted." A pause. "It isn't working, though. Twenty-two, I mean. Four rotations, same as three. Same as two."

"Their diligence is not in question."

"No. It isn't." Sebastian folded the tally into a smaller square and put it away.

The throne room was cold. It was always cold. At the center of it stood the Tree of Knowledge, gold to the root, its branches holding the ruby weight of fruit that had not been touched in an age, and Lucifer sat with his back to it. Beyond the doors, past the fortress and down the flank of the mountain, Hell went on in every direction — dark, close, endless, and lit only where the cell doors leaked. Suffering was the realm's sole illumination. It had been designed that way. It had been designed by him.

He closed the ledger.

"Anything else."

"One thing. Probably nothing." Sebastian tipped his head toward the doors. "Dust in the low corridor. Brimstone, powdered fine, about a handspan deep across ten yards. Patrol swept it last cycle and it was already back before this one closed. They've flagged it twice."

"Which corridor."

"The one under the mountain. The old ones."

---

He did not take the stairs.

He already knew it would be her. Not a guess — a certainty, cold and immediate, arriving before the thought that produced it. He drove his fist into the floor of the throne room and the mountain split beneath him, a clean vertical wound through a thousand feet of rock, and he dropped through it into the dark.

He hit the floor of the first corridor already moving. The cells here were the oldest in the realm, their doors glowing low and steady — patient light, undisturbed by what was about to happen forty feet away.

The door was turning to ash as he reached it. Not breaking. Not wearing through. Simply ceasing to be door, sifting downward in a gray fall the color of everything else down here — containment finally admitting it had never been built to hold this long.

Behind where the door had been, the air itself had split open. A seam of nothing, edges the color of a wound, wide enough to take a person. Eve was already inside it, weightless, one hand out toward him and she smiled slightly.

"Lucifer?" she asked before she was pulled in

He crossed the corridor before he had decided to.

His fingers closed on air. The seam shut the width of a held breath from his hand, and she went with it, and the corridor was ash and silence and nothing else.

"Lillith!"

She was there before he had lowered his hand.

"Full regency until I return. Reinforce, command, maintain — nothing beyond. No possession, unless a soul goes wrong in a way nothing else will hold, and you tell me the instant it happens. No leave for anyone — not the demons, not the patrol, not you. Every hand stays on the floor." He was already turning away from what was left of the door. "Reinforce the section. Every door in it, whether it looks sound or not. Don't touch that one. Don't analyze it. There is nothing left in it I need."

"Understood."

"You can always reach me. Use it if something is actually wrong."

He was climbing before she answered — not the stairs, never the stairs, but straight back up through the wound he had torn on the way down, the mountain sealing itself behind him a hand's width at a time.

---

He reached the throne room already asking.

*Send me to Earth. Now. I know where it went.*

The answer took its time arriving, which was its own kind of answer.

*Azrael already has your instructions.*

His jaw tightened at that — not at her, not at any of it, only at the particular tax of having to work through Azrael, who resented him on principle and had never once pretended otherwise. "I don't need an intermediary," he said aloud, since aloud cost nothing. "I need to go."

*You are not sending yourself anywhere. That has never been the arrangement, and today will not be the day it starts.* A pause that held something close to patience. *You are Adrian on Earth. He is Tristan. No exceptions, no explanations, no clever framing of the truth. They are not to know what is standing next to them.*

"Fine."

There was no reply to that. The floor opened instead — a seam the same wounded color as the one Eve had gone through, already wide enough to take him — and it took him before he had finished bracing for it.

The throne room closed over the space where he had been standing, and Hell went back to being quiet.
