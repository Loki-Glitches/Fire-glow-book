# Introductions Are In Order — Lucifer

Lucifer bit down on the last of the fruit and chewed through the sourness that had set into it over the past week, then flung the core into a hedge outside a shop with a chalkboard sign he hadn't bothered reading. It bounced once off the brick and settled into the mulch, unremarkable. Something in his chest settled with it, though he wasn't in the mood to examine what.

"That's the last of it." Azrael kept his eyes on the hedge a second longer than the moment called for. "Feel lighter?"

"I feel like I've spent three days carrying fruit through a town that doesn't deserve it." Lucifer shoved his hands into his pockets and kept walking. "No. We're not doing the fruit bit again."

"I wasn't going to start the fruit bit again."

"You were building up to it. I can hear it."

Azrael let that go, which was worse than arguing it. They walked another block without talking — nothing companionable in the silence, just two people with nowhere better to stand. The town went on being itself around them: a hardware store, a bank with a flag out front, a woman walking a dog that barked at both of them for no reason either of them could locate.

"We need a faster way to find her." Lucifer kicked a loose stone off the curb. "This is going to take a decade at the rate we're going."

"You have a decade. God didn't put a clock on it that I heard."

"I don't have a decade. I have patience for about a week and I've already spent most of it."

Azrael glanced sideways at him. "Which is exactly why I'm going to say this now, before you decide the fastest way to find one woman in a town of a few thousand people is a parlor trick that turns into something a human can't explain away in the morning."

"You're bringing up the ring."

"I'm bringing up the ring."

"The ring worked."

"The ring worked because James wanted it to work. You got lucky." Azrael's voice didn't rise, but it flattened out, the register he used when he meant something all the way through. "You do that again and it isn't going to read as sleight of hand. It's going to read as exactly what it is, and then I'm the one explaining to God why half a town saw something they shouldn't have."

Lucifer opened his mouth to argue on principle, found he didn't actually disagree, and closed it again. "Fine. We do it the boring way. Ask around, describe her, wait for someone to say the right thing back to us."

"That's not boring. That's just the way people actually do things."

"That's what makes it boring."

They reached the stretch of sidewalk in front of the coffee house a minute later, Lucifer still turning over what other someone might mean, when the door swung open hard and a woman came through it backward, laughing at something behind her, and walked directly into Azrael's chest.

The coffee went first — up, out, down the front of his shirt in one motion — and then the woman herself rebounded off him and nearly went down with it. Azrael caught her arm before she could.

"I'm so sorry." He held her steady until she found her feet, already reaching for the napkins that had come out of the same collision. "That's entirely on me, I wasn't watching where I was standing."

"No, that's — I walked backward into a public sidewalk, that's a me problem." She looked down at the puddle spreading near both their shoes and laughed again, short. "I basically finished it anyway. You want a picture of my aim, though? Because that was impressive."

"Let me get you another one." Azrael was already patting at his own soaked shirt with a handful of napkins, mostly for something to do with his hands. "It's the least I can do."

"Honestly, don't worry about it." She crouched to pick up the empty cup and its lid, tossed both into a nearby bin. "I was going to be sad about it for maybe ten more seconds and then I was going to forget it happened."

Lucifer stood a step back through all of this, watching his brother apologize with a thoroughness that made the whole thing take twice as long as it needed to. The woman was younger than Azrael, dark hair pulled back, in clothes that hadn't been chosen with a spilled drink in mind. She kept glancing down at the mess on the concrete, her face reddening more with each glance.

The door opened again, more carefully this time, and a second woman came out — blonde, a drink of her own still in hand, eyes going straight to the spill.

"Soph. What happened."

"I walked into a wall." The first woman gestured vaguely at Azrael. "The wall apologized. It's fine, nobody's hurt, I'm just going to need a new caffeine source before I can be a person again."

The blonde one crouched without being asked and started gathering the last of the napkins, glancing up at Azrael and then, briefly, at Lucifer, who hadn't said anything yet and didn't plan to unless someone made him.

Azrael looked between the two of them. "I really can replace it. It's not a hardship."

"It's fine." Sophie straightened, wiping her hand on her jeans. "Genuinely. I've had worse days than losing half an iced coffee I'd already decided I was over."

There was a pause where nobody quite knew what came next, the kind of pause that either ends in everyone walking off in separate directions or doesn't. A pair of benches sat facing each other a few feet down the sidewalk, empty, and it was Azrael who nodded toward them.

"For what it's worth — since I ruined your morning — I'm Tristan." He held a hand toward Sophie first, since she was the one he'd hit.

"Sophie." She shook it, easy about it now that the coffee was someone else's problem to feel bad about.

"Adrian." Lucifer said it flatly, hands still in his pockets, and didn't offer a hand to go with it.

The blonde one looked between the three of them and then settled her eyes on Lucifer last, taking a beat longer with him than she had with the other two.

"Elizabeth."

Azrael went very slightly still beside him — not a flinch, nothing a human would have caught, the reaction he kept for moments that mattered. Lucifer understood why half a second later: this was the name James and Claire had sent them after. He didn't turn his head to check whether his brother had noticed too. He already knew he had.

Neither of them said anything about it.

"You're the ones from the orchard," Sophie said, dropping onto one of the benches, already done thinking about the collision. "James texted me. Two trees down and a magic trick that got Claire a ring."

"That's a generous description of the magic trick," Azrael said, sitting across from her.

"He also said —" Sophie glanced at Elizabeth "— that they told you to look for Liz. Which is extremely funny now, considering."

Elizabeth sat on the same bench as Sophie, leaving Lucifer standing until it became more awkward not to sit than to sit, and he lowered himself down beside his brother.

"I do know James and Claire," Elizabeth said, looking at Azrael rather than Lucifer, which suited him fine. "What is it you actually need help with?"

"We're looking for a woman." Azrael kept his hands loose at his sides. "Her name's Eve. She'd stand out — dresses wrong for the time, talks like she's out of it too. Old-fashioned, but not in a costume way. In a way that doesn't track with anything current."

Elizabeth held up a finger. "How long has she been missing?"

"A while." Azrael spread his hands slightly. "We don't have an exact window."

She held up a second finger. "Do you have a photo?"

"No."

"Police involved?"

"No." Azrael's answer came smooth and immediate, closing off the next question before it could form. Elizabeth let it sit for a second, nodded, and moved on without pressing further.

Sophie leaned back on the bench. "That's the strangest missing-person description I've ever heard, and also the strangest coincidence, because I've lived here my whole life and I get told to help exactly one time and it's twenty minutes before a stranger knocks my coffee out of my hand on the street where I was already headed."

Elizabeth checked her watch. "Small town. Everything loops back eventually." She glanced at Sophie, then back to Azrael and Lucifer. "We're walking back to the car. You're welcome to come with us if it helps — I can think while we walk as well as I can sitting here."

Lucifer would have preferred sitting there. Sitting there meant the conversation had an edge to it, a place it could end. Walking meant it kept going until someone's car appeared.

They stood. Sophie fell into step beside Lucifer before he'd decided who he was walking next to, which meant the arrangement had been made for him — Elizabeth ahead with Azrael, asking him something about how long they'd been in town, and Sophie beside Lucifer with her hands in the pockets of her jacket and an expression that told him she considered this an opportunity.

Sophie angled slightly toward him. "So what do you do. For work, I mean, when you're not chasing missing women around apple orchards."

Lucifer let the question sit a beat before answering, turning it over for the version of the truth that gave up the least. "Whatever's needed of me."

"That's not an answer."

"It's the honest one."

"Fine. Where are you from, then."

Another pause, longer this time. "Not from around here. Not for a very long time."

Sophie squinted at him, reassessing. "Are you like this with everyone, or is it just me?"

"I don't know you well enough to answer that."

"You don't know anyone well enough to answer anything, from what I can tell." She said it without heat, more curious than annoyed, and pressed on before he could decide whether to be irritated by it. "Family? Siblings, obviously, since Tristan's right there. Parents?"

"My father and I don't speak much these days."

"That's sad."

"It's accurate." He reached into his jacket pocket and came out with an apple he didn't remember picking up that morning, though he supposed he must have, somewhere between the orchard and here. He bit into it hard enough that it covered the silence he wanted instead of another question.

Sophie watched him eat it for a second, apparently deciding whether that counted as an answer too, then let the parents question go the way Elizabeth had let the police question go — not dropped, just set down for later.

Sophie shrugged, no heat in it. "You're hard to talk to."

"I've been told."

"By who?"

"Everyone." He ate the apple down to nothing worth eating and had nowhere to put what was left, so he crushed it in his fist instead, pulp and seed dropping between his fingers onto the sidewalk. Sophie glanced at his hand, said nothing, and kept walking.

They reached a corner with a crosswalk and a delivery truck idling half over the line, and in the shuffle of everyone stepping around it, the pairs came apart and reformed without anyone announcing it. Elizabeth ended up beside Lucifer. Sophie drifted ahead to walk with Azrael, already asking him something Lucifer didn't catch.

Elizabeth didn't say anything for the first several strides. Lucifer didn't fill the gap either, and the silence sat there, unresolved, until she spoke.

"You were outside Higgins' this morning."

He looked at her properly for the first time since the benches. "I was."

"You and Tristan. Standing by the lot, looking like you'd rather be anywhere else." Her voice sharpened on the last few words. "I saw you through the checkout window."

"That would be us."

"Small town," she said again, flatter this time, watching him for a reaction. He didn't give her one.

"You keep saying that like it explains something."

"It explains why I clocked two strangers twice before lunch. That's not normal for me." She glanced at him sideways. "You're not very forthcoming, you know that? Sophie's been at it for ten minutes and I don't think she's gotten one real answer out of you."

"She's gotten several real answers."

"She's gotten several answers that were technically true and told her nothing. That's not the same thing." Elizabeth said it plainly, no needling in it, and it bothered him more than a jab would have. "It's a little exhausting to watch, honestly."

"You're welcome to stop watching."

"I would, except you're the one my friends are trying to help, so I'd like to know who I'm dealing with." She kept her eyes forward. "How do you know Eve?"

Lucifer felt the same refusal rise in him that always rose when a question got this close, the one built into him before he'd had a say in the matter, and he redirected the way he and Azrael had already worked out how to redirect. "That's really Tristan's story to tell."

"Convenient."

"It's accurate."

"Everything's accurate with you and nothing's an answer. That's a skill, I'll give you that." Her voice made clear it wasn't a compliment.

Ahead of them, Azrael picked the question up without being asked twice, pitching his voice to reach both conversations at once. "We go back a long way. All three of us — Eve included. It's complicated in a way that doesn't fit into a walk to a parking lot."

"Everyone's story is complicated when they don't want to tell it," Elizabeth said, loud enough for him to hear, and Lucifer caught the corner of his brother's mouth twitch — agreement Azrael would never have said out loud.

They reached the lot a few minutes later, a sedan parked crooked across two spaces near the back. Sophie dug her keys out of her jacket and stopped with her hand on the door.

"We should get numbers. In case Eve turns up, or in case you two need help and we're not just standing around outside coffee shops when it happens." She looked between all three of them, waiting for someone to produce a phone. Nobody did. "Great. We're professionals."

"Next time," Elizabeth said, already pulling the passenger door open. She didn't look back at Lucifer before she got in, and he noted it without saying anything.

Sophie waved once, more at Azrael than at either of them, and climbed in behind the wheel. The car pulled out of the lot and turned onto the main road, and then it was just the two of them standing on the asphalt in the heat.

"Well," Azrael said, watching the car go. "That went better than the orchard."

Lucifer didn't answer.

"Nobody got punched. Nothing got repaired that shouldn't have needed repairing. And we have a name for the one person in this town who might actually be useful to us." Azrael glanced over. "You're quiet."

"I'm thinking."

"About Eve."

"About a lot of things." Lucifer put his hands back in his pockets and started walking. Neither of them said Elizabeth's name again, but she was the only lead they had, and both of them knew it the rest of the way down the block.
