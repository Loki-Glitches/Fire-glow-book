# Introductions Are In Order — Lucifer

The fruit from the Tree of Knowledge was down to its core by the time they reached the middle of Main Street, and Lucifer was already regretting it. Not the eating — the running out. He turned the core over once in his palm, found nothing left worth the trouble, and pitched it into a trash can bolted to a lamppost without breaking stride.

"That's the last of it," Tristan said. Not a question.

"Astute."

"You've been carrying that thing since the orchard."

"I liked it."

"You liked it enough to stop eating apples for three days and just chew on that one instead. I noticed."

"You notice a great many things you don't need to."

Tristan let it go. He'd been doing that more lately with Lucifer's barbs — noting them, not answering them. They walked on. The town was ordinary around them on a warm afternoon: a bell over a shop door, a dog barking twice and giving up, someone's radio playing faintly from an open car window. Lucifer hated how easy it was to walk through all of it unnoticed. Centuries of being feared by name, and now he couldn't get so much as a stare crossing a street.

"So," Tristan said. "Ideas."

"I have several."

"None of which involve you doing anything you did at that orchard."

Lucifer glanced sideways at him. "You brought a coin trick up three times yesterday. I'm aware."

"I'll bring it up a fourth time if I have to. You pulled a ring off your own finger in front of two humans and turned it into something it wasn't. It read as a magic trick because you got lucky, not because you were careful."

"It read as a magic trick because I am extremely good at what I do."

"That's not the reassurance you think it is."

"I wasn't trying to reassure you. I was trying to end the conversation." Lucifer exhaled through his nose, something that in a better mood might have been a laugh. "Fine. No tricks. No rings. I'll find her the boring way, since apparently that's the only way that doesn't get you clutching your chest like an old woman."

"The boring way being."

"Asking people. Like you did with the orchard couple. It worked, didn't it? We have a name."

"A name and a description of someone who dresses like she time-traveled out of the wrong century, yes. Very promising lead."

"It's a small town. Someone will have seen her."

"Someone we haven't met yet," Tristan said, "who apparently knows everyone in it. We're looking for a woman named Elizabeth."

"We keep saying that like it's going to summon her."

It did not summon her. What it summoned, half a block later, outside a coffee house with a chalkboard sign propped against its window advertising something involving lavender, was considerably less dignified.

The door swung outward fast, and a young woman came through it walking backward, laughing at something over her shoulder, an iced coffee in one hand and a phone in the other — and Tristan, who had been in the middle of a sentence about door-to-door canvassing, had no warning at all before she collided with him square in the chest.

The cup left her hand entirely. It hit the pavement and burst, ice and pale brown liquid skidding out across the sidewalk.

"Oh — god, I'm so sorry —"

"No, that's — that was entirely me," Tristan said, already crouching, already reaching for the cup's wrecked lid, still trying to save something out of it. "I wasn't watching where I was going."

"You weren't the one who walked out backward." She was laughing again, but it had gone sheepish, one hand pressed to her sternum where she'd hit him. "I do this. I do this constantly, ask anyone who's ever stood near a doorway with me."

Lucifer watched his brother apologize with a thoroughness that bordered on theatrical — offering a napkin from his own pocket, of all things, that he could not possibly have needed a moment ago — and found it, against his will, a little funny. It was very Tristan: methodical, correct, clean, no wasted motion.

"Let me get you another one," Tristan said. "It's the least I can do."

"Honestly, don't worry about it, I was basically finished anyway. It was mostly ice at that point." She waved it off, crouching to help gather the worst of the spill with the flat of her hand, wincing at the sticky mess of it. "This one's on me. I wasn't looking."

The door opened again behind her, more carefully this time, and a second young woman came out — quicker step, scanning the scene in about a second and a half before her face settled into something between concern and amusement.

"Soph, what happened —"

"I destroyed a man," Sophie said, straightening up. "Just walked right into him. Didn't even see him."

"I promise the damage was mutual," Tristan said.

"It really wasn't, you apologized like four times already."

The second woman crouched to help too, and it was in the middle of that — four people now half-kneeling around a puddle of iced coffee on a public sidewalk, trying to sort out whose fault a small accident had been — that Lucifer got his first real look at her. Nothing about it announced itself. She had a bump healing over one eyebrow, dark hair pulled back loosely, sleeves pushed up, and she was already reaching for the empty cup to toss it before anyone asked her to. Ordinary. He noticed her only in passing — the kind of notice anyone competent in a crisis earns — and then stopped, because there was a spill to deal with and his brother was still apologizing.

There was a pair of benches a few feet off, angled to face each other under the shade of the coffee house's awning, and it was Sophie who suggested moving to them once the worst of the mess was swept into a napkin and binned. "Come on, sit, you don't have to stand there being sorry at me all afternoon."

They sat. Tristan and Lucifer on one bench, Sophie and the other woman on the other, close enough that knees nearly touched across the gap.

"I'm Sophie, by the way," Sophie said, "since we've apparently skipped that part."

"Tristan."

"Adrian," Lucifer said.

There was a small pause — the kind that meant nothing to the two women, who were still faintly out of breath from the small disaster of five minutes ago, but that Lucifer felt land somewhere in his chest anyway when the second woman said it easily, without a flicker of hesitation:

"Elizabeth."

Lucifer looked at Tristan. Tristan looked at Lucifer. Neither of them said anything, but something passed between the two glances that had nothing to do with spilled coffee.

"Elizabeth," Tristan repeated, carefully even. "That's — actually, that's a fortunate coincidence. We were told to look for you."

Her eyebrows went up. "That's not usually how people open a conversation with me."

"A couple named James and Claire," Lucifer said. "You know them?"

"Of course I know James and Claire." She glanced at Sophie, who was already grinning, clearly several steps ahead of the conversation. "What did I do?"

"Nothing," Tristan said. "There was an — incident, out at the orchard. We were there, we got to talking with them, and they mentioned you'd be worth finding if we needed help with something."

"Help with what?"

Lucifer let Tristan take the lead on this — the arrangement they'd settled on weeks and one long argument ago. "We're looking for someone. A woman. Her name is Eve."

"That's it? Just Eve?"

"That's the name we have. She'd stand out — dresses and talks like she's out of place, out of time almost. Older than the fashion around here would explain."

Elizabeth considered that with real attention, the kind that made Lucifer think, unhelpfully, that James and Claire hadn't oversold her. "I haven't seen anyone like that. I'd remember."

"That's the general reaction," Tristan said.

"Okay, wait." Sophie held up a hand, looking between the four of them, working something out in her head. "So you two are looking for someone, you asked James and Claire, they sent you here, and on your way here you literally ran into us. That is — that's actually kind of a funny coincidence."

"It's a small town," Elizabeth said. "Coincidences run smaller here too."

"We were headed back to the car anyway," Sophie said, standing, brushing off the back of her shorts. "Walk with us? You can keep asking Liz your twenty questions and I promise she actually is the person to ask."

Lucifer had not expected that, and it must have shown, because Tristan was already on his feet before he had fully decided to be.

"We'd appreciate that," Tristan said.

They fell into a loose line down the sidewalk, and within the first block the pairing had sorted itself without anyone announcing it — Elizabeth drifting up beside Tristan, already asking her first question before they'd gone twenty feet, and Sophie falling in beside Lucifer with the unhurried air of someone who'd already decided she was going to get more than one-word answers out of him.

"So how long has she been missing?" Elizabeth was saying, a few steps ahead. "Like — days? Weeks?"

"Longer," Tristan said. "It's hard to put an exact number on it."

"Do you have a photo? Anything I could actually match against a face?"

"No photo, unfortunately."

"That seems like it'd make this a lot harder."

"It does," Tristan agreed, with the particular smoothness Lucifer had come to recognize as his brother operating at full, practiced fluency. "We're working from description and instinct more than anything else at this point."

"Have you gone to the police?"

"It's a family matter. We're handling it ourselves."

"Family," Elizabeth repeated, taking note of it. "Is she your sister? Cousin?"

"It's complicated," Tristan said, which was, strictly, true, and which Lucifer noted his brother delivered without so much as a hitch.

Beside him, Sophie watched the exchange with open interest, then turned her attention fully onto Lucifer.

"Okay, your turn. What do you do? Like, for work, normally — not the missing-person thing."

Lucifer paused. It wasn't a long pause. It only felt long from the inside, the half-second where he sorted through every true thing he could say and discarded the ones that would require explaining further true things after it.

"Whatever's needed of me," he said.

Sophie blinked. "That's not an answer."

"It's the honest one."

"Okay, mystery man. Where are you from, then?"

Another pause, shorter this time, but there. "Not from around here. Not for a very long time."

"That's also not really an answer."

"It's true, though."

She laughed, not unkindly, more like she'd found something she intended to keep poking at. "Fine. Where'd you grow up?"

He looked ahead, at the back of Tristan's head, at the shopfronts sliding by. "Somewhere far from here."

"Wow. Okay. Very specific." She bumped his shoulder lightly with hers, teasing rather than annoyed. "Do you have siblings? Besides Tristan, I mean."

"Just him."

"See, that one you answered fast."

"That one's simple."

"Are your parents around? Do they live near here, or —"

He felt the question land somewhere it had no business landing, a sudden ache of something old and complicated, and took a beat before answering that one too, weighing each word carefully before he let it out. "My father and I don't speak much these days."

Something in his voice must have shifted, because Sophie, to her credit, didn't push it further. "Sorry. Didn't mean to hit a nerve."

"You didn't." He wasn't sure that was entirely true, but it was close enough to stand.

"You're a hard guy to get information out of, you know that?"

"I've been told."

"By who?"

Another pause. "Him, mostly." He nodded ahead at Tristan's back, and Sophie laughed properly at that, loud enough that Elizabeth glanced back over her shoulder to see what she'd missed.

"What's funny?"

"Nothing, your new friend's just extremely bad at talking about himself."

"Oh, is he," Elizabeth said, amused, and for a moment Lucifer felt genuinely, uselessly exposed — seen more plainly than he wanted to be, with nothing ready to cover it.

They crossed at the corner by the bank, and somewhere in the shuffle of stepping off and on the curb the pairs reshuffled themselves too — Sophie sliding up to fall in step with Tristan, already asking him something about how exactly you go about looking for a person with no photo and no address, and Elizabeth dropping back to walk beside Lucifer, hands tucked into the pockets of her shorts.

"So," she said. "Adrian."

"Elizabeth."

"You're not from around here, Sophie said."

"Sophie says a lot of things."

"She's not wrong very often." A beat. "You don't talk much, do you."

"I talk plenty. I'm selective about the audience."

That got a small laugh out of her, easy, unbothered, and he found he liked having produced it more than he was prepared to examine. They walked a few paces without either of them saying anything, and it was, strangely, not an uncomfortable silence — the kind of quiet Lucifer had almost forgotten existed, the kind that didn't demand to be filled.

"Wait," Elizabeth said suddenly, slowing half a step, looking at him properly for the first time since they'd swapped. "Were you two the guys outside Higgins'? This morning?"

"Higgins'?"

"The grocery store. Two guys standing by the parking lot, one of them looked like he wanted to set the whole street on fire." She said it lightly, no accusation in it at all, just recognition clicking into place. "That was you, wasn't it."

Lucifer considered denying it and found he couldn't — not because of the constraint, though that was part of it, but because there was something almost funny about being caught looking irritated in public by a stranger who'd apparently clocked it from across a parking lot.

"That would have been me," he admitted.

"I knew it. I told Sophie there were two guys out there and she didn't even look up." Elizabeth shook her head, smiling to herself. "Small world."

"Small town."

"Same thing, around here." They walked another block, past a hardware store and a bakery with its lights already off for the afternoon, before she circled back to something that had clearly been sitting in the back of her mind, waiting for an opening. "Can I ask you something?"

"You've been asking me things for the last ten minutes."

"Fair. Different question, though." She glanced at him sideways. "How do you two know Eve? You said family, but Tristan said it like he didn't want to actually say it."

Lucifer felt the shape of every true answer available to him and discarded each one before it reached his mouth — she is the reason I fell, she is the reason I am on this earth at all, she is older than this town and this country and the ground it's built on — and did the only thing that had ever reliably worked in situations like this.

"That's really Tristan's story to tell better than I do." He raised his voice slightly, pitched toward his brother's back. "Tristan. Elizabeth wants to know how we know her."

Tristan turned his head without breaking stride, unbothered, already fluent again, reaching for something smooth and technically true. "Long story. I'll tell you the short version — we go back a long way, all three of us, and it's not the kind of long way that fits into a walk to a parking lot."

"That's a very practiced non-answer," Elizabeth said.

"I've had practice," Tristan agreed.

They reached the lot a minute later, Sophie's car sitting alone near the far end under a tree that had dropped a small drift of leaves across its windshield already, too early for the season, and Lucifer watched Elizabeth brush a few off the glass without seeming to notice she'd done it.

"This is us," Sophie said, digging keys out of her bag. "If you see her — Eve, I mean — you'll let us know? Or, I guess, we'll let you know, since we don't actually have your numbers, which now that I say it out loud is a weird oversight."

"We're around," Lucifer said. "This town's small enough. We'll cross paths."

"We seem to be good at that," Elizabeth said, and there was nothing loaded in it, just an observation, but it sat with him longer than it should have.

Sophie climbed in first. Elizabeth paused at the passenger door a moment longer, looking back at the two of them standing on the curb.

"Good luck finding her," she said. "I hope it's easier than it sounds."

"So do I," Lucifer said, and meant it more than he expected to.

The car pulled out of the lot, turned at the corner, and was gone. Tristan and Lucifer stood there a moment longer, the parking lot quiet except for a bird calling somewhere in the tree overhead.

"Well," Tristan said eventually. "That went better than the orchard."

"No trees died."

"No trees died," Tristan agreed. "Small victories." He glanced sideways, something assessing in it. "You alright?"

"Fine."

"You went quiet toward the end there."

"I'm always quiet. You've apparently only just noticed." Lucifer turned and started walking, hands in his pockets, and after a beat, Tristan fell in beside him, and neither of them said Elizabeth's name again, though it sat between them anyway the whole rest of the walk back.
