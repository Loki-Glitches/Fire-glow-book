# Errands — Lucifer

"I think you broke my back."

Lucifer said it to the back of Azrael's head, since that was the view he had, a few steps behind on the hill road with one hand pressed flat against his own spine. Every third or fourth step pulled a fresh complaint out of the muscle there, low and to the left, right where the column had caught him.

"You'll live." Azrael didn't slow down or turn around.

"That's not the same as being fine. I mean come on, I didn't do anything."

"You've been worse."

It was true, which was the irritating part. He straightened anyway, rolled his shoulder, and let his hand drop before they came level with the first houses at the bottom of the hill, though the limp in his left leg didn't go with it — a short hitch in his stride every few steps, there and gone. The road flattened out, turned to pavement, and the town opened up ahead of them — a gas station, a hardware store with its awning rolled halfway down against the sun, a line of parked cars outside something that smelled like bread.

"Where are we going?" Azrael asked.

"The bank."

"Why?"

"Because the house costs a quarter of a million dollars, and walking in with that much cash in a bag gets someone's attention. A number that size needs to come from somewhere a person can point to." Lucifer pressed his hand back against his spine as the ground dipped under a pothole and jolted up through his hip. "I'd rather not spend the next month answering questions about where I keep my money."

"You've been spending cash out of your pocket all day."

"A thousand dollars doesn't make anyone curious. A house does."

Azrael didn't argue the point, which from him counted as agreement.

They walked for a while, past the hardware store and a shuttered movie theater, before Azrael stopped walking altogether. Lucifer went two more steps before he noticed and turned around, already annoyed about it.

"What?" Lucifer demanded.

Azrael was looking at a storefront across the street — a glass front crowded with cardboard cutouts of phones, a sign over the door with a cartoon signal bar on it. "If you are going to insist that we need help from any humans" — he tipped his head toward the town at large — "they need a way to reach us."

"We don't need them. God has probably been pulling strings since we arrived."

"You are insane if you think that he is doing everything for us while we are her Lucifer."

Lucifer looked at the shop, then back at his brother, then let out a long breath through his nose. "Fine." He crossed the street first, which was as close as he came to admitting Azrael was right.

A bell over the door announced them as usual. The inside was small and too brightly lit, shelves of boxed phones and cases running down both walls, a counter at the back with a young man standing behind it who straightened up off his stool the second the bell rang.

"Afternoon. Can I help you find something?"

"Just a phone," Lucifer said, "Obviously."

"You've come to the right place." The clerk's name tag read KEVIN. "Are you looking to add a line, switch carriers, or something like that?"

"New. Just for him though" Lucifer nodded at Azrael, who had already drifted off toward a wall of display models and was turning one over in his hands.

"No problem, I can set that up right now. I'll just need a photo ID to get the account started — driver's license, passport, anything with a photo's fine."

"We don't have one."

Kevin blinked. "Either of you?"

"Neither."

"I mean — I hear you, but it's not really something I can get around. Carrier policy, it's federal actually, for activating a new line. I can't open an account without ID, I'd lose my job."

"You won't lose your job." Lucifer set both hands flat on the counter, unhurried. "You'll open the account. I'll make it worth not asking twice."

"Sir, I really can't—"

"You can. You're choosing not to, which is different." He said it mildly, stating it rather than arguing it. "I understand the position you think you're in. I'm telling you there's a better one available."

Kevin glanced past him, toward the back of the store, clearly hoping a manager would appear and take the decision out of his hands. None did. "Look, even if I wanted to — the system flags it. There's a field it won't let me skip."

"Then skip it afterward. Fill in whatever the box needs filled and correct it later, or don't correct it at all. I don't care how you solve it. I care that it's solved." Lucifer reached into his jacket and came out with a fold of hundreds, thick enough that Kevin's eyes went to it and stayed there despite himself. "Five thousand dollars. For ten minutes of paperwork that you will forge anyway within the week."

"I could lose my license for this kind of thing. Not just the job."

"You could. Or you could take five thousand dollars home tonight, tell no one, and go on with your week exactly as planned, minus whatever it was you were worried about affording before I walked in." Lucifer wasn't guessing at anything specific — he didn't need to. Every human he'd ever dealt with wanted the same handful of things, and money solved more of them than people liked to admit. "This isn't a complicated decision. It only feels like one because you haven't made it yet. Besides isn't customer service one of your fundamental roles in this place?"

Kevin looked at the money, then at the door, then at the register, clearly stalling. "If anyone ever asks—"

"No one will ask. And if someone does, you tell them exactly what happened. You made a choice. So did I. That's the whole of it." Lucifer set the fold of bills on the counter between them and didn't push it any further than that. "Take it or don't. I'm not going to stand here persuading you all afternoon."

It took him a little longer than that anyway — another round of Kevin turning the bills over and checking each one for a watermark, another round of him glancing at the ceiling where a small black camera dome sat recording the whole thing to no one who'd ever watch it, another visible argument he was having with himself that had nothing to do with Lucifer at all. But he picked the money up.

"Give me a second." He pulled a form up on the screen in front of him and began typing in the blanks that didn't ask for a name attached to a face. "Who's it going under?"

"Adrian Black."

Behind them, Azrael had found the display model he wanted and was pressing at its blank screen with one finger, trying to make it do something. He didn't look up. As far as he was concerned the conversation at the counter was a minor formality, already resolved the moment money came out of Lucifer's jacket, and he'd stopped paying close attention to it somewhere around that point.

Kevin counted the money out and slid the boxed phone across the counter along with a slip of paper for Lucifer to sign — not a name this time, just an acknowledgment that he understood the terms of service, which Lucifer did not read. He signed it anyway. Twelve hundred dollars changed hands for the phone itself, counted out with the same lack of concern as before; Kevin rang it up without comment, already visibly working on not thinking about any of this again.

"You're all set. It should already be activated, you should get a signal the second you step outside."

"Good." Lucifer picked up the box before Azrael could reach for it, and turned to face him. "Don't you dare make me regret this later," he warned him. And handed it over

Kevin called after them, a little too brightly. "Enjoy the phone!"

Neither of them answered.

Outside, the sun had moved enough to put the far side of the street in shade, and Lucifer angled them toward it without discussing it. His back pulled again somewhere around the shoulder blade, and he pressed his palm to it for a few steps before letting his arm swing free.

Azrael had his box open before they'd gone half a block, phone out, turning it over, pressing the side of it, the top of it, the bottom of it, in roughly that order and with roughly equal lack of success.

"It's not doing anything."

"You have to turn it on."

"I've pressed every part of it."

Lucifer held out his hand without breaking stride. Azrael put the phone in it, which was itself a kind of concession. Lucifer held down the correct side for the correct length of time, waited for the screen to light, swiped it open with his thumb, and in a handful of motions had it sitting on a blank list.

"There. Contacts." He held the screen up so Azrael could see it, then handed it back.

Azrael stared at the screen, genuinely baffled. "How did you do that."

"Honestly Azrael, I don't like phones." Lucifer was already looking down the street, toward the bank's sign two blocks on. "That doesn't mean I don't know how to use them."

He didn't explain further, and Azrael didn't ask him to. It wasn't a mystery worth chasing — six thousand years running a domain built entirely on what people wanted and what they'd give up to get it had taught him plenty about the things humans built to want more of it faster. A phone was not a complicated machine.

As they approached Lucifer noticed the bank had a name painted in gold across its front glass — First Link Banking — and a set of doors that opened onto cool air, carpet, and velvet ropes. A woman at the second window looked up as they approached, her name plate read DENISE HARLOW, and she gave a quick, practiced smile.

"Good afternoon. What can I help you with today?"

"I'd like to open an account."

"Of course. Checking, savings, or both?"

"Checking."

"I'll just need some identification to get started, and then we can—"

"I don't have any." He said it flat, already reaching for his jacket, already prepared to go through the whole performance again.

Denise, though, only tapped at her keyboard. "That's all right, we can usually work around that if you've got an account history with us already, or — what name would it be under?"

"Adrian Black I guess." Lucifer looked surprised at the work-around.

She typed it, and her smile dropped away into real concentration. "Huh. That's — there's already an account under that name."

"There shouldn't be." He said it before he'd thought about whether it was true, and then understood, in the space of a breath, that it very much could be.

"It's right here. Opened about a month ago, it's fully set up. Social, address on file, everything. There's just nothing in it." She turned the screen a few degrees, more out of habit than because he could read it from where he stood. "Did you maybe already open this and forget? It happens more than you'd think."

"I didn't open it."

Beside him, Azrael was still fiddling with the phone. He didn't say anything. He just giggled to himself knowingly.

"Gabriel," Lucifer whispered forcefully, low enough that it didn't carry past his own jaw, and with real feeling behind it.

Denise didn't catch it. "Sorry, what was that?"

"Nothing to concern you. It's fine. Use the account that's already there please."

"Sure thing. I'll just need you to sign for access, since it's showing no prior card issued." She slid a form and a pen across the counter. He signed Adrian Black under the line of text he didn't read again.

"I'd also like to deposit funds, and have it linked so my brother can draw on it from his phone." He held his hand out toward Azrael without looking at him. "Give me the phone."

Azrael kept it in his own hand a beat longer than necessary. "I'm holding it."

"Give it to me," His voice stern.

Azrael handed it over, visibly deciding a fight wasn't worth having in front of a stranger. Lucifer set it on the counter, and under Denise's direction — her walking him through a screen at a time, more helpful now that the ID question had resolved itself — linked it to the new account, set a card to it, and confirmed a number.

"And for the deposit itself," Denise said, "are we talking a transfer from another institution, or—"

"A loan. Against collateral." He reached into his jacket a second time, and came out with something small and heavy wrapped in a handkerchief, which he unwrapped and set on the counter between them.

The stone caught the overhead light and reflected it in a way nothing on Earth had ever quite managed, because no cut diamond anywhere had come from where this one had. Uncut, the size of a large knuckle, dense with a color that shifted depending on which way Denise tipped her head to look at it.

She picked it up carefully, turned it, and set it back down with visible unease. "This is — I'm going to need to have this looked at properly, but just visually, this is worth considerably more than the house you're probably trying to buy."

"Don't worry about it I just need a quarter million which should be well below the actual worth."

"I can get you that provisional amount against it today and have the rest released once it's appraised, if that works."

"Don't bother with the rest."

It took a while — a manager was fetched, examined the stone with the same careful disbelief, made a phone call to someone who clearly had more authority than either of them, came back with a number that satisfied everyone in the conversation except possibly Azrael, who stood through all of it with his arms crossed and his face doing nothing at all. By the time they were done, the account that hadn't existed an hour ago and had existed for who knew how long before that held more than enough to buy a burned-out house on a hill, and a notification had landed on the screen of a phone Azrael still didn't entirely trust.

"We're all set," Denise said, sliding a receipt across. "Is there anything else?"

"No. Thank you." He took the handkerchief back and put it in the pocket.

Outside, the sun hit them again, and his back pulled hard enough on the first step down that he had to stop and press his hand flat against it before he could keep walking.

"Oh, Gabriel," Azrael sighed, once they were a few doors down and clear of the glass.

"Don't."

"What?."

"You know what."

Azrael didn't deny that either, and didn't say anything else about it, which was its own kind of answer.

Emma Brock's office. As they entered, she looked up from her desk before the door opened halfway and was on her feet before it had finished swinging shut.

"Adrian. Tristan. I wasn't expecting you back so soon — did you want to talk through the inspection, or—"

"I'd like to pay for the house."

"Oh?" She blinked, recovered, and reached for a folder already sitting at the corner of her desk — she'd clearly had it ready, in case this came today rather than next week. "I have the paperwork ready, if you're sure. The full amount, or were you thinking financing—"

"Full amount."

He held his hand out for the phone again. Azrael passed it over without being asked this time, which saved them both the argument. Emma turned a small card reader around to face him, and he tapped the phone against it — a short beep, a pause, and a green light.

"That's — that's it, that's gone through." Emma checked her own screen a second time before she said anything further. "All right. Well. Congratulations, I do hope to see it again sometime."

She pulled a single folded document out of the folder and held it out across the desk — the deed, her signature already on it from earlier in the day, a blank line waiting for him.

He signed with the same name he used all day. Then she folded it back along its original creases and handed it to him.

"I know it needs work," she said, handing over the deed. "Granted, a lot of work. But it's a good place."

"I know." He took it, folded it once more, and tucked it into the inside pocket of his jacket, against his chest.

Azrael was already at the door, phone in hand, turning it over again, still visibly unconvinced by it. Lucifer pressed his hand briefly to his back, straightened, and followed him out into the street.
