# Ambush — Elizabeth

Elizabeth had the whole plan laid out before they'd even reached the gate: walk the square, look for anything or anyone out of place, and if they spotted Adrian or Tristan, she'd be the one doing the talking. Sophie had agreed to all of it in the car. She was agreeing to a lot less of it now.

"There," Sophie said, not even trying to be subtle about pointing.

Across the plaza, past the dry fountain and the ring of benches around it, two figures were crossing the storefront path — one in dark sleeves despite the heat, arms crossed, plainly resenting the whole outing, the other beside him, talking. Elizabeth didn't need a second look. She'd have known that posture anywhere by now, the particular way Adrian managed to look bored and irritated at the exact same time even from this distance.

"Finally," Elizabeth said, and started forward.

Sophie caught her arm. "Where are they going?"

Elizabeth watched instead of answering. The brothers crossed at the south walk, cut along the row of storefronts, and stopped in front of a glass door with gold lettering across it. BROCK REALTY. Elizabeth recognized the sign if not the woman behind it — everyone in town eventually ended up in that office, buying or selling or just asking questions about the old Tilden place that never sold. Tristan held the door. Adrian went in first.

"Okay," Sophie said. "So we wait."

"We wait," Elizabeth agreed, through her teeth, and didn't move from the spot.

It was, she would think later, the longest kind of patience she'd ever practiced in her life — standing at the edge of the plaza in the full sun, watching two shapes move around behind a tinted window, saying nothing to a woman who was supposed to be finding a missing, scared person named Eve. She didn't go up to the glass. She didn't want to give either of them the satisfaction of being caught checking, and some small, stubborn part of her wanted to see how long whatever they were doing in there would actually take. Sophie found a low wall to lean against and didn't ask Elizabeth to sit, which Elizabeth appreciated, because she wouldn't have been able to.

It took a while. Long enough that Elizabeth ran through five different opening lines and discarded all of them except one.

"I am going to kill them," Elizabeth decided, already moving.

"Liz—"

"I mean it, Soph, I am done being polite about this—" She was three steps gone before Sophie's hand closed around her wrist and hauled her back hard enough that she nearly lost her footing.

"Calm down."

"I am calm." She wasn't. "I have been calm for an entire stretch of this summer, I have been so calm, and I would like to not be calm for about thirty seconds, is that allowed?"

"Not right now, it's not." Sophie didn't let go. "You go over there like that, they're going to shut down and you're not going to get anything out of them, especially not Adrian."

"I don't care what I get out of them, I want to ask what the hell they think they're—"

"Liz," Sophie warned.

"No, listen—" Elizabeth yanked her arm free, but she didn't move yet. "They are supposedly looking for a missing woman. A scared, missing woman, who's been gone for who knows how long. And instead of looking for her, they're in a real estate office. House shopping. While we're standing out here trying to figure out how to even contact them again."

Sophie's mouth opened, something reasonable and leveling already forming — and then closed again.

"Yeah," Elizabeth said. "That's the part that's actually making me insane. Not that they're weird. Not that Adrian's a nightmare to talk to or not talk to for that matter. That they had the nerve to go pick out a house while Eve's out there somewhere."

"...Okay," Sophie said. "That's fair."

"Thank you."

"I still think you should let me do the talking Lizzy."

But Elizabeth was already walking, fast, across the crosswalk, timing it so badly against the ring road's stop sign that a car had to brake for her. She didn't apologize to the car. She reached the sidewalk in front of Brock Realty right as Tristan was pulling the door shut behind him.

"You have got to be kidding me," Elizabeth said, loud enough that both of them turned. "A house? You're buying a house?"

Tristan blinked, which was the most surprised she'd ever seen him look. Adrian didn't blink at all — he just went very still. "No, we already bought it," Adrian said matter of factly.

"Are you seriously making jokes right now!" Elizabeth shouted, bringing her hands to the sides of her head.

Sophie arrived a beat behind her, shaking her head slowly, and reached out and took a fistful of Elizabeth's hair at the back of her neck — not hard, just enough to tug.

"Ow — Sophie—"

"Give them a second." Sophie's grip on her hair didn't ease. "They probably have a reason."

"They don't have a reason to get a house, I'm allowed to be mad about it—"

"You're allowed to be mad, quieter." Sophie let go of her hair slowly.

Elizabeth shook her head free and rounded on Adrian instead, because he was the one standing there with his arms crossed, nothing about any of it seeming to touch him, and he was the one she actually wanted to yell at.

"You." She jabbed a finger at him. "This is you. You are the reason I can't do the one job you people actually asked me to help with. I have been walking around this town keeping an eye out for some woman I've never seen, based on a description that could be anybody, and the two of you have had time to go furniture shopping."

"We weren't furniture shopping," Adrian said.

"You know what I mean."

He didn't answer that. He reached into his jacket pocket instead — Elizabeth hadn't even seen him holding anything, he'd just had empty hands a second ago — and came out with an apple, unnaturally red, and bit into it, already visibly bored by the conversation.

"Are you serious right now," Elizabeth said.

He chewed. He didn't look at her while he did it — he was looking somewhere just past her shoulder, at the fountain, at the storefronts, anywhere that wasn't her face. She'd noticed it before so it didn't make her any less furious now.

"If you're finished," Adrian said, once he'd swallowed, "can we do this sitting down." He tipped his head toward the benches ringing the fountain at the center of the plaza. "It's a long walk to stand through."

And then he looked straight at her.

It wasn't anything dramatic. He didn't hold it, didn't make a thing of it. But it was the first time since they'd met that his eyes had actually landed on hers instead of sliding past them, and it caught Elizabeth so off guard that whatever she'd been about to say simply wasn't there anymore. She opened her mouth anyway, out of habit, with nothing behind it.

"We accept," Sophie said, before Elizabeth could fill the silence with something she'd regret. "Great. Benches. Let's go."

Elizabeth shot her a look. Sophie didn't return it; she was already herding them all toward the crosswalk, brisk and practiced about it.

"You're limping," Sophie said to Adrian as they crossed, not unkindly, just noting it.

"I am."

"What happened?" Sophie kept pace beside him, waiting.

"My brother put my back out." He said — flat, factual, no embarrassment in it at all. "We disagreed about something."

"About what?" Sophie wasn't letting it drop that easily.

"Doesn't matter anymore."

Sophie accepted that with a small shrug, apparently satisfied just to have asked, and Elizabeth bit down on the urge to ask what kind of disagreement ended in somebody's back getting thrown out, because she didn't actually want to know anything about these two that would make them more interesting to her.

The sun had gotten low enough that the sun was beaming down on into their vision, and Elizabeth had to squint hard the rest of the way to the benches massaging the bridge of her nose. When they reached the ring of benches around the fountain, she picked the one facing roughly west and sat with her back to the glare rather than into it, which at least meant she could glare properly at the person she actually wanted to glare at instead of fighting her own eyes the whole time.

Adrian sat across from her, Tristan beside him. Sophie dropped down next to Elizabeth, perfectly at ease, nothing in her manner giving away any of what had just happened.

"Okay." Elizabeth nodded at the apple, already down to its core, and his hand already closing around it. "What is that about? The apple thing. You've had one every single time I've seen you."

"I like the taste," Adrian said. "It settles me."

"Settles you."

"Yes."

"That's just stress eating. With a prop."

"Yeah, I guess it is," Adrian shrugged.

She rolled her eyes, which did nothing to settle the tension still sitting in her chest, and looked past him to Tristan instead, because at least Tristan answered things in full, coherent sentences. "What were you two actually doing in there?"

"Looking into places to stay. While we're in town," Tristan explained

"That's a real estate office," Sophie explained. "That's not a motel. That's for people buying something big— a house, long-term. You don't go in there for a place to crash for a few nights."

"No," Tristan agreed. "You don't."

"So." Sophie waited him out.

"So, yes. Exactly that." He glanced sideways at his brother, who was turning the crushed apple core over in his fingers and saying nothing. "My brother wanted something more permanent. We may be here a while yet."

"Great," Elizabeth said, and the bitterness came out sharper than she meant it to. "We'll be here a while too, then, since apparently nobody's told me what exactly I'm supposed to be looking for in the first place. I don't even know who the hell I'm hunting."

"That's —" Sophie put a hand briefly on Elizabeth's knee, a small let me — "that's actually a useful question If we knew more, we could actually be looking for the right thing instead of just any stranger who walks through town." She looked between the two brothers. "Could we also get a way to reach you? If we see something. Or if you need to reach us."

"As a matter of fact." Tristan's mouth curved, pleased with himself, and his hand went toward his own pocket. "We just bought a phone, for exactly that purpose. I realized that it might be helpful if we were going to ask for help."

But it was Adrian who produced it — out of his own jacket, where he'd apparently been holding onto it the whole time, and held it up before Tristan's hand had finished moving. "He can't work it," he said, by way of explanation, not looking especially pleased about the arrangement himself. "So I took custody of it," Adrian gave a side eye to Tristan.

Sophie leaned in to look at the screen. "That's the newest one, isn't it. How'd you two even afford that?"

"We keep funds set aside" — Tristan said — "for miscellaneous purchases."

"Give him your numbers," Adrian said, already thumbing the screen open, cutting past whatever Sophie might have said back to that. "He needs to learn how to put them in."

Elizabeth rattled hers off first, flat and fast, and Sophie gave hers after. Adrian read them back once to Tristan, who repeated them under his breath while he tapped them in with more care than the task really needed, still visibly unused to the thing in his hands.

Adrian rolled his eyes and took another bite of apple.

"There," Tristan said, when it was done, handing the phone back.

Adrian pocketed it and stood, and Tristan rose a beat after him, and that was apparently that — no goodbye beyond a short nod from Tristan, no word at all from Adrian, who was already turning toward the south path, hands back in his pockets, the whole conversation seeming to have cost him something.

Elizabeth watched them go until they'd crossed back out past the fountain and onto the storefront path, and didn't say anything until they were out of earshot.

"I really don't like him," she stated.

Sophie didn't argue.
