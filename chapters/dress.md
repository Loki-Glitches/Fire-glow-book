# Dress — Lucifer

Azrael was already at the corner by the hardware store when Lucifer got there, arms crossed, watching a delivery truck struggle to parallel park.

"You're later than usual."

"Something like that."

"How did it go?"

"Fine." Lucifer stopped beside him and watched the truck too, because it gave him somewhere to put his eyes that wasn't Azrael's face. "We watched the sunset. Went up on the roof for it."

"That's all."

"That's all."

Azrael studied him a moment longer than the answer warranted — not the words, something underneath them. Lucifer's hands stayed loose at his sides, weight even, nothing different to see. But there was less holding it together than there used to be, and Azrael — who had spent centuries reading exactly how much control another person had left — noticed it immediately. He didn't ask what had changed. He filed it and let it go.

Azrael pushed off the wall. "We should move. James wanted to catch us before noon."

They didn't get the chance to move first. James and Claire came around the corner from the direction of the bakery, James with a coffee in each hand and Claire already waving before she was close enough to be heard.

"There you two are." Claire fell into step first, taking her coffee back from James. "We've been meaning to catch you all week."

"Tristan." James lifted his chin at Azrael, then at Lucifer. "Adrian. You look like you slept, for once."

"I don't sleep."

"Sure." James grinned, unconvinced and not bothered by it. "Good for you either way."

Claire looked between the two of them, then settled on Lucifer, something already decided in her face. "Actually — perfect timing. I need an opinion today that isn't James's."

"What's wrong with my opinion?" James said.

"Nothing's wrong with it. It's just always the same opinion." Claire didn't look away from Lucifer. "He thinks I look good in everything. It's not helpful."

"That's not an insult, that's a compliment."

"It's useless is what it is." Claire turned fully to Lucifer now. "I'm trying on wedding dresses today. Come with me. You'll actually tell me the truth."

Lucifer looked at the dress forms in the shop window instead of at her. "I don't know anything about dresses."

"You don't need to. You just need to not be nice about it."

James laughed at that, then turned to Azrael before Lucifer had answered. "Tristan, you're up too, then. I've got three venues to look at before the weekend and Claire refuses to weigh in on any of them until they're narrowed down."

"I have things to do."

James started walking, done with the subject. "Come on. It's a couple of hours."

Azrael didn't move. "I have actual work."

"You have all day," Lucifer said. "You've told me that yourself. More than once."

"That's not the same as wanting to spend it looking at parking lots with a stranger."

"He's not a stranger, he's your friend." Claire missed the weight under the word entirely, landing on something true by accident.

Azrael's jaw tightened. He said nothing for a moment, just looked at Lucifer, unimpressed. This was somehow his doing, and they both knew it.

"Go," Lucifer said. "It's a couple of hours."

Azrael's mouth flattened. "You're using my own line against me."

"It worked on me."

Azrael exhaled through his nose and turned to James. "Lead the way."

"Excellent." James clapped him once on the shoulder, which Azrael tolerated rather than welcomed, and started them walking before anyone changed their mind. "Actually — while I've got you. Not that I'm asking for money, I would never ask you for money —"

"You're asking for money," Azrael said.

"I'm asking for advice." James held up his free hand. "I put everything I had into her ring. Which was worth it, obviously, don't repeat that to her, she'll get insufferable about it. But now I'm supposed to also produce a wedding band, and I've got exactly none of the budget left for it. If you hear of anyone who does that kind of work cheap, or knows someone who does — I'd take it."

"I'll keep it in mind." Azrael's tone had already closed the subject.

Claire had already turned the other way, toward the shop with the dress forms in the window, and didn't appear to have heard any of it. Lucifer followed her, and behind him he heard James start in on square footage and whether a barn counted as rustic or just as a barn.

---

The shop held the smell of steam and new fabric, and a woman with a tape measure looped around her neck met them at the racks before Claire had finished taking her coat off.

"Second time this month," the woman said, already pulling three dresses without being asked. "Try these first."

Claire took them into the fitting room, and Lucifer sat in the chair provided for exactly this purpose, angled at the small platform in front of the mirrors, and waited.

The first dress had too much at the shoulders, a stiff double layer that fought the line of everything below it.

"Not that one." Lucifer didn't get up from the chair to look closer.

"You didn't even look at it properly."

"The shoulders are wrong." He nodded at the platform without leaning forward. "It's fighting the rest of the dress instead of matching it."

Claire looked at herself in the mirror for another second, then nodded once and went back in.

The second dress fit better through the middle but pulled tight and shiny across the back when she turned.

"Not that one either." Lucifer tipped his head at the mirror behind her.

"Why."

"It's too tight in the back." He nodded at her reflection. "You can see the seam working to hold it."

Claire crossed her arms. "You can see a seam working."

"You asked me to look. I'm looking." Lucifer didn't back off it.

The shop woman, refolding something at the counter, didn't bother hiding that she was listening. "He's right about the back, for what it's worth."

Claire disappeared again, and came out a third time in something plainer — simple, unremarkable, the kind of dress that would photograph fine and mean nothing.

"That one's fine," Lucifer said.

"Fine's not the goal." Claire smoothed the skirt, unconvinced.

"Then don't ask me if it's fine."

Claire laughed — a real one, surprised out of her — and went back for the fourth.

She came out slower this time, one hand still smoothing the fabric at her hip. The dress fell in a single clean line from her shoulders to the floor, the seams along the bodice so fine they read as one unbroken piece rather than dozens stitched and pressed and stitched again. Someone had spent real time getting the fall of it right.

Lucifer didn't say anything right away. It wasn't Claire he was looking at — it was the work in the dress, the hundred small decisions that had gone into it. Something settled in his chest with no use he could put it to, nothing it was for. It passed almost as soon as he noticed it.

"That one," he said.

"Yeah?" She turned another degree toward the mirror.

"That one."

The shop woman came over and turned the tag pinned inside the collar between two fingers, then named a number that made Claire's face do something complicated.

"That's — a lot more than the others."

"It's hand-finished." The woman let the tag drop back against the fabric. "The beading alone is three days' work."

Claire checked the tag a second time. It hadn't moved. She looked at Lucifer, not asking him to fix it, just letting the disappointment show.

"Is this the display piece, or do you have it in her size in stock?" Lucifer asked.

"It's the only one we've got in that size right now."

"Then it's already been tried on." Lucifer glanced at the dress still on Claire. "Handled. Not new-new."

"It's been tried on twice, but it's never left the shop, so it's still—"

"It's not new-new." Lucifer talked over the rest of it. "You've said that yourself, technically. And you'll want the size out of your inventory before the season turns over anyway — it's less useful to you sitting on a rack than it is sold today."

The woman opened her mouth, closed it, and looked at the tag again. "I could do ten percent."

"Fifteen. That's a fair number for both of us."

The woman wrote it up before she could think better of it. Claire watched the whole exchange from the platform, dress still on, expression somewhere between delight and alarm at how easily he'd done it.

"You didn't lie once," she said, once the woman had gone to ring it up.

"I didn't need to."

"That's almost worse."

They walked back together once the dress was boxed and paid for, Claire carrying it in both arms, talking the whole way about the beading, the fifteen percent, whether James would even notice the difference in price. Lucifer let her talk. He didn't bring up the moment on the platform, and it evidently hadn't occurred to her there'd been one to bring up.

James and Azrael were waiting near the corner where they'd split, James still describing something with his hands, Azrael standing with his arms crossed, clearly full of opinions about barns he wasn't sharing.

"There they are," James said, spotting the box in Claire's arms before he'd finished the sentence. "That good a day?"

"You have no idea." Claire went straight to him, already starting in on the beading again before she'd fully reached his side. James wrapped an arm around her, said something Lucifer didn't catch, and the two of them started off toward the bakery, already talking about something else entirely.

Lucifer crossed the last stretch of sidewalk toward his brother.

"Well?" Azrael said.

"She found one."

"That's all."

"That's all," Lucifer said, and Azrael, who had clearly had an afternoon of his own opinions about parking lots, didn't push for more than that either.
