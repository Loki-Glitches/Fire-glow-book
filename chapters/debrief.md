# Debrief — Elizabeth

Elizabeth watched the two of them walk off across the plaza, Adrian with his hands jammed in his pockets and Tristan saying something low that Adrian didn't answer. Neither one looked back.

When they were out of view Sophie bent down to pick up her bag from the bench, slung it over her shoulder, and started toward the car without saying anything else, which was somehow worse than if she'd tried to talk Elizabeth out of it.

"That's it?" Elizabeth said, catching up to her. "No 'he's not that bad,' no 'give him a chance'?"

"I was there for the whole thing," Sophie said. "I'm not going to stand here and tell you he's a delight."

They didn't talk much on the drive. Sophie kept both hands on the wheel and didn't bother with the radio. Outside, the storefronts went by in their familiar order — the grocery, the bank, the coffee house — the afternoon sun low enough now that it flared off the windows every few seconds. Elizabeth stared out at all of it without really registering it.

"You're doing the thing," Sophie said, eyes on the road, "where you go quiet instead of yelling. That's usually worse."

"I'm not doing anything."

"Okay," Sophie raised a hand in surrender.

At the stop sign before the turn onto Elizabeth's street, Sophie glanced over instead of pulling through right away. "For what it's worth, I don't think he did it to be cruel. I think he's just like that."

"That's not actually comforting." Elizabeth crossed her arms over her chest.

"I know. I wasn't trying to comfort you. I was trying to explain him." Sophie eased through the intersection.

Elizabeth kept replaying it — the house, the apple, the one second he'd actually looked at her instead of past her, which still didn't make any sense, doing her best not to think about it. By the time Sophie pulled into the driveway, Elizabeth had mostly stopped being angry and started just being tired of being angry, which wasn't the same thing but felt close enough.

Sam was already coming down the porch steps before Sophie had the car fully in park. He must have been watching from the front window. The porch light was still not replaced, leaving the porch in darkness, and down the street somebody's sprinkler was still ticking back and forth across a lawn. Sam didn't say anything, just opened Elizabeth's car door for her and then Sophie's, and fell into step between them on the way up the walk, close enough that his shoulder knocked Elizabeth's a few times.

"So," he said, once they were on the porch. "How'd it go?"

"I don't even know," Elizabeth said. She picked up her pace and stormed into the house.

He looked at her for a second, clearly deciding whether that needed following up on, and then apparently decided it didn't, not right now. He held the door for Sophie, said something about a show he'd been halfway through, and peeled off toward the living room without another word. A minute later they could hear the TV come on low.

Upstairs, Elizabeth didn't bother with the lights. She just went straight across her room and dropped face-first onto the bed, which told Sophie everything she needed to know before Elizabeth said a word.

"He is the single most exhausting person I have ever met," Elizabeth said into the comforter. "I asked him one question. One. And somehow it turned into a whole thing about a house I didn't even know they'd bought until he just — announced it, like it was nothing, like we hadn't spent the entire last however-long thinking we were looking for a missing woman together."

"You've said all this already." Sophie sat down on the edge of the bed near Elizabeth's feet.

"I'm saying it again."

"I noticed."

Elizabeth turned her head sideways so she wasn't talking directly into the mattress. "He didn't even apologize. He just ate an apple at me."

"I don't think it's personal," Sophie encouraged her.

"Everything he does is personal. It's personal that he won't look at anyone. It's personal that he won't answer a straight question. The fact that he ate an apple instead of saying sorry is extremely personal, Sophie."

Sophie didn't say anything to that, just let her keep going.

"And the house." Elizabeth threw an arm over her eyes. "This whole time they already had money sitting around for a house."

Sophie reached over and patted her knee, which wasn't really comfort so much as acknowledgment that she'd heard it.

"And then he did the thing," Elizabeth said, quieter than the rest of it.

"What thing?"

Elizabeth lowered her arm just enough to look at Sophie, then put it back over her eyes. "Nothing. Forget it."

Sophie let her go a while longer — the house, the apple, the single unbearable second of actual eye contact, which Elizabeth left vaguer than the rest, not totally sure why — and when Elizabeth finally stopped talking, Sophie just sat there with a hand resting on her ankle until her breathing evened back out.

"Feel better?" Sophie said.

"No. But I'm done, if that's what you're asking."

"Good. Because I have an idea, and I need you to not be mid-rant when I bring it up."

Elizabeth pushed herself up onto her elbows. "What."

"We have his number. Well — Tristan's." Sophie was already pulling it out of her pocket. "I could just text him. Ask him stuff. You don't even have to be in the conversation."

"I don't want to talk to either of them right now."

"I know. That's why I said I'd do it." Sophie was already thumbing through to find the thread. "You don't have to do anything except tell me what to ask."

Elizabeth flopped back down, but she was listening now, which Sophie was able to notice, because she was still going without needing to wait for Elizabeth to agree out loud.

"Okay. Eve stuff first, since that's the actual point." Sophie started typing, reading each line back to Elizabeth half under her breath before she sent it.

**Any new leads on Eve?**

They both watched the screen. It took a minute — long enough that Sophie had started scrolling something else — before three dots appeared and then his answer came through.

**Not yet. Still looking.**

"Riveting," Elizabeth said.

"Give it a second." Sophie was already typing the next one.

**What does she actually look like? Nobody's ever described her to us. Feels like something we should know if we're supposed to be watching for her.**

This one took longer. Long enough that Elizabeth sat up to see the screen herself.

**Long hair. Wavy. Black.**

"That's it?" Elizabeth said. "That's the whole description?"

"I guess that's the whole description." Sophie was already typing again.

**Anything Else?**

**She may talk a bit strangely. She may not always know what language to speak in.**

Neither of them said anything right away. Elizabeth read it over again, trying to picture it — a total stranger walking up to someone at a register or a bus stop and just launching into Spanish for no reason anyone could follow. It didn't fit with anything else they'd been told about this woman, but then again they'd barely been told anything at all, so there wasn't much for it to clash with.

Elizabeth made a face at the phone. "That's bizarre."

"It's something, at least." Sophie didn't look up from the screen. "More than we had before."

"Barely."

"I said something, not much." Sophie glanced at her sideways. "Okay. My turn, or yours — I'm asking the other stuff now. You don't have to claim any of it."

"Don't tell him it's from me."

"I wasn't going to." Sophie was already typing.

**Random question — why is Adrian always so quiet? Like, barely talks unless he has to.**

The reply came faster this time.

**He's not much of a talker. He hasn't been not for a very long time.**

"Well it's more information," Sophie shrugged.

**What's the apple thing? Does he always have one on him? Is that a whole thing?**

**I am not actually sure why he does it. He hasn't done it around me before we got here.**

"See, that one I actually believe," Elizabeth said. "I would also stop asking."

"Last one." Sophie glanced at her before she typed it, giving her a chance to veto it. Elizabeth didn't. "This one's really yours. I'm just typing it."

**One more. Why does he even care this much about finding her? He doesn't seem to care about much else.**

This reply took the longest of all of them. Long enough that Sophie set the phone face-up on the comforter between them and they both just watched it, waiting, which felt a little stupid once Elizabeth noticed they were doing it.

**It's not something I am able to explain to you. She is someone that he would like to forget but she needs to be found quickly.**

Neither of them reached for the phone right away. Elizabeth lay there with her eyes on the words, turning them over, looking for an actual fact buried somewhere inside them, and not finding one. It didn't explain why he cared or what this woman was to him — it just confirmed that there was a reason, which somehow felt worse than getting nothing at all.

Elizabeth read it twice. "That is the least helpful sentence I have ever received."

"Agreed." Sophie picked the phone back up, typed a quick **thanks, let us know if anything changes**, and set it face-down on the nightstand, done with it for the night.

Elizabeth lay back and stared at the ceiling. "So. We know she has black hair and apparently bad language skills."

"We know more than we did before," Sophie said. "That's something."

"It's barely anything."

"It's not nothing, though."

Elizabeth didn't answer that one, because Sophie wasn't wrong, exactly — it just didn't seem to matter much compared to everything it hadn't told them. She pressed the heels of her hands into her eyes and held them there, she genuinely did not understand how two brothers could manage to say so many words and leave someone knowing almost nothing at all.

"How is it this hard," Elizabeth said, mostly to the ceiling, "to get a straight answer out of either one of them."
