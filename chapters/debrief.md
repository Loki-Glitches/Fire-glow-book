# Debrief — Elizabeth

Elizabeth watched the two of them walk off across the plaza, Adrian with his hands jammed in his pockets and Tristan saying something low that he didn't answer. Neither one looked back.

"I really don't like him," she said.

Sophie didn't argue the point. She bent down to pick up her bag from the bench, slung it over her shoulder, and started toward the car without saying anything else, which was somehow worse than if she'd tried to talk Elizabeth out of it.

"That's it?" Elizabeth said, catching up to her. "No 'he's not that bad,' no 'give him a chance'?"

"I was there for the whole thing," Sophie said. "I'm not going to stand here and tell you he's a delight."

They didn't talk much on the drive. Elizabeth kept replaying it — the house, the apple, the one second he'd actually looked at her instead of past her, which still didn't make any sense and which she was doing her best not to think about. By the time Sophie pulled into the driveway, Elizabeth had mostly stopped being angry and started just being tired of being angry, which wasn't the same thing but felt close enough.

Sam was already coming down the porch steps before Sophie had the car fully in park. He must have been watching from the front window — he does that, pretends he isn't, and then he's outside before anyone's even cut the engine. He didn't say anything, just opened Elizabeth's door for her and then Sophie's, and fell into step between them on the way up the walk.

"So," he said, once they were on the porch. "How'd it go?"

"I don't even know," Elizabeth said.

He looked at her for a second, clearly deciding whether that needed following up on, and then apparently decided it didn't, not right now. He held the door for them, said something about a show he'd been halfway through, and peeled off toward the living room without another word. A minute later they could hear the TV come on low.

Upstairs, Elizabeth didn't bother with the lights. She just went straight across her room and dropped face-first onto the bed, which probably told Sophie everything she needed to know before Elizabeth said a word.

"He is the single most exhausting person I have ever met," Elizabeth said into the comforter. "I asked him one question. One. And somehow it turned into a whole thing about a house I didn't even know they'd bought until he just — announced it, like it was nothing, like we hadn't spent the entire last however-long thinking we were looking for a missing woman together."

"You've said all this already." Sophie sat down on the edge of the bed near Elizabeth's feet.

"I'm saying it again."

"I noticed."

Elizabeth turned her head sideways so she wasn't talking directly into the mattress. "He didn't even apologize. He just ate an apple at me."

"He does that a lot," Sophie said, not unkindly. "I don't think it's personal."

"Everything he does is personal. It's personal that he won't look at anyone. It's personal that he won't answer a straight question. The fact that he ate an apple instead of saying sorry is extremely personal, Sophie."

Sophie let her go a while longer — the house, the apple, the single unbearable second of actual eye contact, which Elizabeth left vaguer than the rest, not totally sure why — and when Elizabeth finally stopped talking, Sophie just sat there with a hand resting on her ankle until her breathing evened back out.

"Feel better?" Sophie said.

"No. But I'm done, if that's what you're asking."

"Good. Because I have an idea, and I need you to not be mid-rant when I bring it up."

Elizabeth pushed herself up onto her elbows. "What."

"I have his number. Well — Tristan's. It's in my phone from the park." Sophie was already pulling it out of her pocket. "I could just text him. Ask him stuff. You don't even have to be in the conversation."

"I don't want to talk to either of them right now."

"I know. That's why I said I'd do it." Sophie was already thumbing through to find the thread. "You don't have to do anything except tell me what to ask."

Elizabeth flopped back down, but she was listening now, which Sophie clearly noticed, because she kept going without waiting for Elizabeth to agree out loud.

"Okay. Eve stuff first, since that's the actual point." Sophie started typing, reading each line back to Elizabeth half under her breath before she sent it.

**Any new leads on Eve?**

They both watched the screen. It took a minute — long enough that Sophie had started scrolling something else — before three dots appeared and then his answer came through.

**Not yet. Still looking.**

"Riveting," Elizabeth said.

"Give it a second." Sophie was already typing the next one.

**What does she actually look like? Nobody's ever described her to us. Feels like something we should know if we're supposed to be watching for her.**

This one took longer. Long enough that Elizabeth sat up to see the screen herself.

**Long hair. Wavy. Black.**

"That's it?" Elizabeth said. "That's the whole description?"

"I guess that's the whole description." Sophie was already typing again.

**Does she speak English?**

**Mostly. She knows a lot of languages, actually — more than she needs. She doesn't always pick the right one for whoever she's talking to. Don't be surprised if she tries Spanish on you for no reason. She does that to people who don't speak a word of it.**

Elizabeth made a face at the phone. "That's bizarre."

"It's something, at least." Sophie didn't look up from the screen. "More than we had before."

"Barely."

"I said something, not much." Sophie glanced at her sideways. "Okay. My turn, or yours — I'm asking the other stuff now. You don't have to claim any of it."

"Don't tell him it's from me."

"I wasn't going to." Sophie was already typing.

**Random question — why is Adrian always so quiet? Like, barely talks unless he has to.**

The reply came faster this time.

**He's not much of a talker. Never has been, not for as long as I've known him.**

"That's not an answer," Elizabeth said. "That's a non-answer shaped like an answer."

"Welcome to texting his brother." Sophie was typing the next one without waiting for Elizabeth to weigh in.

**Also — the apple thing. Does he always have one on him? Is that a whole thing?**

**Habit. He's had it a long time. I've stopped asking.**

"See, that one I actually believe," Elizabeth said. "Because I would also stop asking. I would give up entirely."

"Last one." Sophie glanced at her before she typed it, giving her a chance to veto it. Elizabeth didn't. "This one's really yours. I'm just typing it."

**One more. Why does he even care this much about finding her? He doesn't seem to care about much else.**

This reply took the longest of all of them. Long enough that Sophie set the phone face-up on the comforter between them and they both just watched it, waiting, which felt a little stupid once Elizabeth noticed they were doing it.

**It's complicated. She matters to him. That's about as much as I can tell you.**

Elizabeth read it twice. "That is the least helpful sentence I have ever received."

"Agreed." Sophie picked the phone back up, typed a quick **thanks, let us know if anything changes**, and set it face-down on the nightstand, done with it for the night.

Elizabeth lay back and stared at the ceiling. "So. We know she has black hair and apparently bad Spanish."

"We know more than we did before," Sophie said. "That's something."

"It's barely anything."

"It's not nothing, though."

Elizabeth didn't answer that one, because Sophie wasn't wrong, exactly — it just didn't seem to matter much compared to everything it hadn't told them. She pressed the heels of her hands into her eyes and held them there, not because anything hurt, just because she genuinely did not understand how two brothers could manage to say so many words and leave someone knowing almost nothing at all.

"How is it this hard," Elizabeth said, mostly to the ceiling, "to get a straight answer out of either one of them."

Sophie didn't have an answer for that either. She just reached over and turned on the lamp.
