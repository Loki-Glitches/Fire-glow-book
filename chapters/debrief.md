# Debrief — Elizabeth

I watched the two of them walk off across the plaza, Adrian with his hands jammed in his pockets and Tristan saying something low that he didn't answer. Neither one looked back.

"I really don't like him," I said.

Sophie didn't argue the point. She bent down to pick up her bag from the bench, slung it over her shoulder, and started toward the car without saying anything else, which was somehow worse than if she'd tried to talk me out of it.

"That's it?" I said, catching up to her. "No 'he's not that bad,' no 'give him a chance'?"

"I was there for the whole thing," Sophie said. "I'm not going to stand here and tell you he's a delight."

We didn't talk much on the drive. I kept replaying it — the house, the apple, the one second he'd actually looked at me instead of past me, which still didn't make any sense and which I was doing my best not to think about. By the time Sophie pulled into the driveway I'd mostly stopped being angry and started just being tired of being angry, which wasn't the same thing but felt close enough.

Sam was already coming down the porch steps before Sophie had the car fully in park. He must have been watching from the front window — he does that, pretends he isn't, and then he's outside before you've even cut the engine. He didn't say anything, just opened my door for me and then Sophie's, and fell into step between us on the way up the walk.

"So," he said, once we were on the porch. "How'd it go?"

"I don't even know," I said.

He looked at me for a second, clearly deciding whether that needed following up on, and then apparently decided it didn't, not right now. He held the door for us, said something about a show he'd been halfway through, and peeled off toward the living room without another word. A minute later I could hear the TV come on low.

Upstairs, I didn't bother with the lights. I just went straight across my room and dropped face-first onto the bed, which probably told Sophie everything she needed to know before I said a word.

"He is the single most exhausting person I have ever met," I said into the comforter. "I asked him one question. One. And somehow it turned into a whole thing about a house I didn't even know they'd bought until he just — announced it, like it was nothing, like we hadn't spent the entire last however-long thinking we were looking for a missing woman together."

"You've said all this already." Sophie sat down on the edge of the bed near my feet.

"I'm saying it again."

"I noticed."

I turned my head sideways so I wasn't talking directly into the mattress. "He didn't even apologize. He just ate an apple at me."

"He does that a lot," Sophie said, not unkindly. "I don't think it's personal."

"Everything he does is personal. It's personal that he won't look at anyone. It's personal that he won't answer a straight question. The fact that he ate an apple instead of saying sorry is extremely personal, Sophie."

She let me go a while longer — I ran through the house, the apple, the single unbearable second of actual eye contact (I left that part vaguer than the rest, I'm not totally sure why) — and when I finally stopped talking she just sat there with her hand resting on my ankle until my breathing evened back out.

"Feel better?" she said.

"No. But I'm done, if that's what you're asking."

"Good. Because I have an idea, and I need you to not be mid-rant when I bring it up."

I pushed myself up onto my elbows. "What."

"I have his number. Well — Tristan's. It's in my phone from the park." She was already pulling it out of her pocket. "I could just text him. Ask him stuff. You don't even have to be in the conversation."

"I don't want to talk to either of them right now."

"I know. That's why I said I'd do it." She was already thumbing through to find the thread. "You don't have to do anything except tell me what to ask."

I flopped back down, but I was listening now, which she clearly noticed, because she kept going without waiting for me to agree out loud.

"Okay. Eve stuff first, since that's the actual point." She started typing, reading each line back to me half under her breath before she sent it.

**Any new leads on Eve?**

We both watched the screen. It took a minute — long enough that Sophie had started scrolling something else — before three dots appeared and then his answer came through.

**Not yet. Still looking.**

"Riveting," I said.

"Give it a second." Sophie was already typing the next one.

**What does she actually look like? Nobody's ever described her to us. Feels like something we should know if we're supposed to be watching for her.**

This one took longer. Long enough that I sat up to see the screen myself.

**Long hair. Wavy. Black.**

"That's it?" I said. "That's the whole description?"

"I guess that's the whole description." Sophie was already typing again.

**Does she speak English?**

**Mostly. She knows a lot of languages, actually — more than she needs. She doesn't always pick the right one for whoever she's talking to. Don't be surprised if she tries Spanish on you for no reason. She does that to people who don't speak a word of it.**

I made a face at the phone. "That's bizarre."

"It's something, at least." Sophie didn't look up from the screen. "More than we had before."

"Barely."

"I said something, not much." She glanced at me sideways. "Okay. My turn, or yours — I'm asking the other stuff now. You don't have to claim any of it."

"Don't tell him it's from me."

"I wasn't going to." She was already typing.

**Random question — why is Adrian always so quiet? Like, barely talks unless he has to.**

The reply came faster this time.

**He's not much of a talker. Never has been, not for as long as I've known him.**

"That's not an answer," I said. "That's a non-answer shaped like an answer."

"Welcome to texting his brother." Sophie was typing the next one without waiting for me to weigh in.

**Also — the apple thing. Does he always have one on him? Is that a whole thing?**

**Habit. He's had it a long time. I've stopped asking.**

"See, that one I actually believe," I said. "Because I would also stop asking. I would give up entirely."

"Last one." Sophie glanced at me before she typed it, giving me a chance to veto it. I didn't. "This one's really yours. I'm just typing it."

**One more. Why does he even care this much about finding her? He doesn't seem to care about much else.**

This reply took the longest of all of them. Long enough that Sophie set the phone face-up on the comforter between us and we both just watched it, waiting, which felt a little stupid once I noticed we were doing it.

**It's complicated. She matters to him. That's about as much as I can tell you.**

I read it twice. "That is the least helpful sentence I have ever received."

"Agreed." Sophie picked the phone back up, typed a quick **thanks, let us know if anything changes**, and set it face-down on the nightstand, done with it for the night.

I lay back and stared at the ceiling. "So. We know she has black hair and apparently bad Spanish."

"We know more than we did before," Sophie said. "That's something."

"It's barely anything."

"It's not nothing, though."

I didn't answer that one, because she wasn't wrong, exactly — it just didn't seem to matter much compared to everything it hadn't told us. I pressed the heels of my hands into my eyes and held them there, not because anything hurt, just because I genuinely did not understand how two brothers could manage to say so many words and leave you knowing almost nothing at all.

"How is it this hard," I said, mostly to the ceiling, "to get a straight answer out of either one of them."

Sophie didn't have an answer for that either. She just reached over and turned on the lamp.
