# Chapter 3 — Elizabeth

Elizabeth came downstairs with her hair still damp and the bump on her forehead half-hidden under it, and the kitchen already smelled like something worth being awake for.

Sam didn't look up from the stove. "You're going to want to sit down for this one."

"For eggs?"

"For my eggs." He nudged the pan, and something in it hissed obligingly. "There's a difference."

She dropped onto the stool across the counter from him and let her chin rest on her folded arms, watching him work with the ease of someone who'd watched him do this exact thing several hundred mornings in a row. He had flour on one forearm from something that had nothing to do with eggs.

She almost told him about the headache that had followed her down the stairs. She didn't.

"What happened to your head." He said it without turning around, which was somehow worse than if he'd made a thing of it.

"I fought the nightstand."

"Who won?"

"It's a nightstand, Sam. It doesn't have to try."

That got a real laugh out of him, short and surprised out of him, and he finally turned around long enough to look at the bump properly, tilting her chin up with two fingers still faintly floury. "You're going to have a bruise."

"I'm aware."

"Ice it."

"I'm aware of that too."

"Doesn't mean you'll do it." He went back to the eggs. "You never do."

A postcard from somewhere with better weather than here was stuck to the fridge with a magnet shaped like a lemon. Neither of them had mentioned it since it arrived.

"Are you still going to sign up for that welding class?" she asked, mostly to the counter.

"Maybe once things calm down."

"Things are always about to calm down."

"Eat your eggs."

She ate her eggs. It wasn't a real answer and they both knew it, and she filed it away in the place she kept things she wasn't going to fix by asking twice.

By the time the plates were in the sink she'd made the list in her head twice already — the pharmacy for her prescription, the hardware store for a bulb since the porch light had died again, and somewhere in there a stop at the good bakery for the thing Sophie liked, which was really just an excuse to see Sophie.

"You need anything from town?" She pulled her hair back and started hunting for her keys.

"A break." Sam dried his hands on the towel over his shoulder. "But bring something back from the bakery if she drags you there."

"She always drags me there."

"That's why I said it."

Her keys were exactly where she'd left them, which felt like a small miracle every single time. She found them on the second try anyway.

"Lock the door behind you," he called after her.

"It locks itself."

"Lock it anyway."

She locked it anyway, because arguing with Sam over a door was never worth the two seconds it would have saved, and stepped out into the last real heat of summer with the whole list still running in her head.
