# Chapter 3 — Elizabeth

Elizabeth came downstairs with her hair still damp and the bump on her forehead half-hidden under it, and the kitchen already smelled like something worth being awake for.

Sam didn't look up from the stove. "You're going to want to sit down for this one."

"For eggs?" She leaned over the counter to look into the pan.

"For my eggs." He nudged the pan, and something in it hissed obligingly. "There's a difference."

She giggled as she dropped onto the stool across the counter from him and let her chin rest on her folded arms, watching him work. He had flour on one forearm from something that had nothing to do with eggs.

She almost told him about the headache that had followed her down the stairs. She didn't.

"What happened to your head?" He asked it without turning around.

"I fought the nightstand." She touched the bump and winced.

"Who won?" He flipped the eggs.

"It's a nightstand, Sam. It doesn't have to try." She dropped her hand back onto the counter.

That got a real laugh out of him, short and surprised, and he turned around long enough to look at the bump, tilting her chin up with two fingers still faintly floury. "You're going to have a bruise."

"I'm aware." She pulled her chin back.

"Ice it."

"I'm aware of that too." She reached past him for the orange juice instead.

"Doesn't mean you'll do it." He went back to the eggs. "You never do."

He slid a plate across the counter to her a minute later, and she had her fork in it before he'd fully let go.

A postcard from somewhere with better weather than here was stuck to the fridge with a lemon-shaped magnet. Neither of them had mentioned it since it arrived.

"Heard anything else from them?" she asked around a mouthful of eggs, nodding at it.

"Just that." He didn't look over. "Some island. Said the water's nice."

"That's it?" She set her fork down.

"That's it." He plated his own eggs and leaned against the counter to eat standing up.

"Hot again today." She glanced toward the window, where the light already had that thick, early look to it. "Radio said triple digits by three."

"Then don't melt in a parking lot arguing with a shopping cart."

"That was one time."

"It was memorable." He nudged her water glass an inch closer to her. "Drink something before you go. You get squirrelly when you're dehydrated."

"I do not get squirrelly."

"You alphabetized the spice rack last summer at ninety-four degrees."

"That needed to happen regardless of the temperature."

"Are you still going to sign up for that welding class?" She pushed the eggs around her plate.

"Maybe once things calm down."

"Things are always about to calm down." She pointed her fork at him.

"Eat your eggs." He pointed his own fork right back.

She ate her eggs. It wasn't a real answer and they both knew it, and she didn't ask again.

By the time the plates were in the sink, she'd already run through the list twice — the pharmacy for her prescription, the hardware store for a new bulb since the porch light had died again, and the bakery for the thing Sophie liked, which was really just an excuse to see Sophie.

"You need anything from town?" She pulled her hair back, already hunting for her keys.

"Actually — yeah." Sam dried his hands on the towel over his shoulder. "Chicken, if they've got anything decent. And whatever looks good for a salad, your call."

"That's oddly specific for a man who ate cereal standing over the sink last night."

"Cereal doesn't count if you're standing up. Everyone knows that."

"That is not a rule."

"It's my rule. I'm allowed one."

"You're doing dinner too? It's not even ten."

"Someone should feed you right while I've still got the excuse to." He tossed the towel over his shoulder. "Bring something back from the bakery too, if Sophie drags you there."

"She always drags me there." She checked her pockets for her phone.

"That's why I said it."

Her keys were exactly where she'd left them. It still took her two tries to find them.

"Lock the door behind you," he called after her.

"It locks itself." She pulled the door open.

"Lock it anyway."

She locked it anyway and stepped out into the last real heat of summer, the list still running in her head.

The porch light hung dead in its socket and she added it to the list a third time for good measure. The car was an oven with a door handle. She rolled the windows down before she started the engine, more out of habit than any hope it would help, and pulled out of the driveway already planning the fastest route through the pharmacy so she'd have time left over for Sophie.
