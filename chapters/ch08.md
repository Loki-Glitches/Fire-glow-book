# Chapter 8 — Elizabeth

The walk back toward Main Street went slower than the walk out had — five people don't move at the same pace as four, and nobody had bothered to sort out whose pace that should be.

"So," Claire said, falling into step on Elizabeth's other side, "you're just going to invite yourself along."

"You invited me. I distinctly remember being invited."

"I said 'if you wanted.'" Claire held up a finger. "That's not the same as an invitation."

"It's exactly the same as an invitation."

James laughed at that from somewhere behind them, still catching up with the basket. "She's not wrong. You do this every time you don't actually want the answer to be no."

"I don't know what you're talking about." Claire looped her arm through Elizabeth's, wide-eyed and entirely unconvincing. "Anyway. Tell me you're at least a little excited about the wedding."

"I'm a lot excited about the wedding. I've known you were getting married for less than an hour and I'm already emotionally invested."

"That tracks." Claire smirked. "You cried at my dentist's retirement party."

"He'd been my dentist since I was seven. That was earned."

Adrian walked slightly ahead of the three of them, not participating, which nobody seemed to expect him to. Tristan kept pace beside him, watching the storefronts go by with the same flat attention he'd given the porch.

"Where are we even going," Elizabeth asked, over Claire's shoulder. "Is there a plan, or are we just orbiting the block until something happens."

"There's a plan," James said. "The plan is walking around until something happens. It's a very sophisticated plan."

"You two are supposed to be finding someone. That seems like it should involve more than walking."

"It involves exactly this much walking," Adrian said, without turning around. "For now."

"That was almost a full sentence. Feeling talkative?"

He didn't answer that, and Claire caught Elizabeth's eye and grinned, clearly having noticed the same thing.

They passed the bakery, still holding the last of its morning smell out onto the sidewalk, and Elizabeth's stomach reminded her she hadn't actually eaten since the roll from that morning. She didn't stop. Claire didn't stop either, apparently on a schedule only she and the florist knew about, but she did steal a glance through the window, filing it away for later.

"You've been quiet," Claire said to Elizabeth, low enough that it was mostly just for her. "You okay? You get that face when something's bugging you."

"I don't have a face."

"You have several faces." Claire tapped her own cheek. "That one specifically means you're turning something over."

"I'm fine. I'm just trying to figure out why two grown men need a woman who knows what everyone in this town looks like to find one specific stranger, instead of just asking around themselves."

"Maybe they tried that already." Claire didn't sound convinced.

"Maybe." Elizabeth glanced ahead at Adrian's back, which gave away exactly nothing. "Maybe I just like a mystery."

A woman sweeping the front step of the dry cleaner's looked up as they passed, her attention skipping past James and Claire — old news, both of them — and landing on Adrian and Tristan instead, new enough to earn a second look in a town this size.

"Morning," she said, mostly to Elizabeth. "New friends?"

"Something like that."

The woman went back to her sweeping, apparently satisfied.

"Small town," Claire said, unnecessary, since Elizabeth had lived there her whole life and knew exactly how small it was.

"You'd think after twenty years I'd stop being surprised by it."

"You say that every single time." Claire bumped her with an elbow.

"It's true every single time."

James caught up enough to walk backward in front of them for a few steps, which he did badly and without any apparent self-preservation instinct. "You're not going to let up on this, are you."

"Not until someone gives me something real. Or the ring story. I'll take either."

"That's blackmail," James said.

"It's leverage. There's a difference."

"I don't think there is." James shook his head.

"There is if I say there is." Elizabeth glanced at Tristan, testing the same question on a new target. "You could just tell me. Save everyone the trouble."

"No," Tristan said.

"Not even a little?"

"No," Tristan said again.

"You're worse than he is." She nodded at Adrian, who didn't dispute it.

The afternoon heat had thickened again, the same shimmer from her two trips through it already that morning.

"You're sure you're okay with this," James said, quieter now, falling into step beside her while Claire got briefly distracted by a window display. "You don't actually know these two."

"I know them as well as I knew you two hours after you started dating Claire, and that turned out fine."

"That's different." James frowned.

"Is it? You vouched for them. That's usually enough for me." She bumped his shoulder with hers. "Also I'm curious. You know I can't help that part."

"I do know that part." He didn't look entirely reassured, but he let it go — she'd known him long enough to recognize when he'd decided pushing further wouldn't get him anywhere new.

"How much longer are we walking before someone tells me the actual plan," Elizabeth asked, aiming it at Adrian this time.

"Not much longer."

"That's not a real answer," Elizabeth said.

"It's the true one, though." He didn't look back at her.

"That's not an answer either. That's the same non-answer in fewer words."

He let that stand.

They were nearly to Main Street before Claire checked her phone and made a face at it.

"The florist wants an answer on centerpieces today or we lose the date on the good ones." She looked at James. "That's us."

"That's us," James agreed, hefting the basket like it had been waiting for an excuse. "You've got backup." He nodded at Adrian and Tristan. "Try not to lose her."

"I'm not luggage," Elizabeth said.

"You're the only one of the three who knows where anything in this town actually is. That makes you more valuable than luggage." Claire was already backing away, walking half-turned so she could keep talking. "Call me later. I'll give you the full report on the ring story. I mean it."

"I want the whole thing. Dates, tears, everything."

"There were tears," James called back.

"There were not," Claire said, and then they were far enough that the rest was just the two of them, laughing about something Elizabeth couldn't hear, heading off toward the dress shop side of Main Street.

Which left three.

Elizabeth fell into step between them without deciding to — Adrian on one side, saying nothing, same as the whole walk so far, and Tristan on the other, hands still in his pockets, eyes on the street the same as they'd been since the porch.

"Okay," she said. "Now that it's just us. What's the actual task here."

"Finding her."

"That's the goal." Elizabeth stopped walking for a second, just to make the point. "I asked about the task. What am I supposed to be doing, specifically, that's different from what I was doing an hour ago."

"Being available if you notice something."

"That's not a task, that's a description of every person in this town." She glanced sideways at him. "You're very good at answering a question without answering it."

"I answered it."

"You answered a version of it," she said.

Tristan's eyes cut toward Adrian for half a second, there and gone. Adrian didn't look back at him. Elizabeth caught the exchange without catching what it meant — just two people having an argument with no words in it.

"Fine," Elizabeth said, when the version-of-an-answer didn't improve. "How do you two know James and Claire?"

"We met them recently."

"How recently." She wasn't going to let that one go easily.

"Recently enough that the specifics aren't interesting."

"Everything about how two strangers end up best friends with a couple in one afternoon is interesting." She counted it off on her fingers, walking backward a step to face him before turning forward again. "Where you're from. What you actually do. Why 'available if you notice something' is a real job description anywhere. I have a lot of these."

"I've noticed."

Tristan didn't say anything, but his jaw had opinions about something, and Adrian was very carefully not looking at him.

"You went somewhere," Elizabeth said. "Just now. Somewhere that wasn't here."

"I'm still here."

"You know what I mean," Elizabeth said.

He didn't answer that either, which she decided to take as an answer.

By the time they reached the park, he'd fielded eleven more of her questions with varying degrees of success, and something in his jaw had gone tighter than it had been outside her house. She couldn't have said why. Whatever patience he'd started the walk with clearly had a bottom, and they were getting close to it.

The green opened up ahead of them, the same fountain still not running, a couple of kids kicking a ball near the benches.

"Why here," Elizabeth asked, dropping onto a bench, apparently deciding the questioning could happen just as well sitting down. "The park, I mean. Is this a task-related stop or are we just walking."

"We're looking for someone." He said it flat and factual, the same register as everything else today, no cushioning around it. "A young woman. No name. No face to go on. That's most of what I can tell you."

She sat with that for a second, turning it over. "That's not a lot to work with."

"No. It isn't."

"Noted." She didn't press it further. He didn't say anything for a moment — longer than the question itself had taken. She just filed it away, same as anything else that didn't come with an explanation attached, and looked out at the fountain instead. "My parents won the lottery when I was fourteen. Did you know that."

"No."

"Nobody outside town does. They didn't move, didn't buy anything stupid — just started disappearing for months at a time. Funded everything and then weren't there for any of it." There was a practiced ease to how she said it — the kind that comes from repeating something enough times to sand the edges off it. "Sam was seventeen. He basically raised the rest of it himself. I don't know why I'm telling you this."

"You don't have to."

"I know. I'm telling you anyway." She shrugged. That settled it, as far as she was concerned. "What happened to your forehead."

Elizabeth blinked. "What?"

"The bump. It's mostly gone now, but it's still there if you know to look."

Tristan, a step behind them, went very still — the same stillness she'd already clocked once today and still couldn't place.

"I fought a nightstand," Elizabeth said. "Lost. It's a whole story — alarm clock, bad landing, very undignified. Sam laughed at me over eggs the next morning." She touched the spot without thinking about it. "That's the first thing you've asked me that wasn't part of an investigation."

"Was it."

"You know it was," Elizabeth said.

He didn't argue the point. Tristan, beside him, didn't say anything either.

"Can I ask you something else," Elizabeth said, "and will you actually answer this one?"

"Ask it and find out."

"What are you? Actually." She leaned forward slightly. "Not the runaround version."

He looked at her for a moment before he answered — long enough that Tristan shifted his weight, the same small tell from the porch, still unexplained.

"We're angels," Adrian said.

She didn't laugh, and she didn't flinch. She gave him the same unhurried study she'd given the fountain — assessing, filing it away for later. "Okay."

"That's it? Okay?"

"You don't seem like someone who'd say it as a joke." She glanced at Tristan. "Is he an angel too."

"Yes."

"Do you have a task description, or is it also 'available if you notice something.'" She tilted her head at Tristan.

"I evaluate things," Tristan said.

"That's not an answer either. You two are clearly related."

Neither of them corrected her.

The kids by the benches lost their ball into the grass and went chasing after it, shrieking about nothing. Elizabeth watched them for a second, then looked back at Adrian — still not asking the next ten questions lined up behind her eyes, just sitting with the one answer she'd gotten. It went in with the rest of it: her parents' money, her brother's silences, everything else in her life that never came with an explanation attached.

Tristan didn't say anything else the rest of the afternoon. He'd gone quiet in a settled way, not a watchful one — a difference Elizabeth couldn't have explained, only felt.
