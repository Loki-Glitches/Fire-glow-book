# Chapter 8 — Lucifer

The walk back toward Main Street went slower than the walk out had — five people don't move at the same pace as four, and nobody had bothered to sort out whose pace that should be.

"So," Claire said, falling into step on Elizabeth's other side, "you're just going to invite yourself along."

"You invited me. I distinctly remember being invited."

"I said 'if you wanted.'" Claire held up a finger. "That's not the same as an invitation."

"It's exactly the same as an invitation."

James laughed at that from somewhere behind them, still catching up with the basket. "She's not wrong. You do this every time you don't actually want the answer to be no."

"I don't know what you're talking about." Claire looped her arm through Elizabeth's, wide-eyed and entirely unconvincing. "Anyway. Tell me you're at least a little excited about the wedding."

"I'm a lot excited about the wedding. I've known you were getting married for less than an hour and I'm already emotionally invested."

"That tracks." Claire smirked. "You cried at my dentist's retirement party."

"He'd been my dentist since I was seven. That was earned."

Lucifer kept himself slightly ahead of the other three, uninterested in whatever the wedding required of him. Azrael kept pace beside him, watching the storefronts go by with the same flat attention he'd given the porch. Lucifer's attention went to the same storefronts, though not for the same reason — faces behind glass, faces crossing the sidewalk, each one measured against a description that didn't amount to much and discarded just as fast.

"Where are we even going?" Elizabeth asked, over Claire's shoulder. "Is there a plan, or are we just orbiting the block until something happens?"

"There's a plan," James said. "The plan is walking around until something happens. It's a very sophisticated plan."

"You two are supposed to be finding someone. That seems like it should involve more than walking."

"It involves exactly this much walking," Lucifer said, without turning around. "For now."

"That was almost a full sentence. Feeling talkative?"

He didn't answer that, and Claire caught Elizabeth's eye and grinned, clearly having noticed the same thing.

They passed the bakery, still holding the last of its morning smell out over the sidewalk. Elizabeth didn't stop, though her step slowed half a beat longer there than it had anywhere else on the block. Claire didn't stop either, apparently on a schedule only she and the florist knew about, but she did steal a glance through the window on her way past.

"You've been quiet," Claire said to Elizabeth, low enough that it was mostly just for her. "You okay? You get that face when something's bugging you."

"I don't have a face."

"You have several faces." Claire tapped her own cheek. "That one specifically means you're turning something over."

"I'm fine. I'm just trying to figure out why two grown men need a woman who knows what everyone in this town looks like to find one specific stranger, instead of just asking around themselves."

"Maybe they tried that already." Claire didn't sound convinced.

"Maybe." Elizabeth glanced at him. He kept walking, giving nothing away on purpose. "Maybe I just like a mystery."

A woman sweeping the front step of the dry cleaner's looked up as they passed, her attention skipping past James and Claire — old news, both of them — and landing on Lucifer and Azrael instead, new enough to earn a second look in a town this size.

"Morning," she said, mostly to Elizabeth. "New friends?"

"Something like that."

The woman went back to her sweeping, apparently satisfied.

"Small town," Claire said, unnecessary, since Elizabeth had lived there her whole life and knew exactly how small it was.

"You'd think after twenty years I'd stop being surprised by it."

"You say that every single time." Claire bumped her with an elbow.

"It's true every single time."

James caught up enough to walk backward in front of them for a few steps, which he did badly and without any apparent self-preservation instinct. "You're not going to let up on this, are you."

"Not until someone gives me something real. Or the ring story. I'll take either."

"That's blackmail," James said.

"It's leverage. There's a difference."

"I don't think there is." James shook his head.

"There is if I say there is." Elizabeth glanced at Azrael, testing the same question on a new target. "You could just tell me. Save everyone the trouble."

"No," Azrael said.

*Don't.* Azrael's voice arrived flat, quieter than the word he'd just said out loud.

*I wasn't going to.*

There was no reply to that.

"Not even a little?"

"No," Azrael said again.

"You're worse than he is." She nodded at Lucifer, who didn't dispute it.

The afternoon heat had thickened again, heavy enough to sit in the back of the throat.

"You're sure you're okay with this," James said, quieter now, falling into step beside her while Claire got briefly distracted by a window display. "You don't actually know these two."

"I know them as well as I knew you two hours after you started dating Claire, and that turned out fine."

"That's different." James frowned.

"Is it? You vouched for them. That's usually enough for me." She bumped his shoulder with hers. "Also I'm curious. You know I can't help that part."

"I do know that part." He didn't look entirely reassured, but he let it go, dropping back half a step to let Claire catch up.

"How much longer are we walking before someone tells me the actual plan?" Elizabeth asked, aiming it at Lucifer this time.

"Not much longer."

"That's not a real answer," Elizabeth said.

"It's the true one, though." He didn't look back at her.

"That's not an answer either. That's the same non-answer in fewer words."

He let that stand.

They were nearly to Main Street before Claire checked her phone and made a face at it.

"The florist wants an answer on centerpieces today or we lose the date on the good ones." She looked at James. "That's us."

"That's us," James agreed, hefting the basket like it had been waiting for an excuse. "You've got backup." He nodded at Lucifer and Azrael. "Try not to lose her."

"I'm not luggage," Elizabeth said.

"You're the only one of the three who knows where anything in this town actually is. That makes you more valuable than luggage." Claire was already backing away, walking half-turned so she could keep talking. "Call me later. I'll give you the full report on the ring story. I mean it."

"I want the whole thing. Dates, tears, everything."

"There were tears," James called back.

"There were not." Claire nudged her fiancé, and then they were far enough that the rest was just the two of them, laughing about something already lost to the distance, heading off toward the dress shop side of Main Street.

Which left three.

Half of Lucifer's attention was still on the street — a woman with a stroller, two teenagers sharing a set of earbuds, a man locking up a shop two doors down. None of them were her. None of them had been her all morning.

Elizabeth fell into step between them without seeming to decide to. Azrael kept his hands in his pockets, eyes on the street the same as they'd been since the porch. Lucifer had said little the whole walk and didn't see a reason to start now.

"Okay," she said. "Now that it's just us. What's the actual task here?"

"Finding her."

"That's the goal." Elizabeth stopped walking for a second, just to make the point. "I asked about the task. What am I supposed to be doing, specifically, that's different from what I was doing an hour ago?"

Lying wasn't an option available to him; it never had been. Framing was the only tool left, and he'd been reaching for it since the porch.

"Being available if you notice something."

"That's not a task, that's a description of every person in this town." She glanced sideways at him. "You're very good at answering a question without answering it."

"I answered it."

"You answered a version of it," she said.

*You're letting this one get close.* Azrael's voice, flat, arriving under whatever she'd just said.

*I'm doing the job.*

*Convenient, that the job looks like this.*

Lucifer didn't answer that. He kept his eyes on Elizabeth instead, which took more effort than it should have.

"Fine," Elizabeth said, when the version-of-an-answer didn't improve. "How do you two know James and Claire?"

"We met them recently."

"How recently." She wasn't going to let that one go easily.

"Recently enough that the specifics aren't interesting."

"Everything about how two strangers end up best friends with a couple in one afternoon is interesting." She counted it off on her fingers, walking backward a step to face him before turning forward again. "Where you're from. What you actually do. Why 'available if you notice something' is a real job description anywhere. I have a lot of these."

"I've noticed."

Azrael's jaw had opinions about something. Lucifer didn't look at him.

"You went somewhere," Elizabeth said. "Just now. Somewhere that wasn't here."

"I'm still here."

"You know what I mean," Elizabeth said.

He didn't answer that either. She let it go, the same as she had the last one.

By the time they reached the park, he'd fielded eleven more of her questions, with varying success. His jaw had gone tighter than it had outside her house. Whatever patience he'd started the walk with clearly had a bottom, and they were getting close to it.

The green opened up ahead of them, the same fountain still not running, a couple of kids kicking a ball near the benches.

"Why here?" Elizabeth asked, dropping onto a bench, apparently deciding the questioning could happen just as well sitting down. "The park, I mean. Is this a task-related stop or are we just walking?"

Lucifer sat at the other end of it.

"We're looking for someone." He said it flat and factual, the same register as everything else today, no cushioning around it. "A young woman. No name. No face to go on. That's most of what I can tell you."

She sat with that for a second, turning it over. "That's not a lot to work with."

"No. It isn't."

"Noted." She didn't press it further. Lucifer let the silence sit rather than fill it — there was nothing to add to a fact that flat. When she spoke again, it wasn't another question. She was looking out at the fountain. "My parents won the lottery when I was fourteen. Did you know that?"

"No."

"Nobody outside town does. They didn't move, didn't buy anything stupid — just started disappearing for months at a time. Funded everything and then weren't there for any of it." Lucifer noted the practiced ease in how she said it, the kind that only comes from repeating something enough times to sand the edges off it. "Sam was seventeen. He basically raised the rest of it himself. I don't know why I'm telling you this."

"You don't have to."

"I know. I'm telling you anyway." She shrugged.

Azrael's attention, which had spent most of the walk on the street behind them, moved onto Lucifer instead. It didn't move again for a while.

Lucifer looked at her a moment longer than the shrug needed. "What happened to your forehead."

Elizabeth blinked. "What?"

"The bump. It's mostly gone now, but it's still there if you know to look."

Azrael, a step behind them, had gone still again — a different stillness than the one from the porch, just as unreadable.

"I fought a nightstand," Elizabeth said. "Lost. It's a whole story — alarm clock, bad landing, very undignified. Sam laughed at me over eggs the next morning." She touched the spot without thinking about it. "That's the first thing you've asked me that wasn't part of an investigation."

"Was it."

"You know it was," Elizabeth said.

He didn't argue the point. Azrael, beside him, didn't say anything either.

"Can I ask you something else?" Elizabeth said, "and will you actually answer this one?"

"Ask it and find out."

"Who are you, really?" She said it more like a statement than a question, watching to see if he'd correct her. "The truth no workarounds."

Answering it himself meant answering everything underneath it too. There was no version of the truth that stopped where it needed to, not from him — not today, maybe not ever.

Elizabeth's eyes moved from one to the other, watching to see which of them would speak first. Neither of them did — not with anything she could hear.

*Say it.* Lucifer's voice, quieter than usual even for something only Azrael could hear.

*That's not mine to give.*

*It has to be. Whatever she asks after this, you can bend it. I can't.*

Azrael held the stillness another second — the same weight from the porch — and let it go.

"We're angels," Azrael said.

Nothing came out of her for several seconds. She didn't laugh. She didn't flinch. Her hands, which had been working at something on the hem of her sleeve a moment before, had gone still in her lap. Her eyes went to Lucifer — not the fountain, not the kids shrieking somewhere behind her, not even the one who'd actually spoken. When she finally said something, it came out slower than anything she'd said all afternoon, and quieter.

"Okay."

"That's it? Okay?" It was out of Lucifer before he'd decided to say it.

"What was I supposed to do instead?" She spread her hands. "You don't seem like someone who'd say it as a joke. You've been too careful with everything else you've told me today to suddenly get sloppy about that one." She sat back against the bench. "So it's true. Okay."

"You believe it because I've been consistent."

"I believe it because you clearly think it's true, and you don't strike me as someone who believes things by accident." She glanced at Azrael.

"Do you have a task description, or is it also 'available if you notice something'?" She tilted her head at Azrael.

"I evaluate things," Azrael said.

"That's not an answer either. You two are clearly related."

Neither of them corrected her.

The kids by the benches lost their ball into the grass and went chasing after it, shrieking about nothing. Elizabeth watched them for a second, then looked back at Lucifer. Whatever else she still wanted to ask, she left it where it was. She hadn't pushed on the vague description at the fountain either, or on her brother's silences a few minutes before. If she noticed she was collecting a set, she didn't say so.

Azrael didn't say anything else the rest of the afternoon. Whatever had settled into him after the forehead question stayed settled, quieter than his usual watching. Lucifer couldn't have said what shifted. Only that something had.
