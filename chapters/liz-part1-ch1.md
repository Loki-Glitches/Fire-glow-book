The coffee maker is doing its slow thing again, the loud gurgle before it actually starts brewing. I give it a minute, then wipe down the counter because it doesn't need it, but standing there doing nothing feels worse.

Two bowls in the sink from last night. Neither of us washed them. I do them now, cold water, no gloves, and call it waking up.

"Please tell me that's for me," Sam says from the doorway, hair a mess, reaching past me for a mug before the coffee's even done.

"Whoever gets there first."

"That's rigged. You're standing right here."

"I got here first."

He bumps the counter with his hip on the way to the fridge — about the only soft thing he does before nine in the morning — and asks if I slept okay. I say fine, because I did, mostly, and fine covers it close enough. He doesn't push on it. He asks if I want eggs, and I check the fridge, and we don't have any.

I grab the pad stuck to the fridge with a magnet shaped like a lemon and write: eggs, my refill, battery for the clock radio. Sam reads it over my shoulder and says we need bread too, so I add that.

"You register for anything yet?" I ask, not looking up from the list.

"Register for what."

"Fall classes. School starts back up in a few weeks."

"I know when school starts."

"That's not an answer."

"It's the answer you're getting." He pours his coffee into the mug he just took. "You want to talk about your own stuff first? Or should I just wait for that, too?"

"Fine. Drop it."

"You dropped it, I just agreed."

I let it go, because that's usually how it goes — I push a little, he pushes back enough to end it, and neither of us actually wins.

"I'll come with you," he says, already reaching for his keys.

"I can carry a bag of eggs by myself, Sam."

"Didn't say you couldn't."

"You grabbed your keys."

"Reflex." He sets them back down. "Go. Don't forget the eggs this time."

"When have I ever forgotten the eggs."

"More than once."

Outside it's already hot, thick August heat sitting on the whole town before nine. I cut across the square instead of around it, past the fountain where the Henderson twins are running through it with a hose nobody's stopped them on yet, past the bench where Mrs. Alvarez is reading the same paperback she's been reading all summer. I lift a hand at her. She lifts one back without looking up.

The pharmacy is freezing compared to outside, and smells like band-aids. The woman at the counter — I don't know her name, just her face, glasses always pushed up into her hair instead of on her nose — rings up my refill and the battery I grabbed off a rack on the way in, and tells me to have a good one. I tell her the same.

Grocery store is two doors down. I get the eggs, a loaf of the bread Sam likes, and almost walk out without the eggs again because I set them down to dig a coupon out of my pocket and then forget where I put them. I find them two aisles over, still in the carton, and feel dumb about it the whole way to the register.

The walk home is the same walk it always is. Someone's running a sprinkler two houses down from ours, and the kids from down the block are daring each other to run through it in their clothes. I think about what we actually have in the house for dinner, which is probably nothing, and decide that's Sam's problem to solve since I'm the one who got the eggs.

Sam's at the kitchen table when I get in, laptop open to something he closes before I can see what it is.

"Get the eggs?"

"Got the eggs." I set the bag on the counter and start putting things away. "And bread. And my refill. And a battery, which, by the way, the clock radio's been broken for a while, nobody's noticed but me."

"I noticed. I just didn't care."

"That's worse."

"Is it, though."

I hand him the battery and he fixes the clock radio right there at the counter — takes him about ten seconds — and the two of us stand there and watch it blink back to the right time. It's nothing, but it's done.

I pour myself what's left of the coffee, cold by now, and drink it anyway. In the other room, the TV comes on, some rerun with a laugh track every few minutes. Nothing else happens the rest of the morning. I'm fine with that.
