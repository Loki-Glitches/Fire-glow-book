# Chapter 5 — Elizabeth

The pharmacy smelled like it always did — cough drops and the particular plastic of a blood pressure cuff nobody used. Elizabeth waited at the counter while the pharmacist found her bag, half-listening to two women arguing pleasantly about a casserole recipe two aisles over.

"Here you go, hon." The pharmacist slid the bag across the counter. "Allergy pills, and I threw in the good sunscreen since you're headed out in this heat. Tell your brother I said hello for me."

"Will do." Elizabeth glanced into the bag — the white pill bottle, a travel-size tube wedged beside it — and folded the top over.

Outside, the heat had only gotten worse. She checked the time and started toward the hardware store, the prescription bag swinging against her leg.

"You didn't tell me you were coming into town today." Sophie fell into step beside her out of nowhere — sunglasses shoved up onto her head, a loose tank top and cutoffs, a coffee balanced in each hand. "I brought you one. You're welcome."

"You're a witch."

"I prefer psychic. Witch implies I have to work for it."

Elizabeth took the coffee and looped her free arm through Sophie's, the way she'd been doing since they were twelve and Sophie had decided, without asking permission, that they were going to be best friends whether Elizabeth agreed to it or not. "I need a bulb for the porch light. It's been dead a week."

"A week? Sam's been letting that go a week?"

"Sam's been letting a lot of things go a week." Elizabeth retorted.

Sophie didn't ask what that meant.

The hardware store was empty except for the two of them and a teenager restocking shelves who didn't look up. Elizabeth found the right bulb on the second aisle and held it while Sophie wandered the next one over, pulling things off pegs and putting them back without buying any of them.

She lifted a curved brass hook first, turning it over in her hand. "This is a good hook, though. A person could always use a good hook."

"For what." Elizabeth joked.

"I don't have anything specific in mind. That's what makes it a good hook — infinite potential." She hung it back on its peg and came back a moment later with a folded tarp under one arm and a package of drawer magnets balanced on top of it.

"Put those down."

"Already down." Sophie dropped the tarp back into its bin and slotted the magnets in beside it. "See how easy that was. I have excellent restraint."

When they went back outside, James and Claire were crossing at the far corner, and Elizabeth lifted a hand before she'd finished registering it was them. Claire, deep in conversation with two men Elizabeth had never seen before, kept on walking. New faces didn't happen often enough not to notice.

"Who's that with them?" Sophie shielded her eyes against the glare.

"No idea."

"Huh." Sophie watched a beat longer than Elizabeth did, then let it go. "Bakery? You know you have to. It's basically a law."

"That's exactly what Sam said about eating cereal standing up this morning."

"Sam's rules are good rules. I stand by all of them, especially the ones I didn't know about until just now."

They crossed at the corner, Sophie's arm still looped through hers, and the bakery door stuck as always until Sophie shouldered it open. Inside was as warm as July could be, thick with butter and yeast, worth it anyway for Sophie's treat — a cinnamon twist bigger than her hand — which she ordered without looking at a menu that hadn't changed in three years. Elizabeth got a plain roll, more out of obligation than hunger, and a chocolate croissant for Sam. They took the one table left with any shade, and Sophie dragged the extra chair out of the sun before she sat down.

"So this woman comes in," Sophie said, once they'd sat down, "and spends eleven minutes — I timed it, I had nothing else to do — telling me this shade of yellow is, quote, 'an insult to the concept of yellow.'"

"What did you say?"

"Nothing. I let her finish. That's customer service." Sophie stirred her coffee, which didn't need stirring. "Then she bought two gallons of it."

"Of the insult?" Elizabeth shouted and covered her mouth immediately.

"Of the insult. Full price. Didn't even ask about the employee discount, which she also doesn't get, but still." Sophie laughed.

"You good?" Sophie asked it lightly.

"I'm good."

"Okay." Sophie didn't press. She never did. "Tell Sam he owes me a rematch."

"At what?" Elizabeth giggled.

"He'll know."

Elizabeth walked back to the car with the bulb, the chicken, and a paper bag of something Sophie had insisted she take home for Sam.
