# Chapter 5 — Elizabeth

The pharmacy smelled like it always did — cough drops and the particular plastic of a blood pressure cuff nobody used. Elizabeth waited at the counter while the pharmacist found her bag, half-listening to two women arguing pleasantly about a casserole recipe two aisles over.

"Here you go, hon." The pharmacist slid the bag across the counter. "Tell Sam I said hello."

"Will do."

Outside, the heat had only gotten worse. She checked the time and started toward the hardware store, the prescription bag swinging against her leg.

"You didn't tell me you were coming into town today." Sophie fell into step beside her out of nowhere, sunglasses pushed up into her hair, a coffee in each hand. "I brought you one. You're welcome."

"You're a witch."

"I prefer psychic. Witch implies I have to work for it."

Elizabeth took the coffee and looped her free arm through Sophie's, the way she'd been doing since they were twelve and Sophie had decided, without asking permission, that they were going to be best friends whether Elizabeth agreed to it or not. "I need a bulb for the porch light. It's been dead a week."

"A week? Sam's been letting that go a week?"

"Sam's been letting a lot of things go a week."

Sophie didn't ask what that meant.

The hardware store was empty except for the two of them and a teenager restocking shelves who didn't look up. Elizabeth found the right bulb on the second aisle. Sophie found approximately eleven things she didn't need and put every one of them back, narrating her reasoning for each.

"This is a good hook, though. A person could always use a good hook."

"For what."

"I don't have anything specific in mind. That's what makes it a good hook — infinite potential."

"Put it down."

"Already down." Sophie set it back on the shelf with exaggerated care. "See how easy that was. I have excellent restraint."

Outside again, James and Claire were crossing at the far corner, and Elizabeth lifted a hand before she'd finished registering it was them. Claire waved back without quite looking over, deep in conversation with two men Elizabeth had never seen before. New faces didn't happen often enough not to notice.

"Who's that with them?" Sophie shielded her eyes against the glare.

"No idea."

"Huh." Sophie watched a beat longer than Elizabeth did, then let it go. "Bakery? You know you have to. It's basically a law."

"That's exactly what Sam said about eating cereal standing up this morning."

"Sam's rules are good rules. I stand by all of them, especially the ones I didn't know about until just now."

The bakery was warm in the way only an oven-adjacent room in July could be, and worth it anyway for the thing Sophie always got, which she ordered without looking at a menu that hadn't changed in three years. They took the one table left with any shade.

"So this woman comes in," Sophie said, once they'd sat down, "and spends eleven minutes — I timed it, I had nothing else to do — telling me this shade of yellow is, quote, 'an insult to the concept of yellow.'"

"What did you say?"

"Nothing. I let her finish. That's customer service." Sophie stirred her coffee, which didn't need stirring. "Then she bought two gallons of it."

"Of the insult."

"Of the insult. Full price. Didn't even ask about the employee discount, which she also doesn't get, but still."

"You good?" Sophie asked it lightly.

"I'm good."

"Okay." Sophie didn't press. She never did. "Tell Sam he owes me a rematch."

"At what?"

"He'll know."

Elizabeth walked back to the car with the bulb, the chicken, and a paper bag of something Sophie had insisted she take home for Sam, already thinking about dinner and not much else.
