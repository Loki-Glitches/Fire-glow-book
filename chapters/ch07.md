# Chapter 7 — Elizabeth

The knock came while she was still holding the porch bulb, halfway between the kitchen and actually doing something about it.

She set it on the hall table instead of answering with it in her hand, and pulled the door open already smiling — Claire's knock had a rhythm to it, three quick and one late, and Elizabeth had known it since they were sixteen.

"You're just in time to help me feel bad about a light bulb I've been ignoring for a week," Elizabeth said, and then her eyes caught on Claire's hand before anything else registered. "Wait. Wait. Is that — Claire."

"I was getting to it."

"You were not getting to it, you let me talk about a light bulb first." Elizabeth grabbed her hand outright, turning it toward the porch light for a better look, which told her nothing since the porch light was dead. "When. Who. James, obviously, but when."

"This morning. Sort of a whole thing." Claire glanced past her at the other three, aware of the audience now. "I'll tell you everything. Actually tell you, not the four-word version."

"You'd better." Elizabeth let go of her hand, still grinning, and swatted James's arm on principle. "You couldn't have told me first? I've known you both for years."

"She wanted to tell you herself," James said. "I was under strict instructions."

"Good instructions."

Only then did she actually look at who else Claire had brought with her.

James, she knew. The other two she didn't — one of them so still it was the first thing she noticed about him, before anything he said, nothing restless in his hands or his face; the other a half-step back, hands in his pockets, watching the street more than the porch.

"This is Adrian," Claire said, nodding at the still one, "and Tristan. Don't ask what they're doing in town, it's a long story and I'm still not sure I believe all of it."

"Very well," Adrian said, "I'll ask myself. May we come in."

Nobody said that. Elizabeth filed it away — not a lie, just a shape that didn't quite match the mold. "You can stay on the porch and ask, that works too."

"Fair," James said, and dropped the picnic basket by his feet.

"We're looking for someone," Claire said. "A young woman, new to town, maybe within the last while. No name. No description, really, beyond that." She'd clearly tried to make it sound less strange already, and given up on it. "I told them if anyone would've clocked a new face, it's you. You run more errands in a week than the rest of us combined."

"That's flattering and slightly insulting."

"It's accurate," Adrian said.

"See, everyone in this town says that like it's a whole personality trait." Elizabeth leaned against the doorframe, arms crossed loosely, more curious than careful. "Why are you looking for her?"

"It's easier to explain once we find her."

"That's not an answer."

"No," Adrian agreed. "It isn't."

He didn't look away when he said it, and she didn't either — a beat too long for a stranger on a porch, long enough that Tristan shifted his weight beside him, some small adjustment Elizabeth couldn't have named a reason for. She noticed it anyway. She noticed most things she couldn't name a reason for; she just didn't usually say so out loud.

"You're not going to tell me anything, are you."

"Not today."

"Okay." She meant it. Sam's silences had taught her that a long time ago — pressing never got her anywhere. "So what do you actually need from me. Since apparently I'm the town's foremost expert on faces."

"Have you seen her." Adrian's attention came back to her fully when he asked it — not friendly, not unfriendly, just entirely present, more than most people managed when they asked her things. "Or heard of her. Anything."

"No. Not that I know of." She thought about it properly instead of just answering — the pharmacy, the hardware store, the bakery, the two men across the street she hadn't recognized either, though she didn't think to mention it, filed under new faces, not worth much, along with everything else that morning. "I can ask around, though. People tell me things. I don't know why. I think I just look like I'll actually listen."

"You do," James said.

"That wasn't a compliment fishing expedition, but thank you."

Claire laughed, and even Tristan's mouth twitched, almost a smile.

James hefted the basket with a small groan. "Can we at least put this somewhere first? I've been carrying it since breakfast."

"Nobody told you to bring it."

"It had the thermos in it. That thing's older than both of us combined. Forgive me for being careful."

"We were going to walk the rest of town," Claire said. "See if anything turns up. You could come, if you wanted. You know it better than any of us."

Elizabeth glanced back at the hall table — the bulb still sitting there, the porch light still dead, the whole ordinary afternoon she'd had planned around exactly nothing. Sam wouldn't be back for hours. The house would still be here.

"Give me one second," she said, and went to find her shoes instead of the ladder. She grabbed the first pair by the door and skipped the socks, already thinking about which direction through town would actually be useful to walk and which was just walking.

When she came back out, Adrian was frowning at the dead porch fixture.

"It's on the list," she said, stepping past him toward the walk.

"I didn't say anything."

"You didn't have to."

She pulled the door shut behind her, tested the handle out of habit, and stepped down onto the walk to join them — four of them, and now her, heading back toward the street she'd already crossed twice that day without anything in it worth remembering. The heat hadn't let up at all. Behind her, the porch light stayed exactly as dead as she'd left it.
