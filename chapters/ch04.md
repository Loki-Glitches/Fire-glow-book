# Chapter 4 — Lucifer

The portal didn't set him down. It threw him.

He hit an apple tree shoulder-first, hard enough to shake fruit loose around him, and landed in the grass at its roots without anything dignified about it.

He got up, brushed a crushed apple off his sleeve, and looked past the branches at the sky beyond them. He pointed one finger straight up. "That was unnecessary."

There was no reply.

"Graceful." Azrael stood under the next tree over, arms crossed, having apparently watched the whole thing. "You're late."

"I didn't choose the timing, or the landing." Lucifer brushed grass from his sleeve, unnecessary, done anyway. "Neither did you, I take it."

"No." Azrael's eyes went once over him and came back without comment. "You look the part."

He looked down at himself — plain clothes, human-cut, human-colored, nothing about them would make a passerby look twice.

Azrael hadn't followed the finger. "Ready."

"For?"

"The part where I do my actual job and you don't get either of us noticed doing it." Azrael's arms stayed crossed. "Rules, in order. You'll want them before anyone's close enough to hear."

"Go on then."

"There are three tiers you can reach with a human here, and none of them happen by accident. The first is a Travel Bond. It costs you nothing and gives you nothing — no strength, no reach, nothing you'd notice missing if it ended tomorrow. It forms on its own if someone genuinely gets to know you. You don't control when. It just exists, once it does."

"And it requires."

"Nothing from you but being someone worth knowing." Something flickered at the corner of Azrael's mouth. "Manage that much."

Lucifer did not dignify it.

"The second tier requires you to tell them what you are and why you're here. Both. Not one dressed as the other. Once you do, if they're still standing there, you have strength, and you can pull small things across — nothing past ash-scale, nothing that draws attention doing it. The third tier is not a conversation for an orchard."

"Understood."

"Say it back to me. I don't trust that word from you."

Lucifer said it back to him, word for word.

"And the names." He said it while Azrael was still watching him.

"Adrian." Azrael was already sitting back against the tree. "Tristan. The moment there's a person in earshot, those are the only two words either of us answers to."

"You've reminded me zero times, if we're counting."

"I'm counting now."

---

A woman's laugh reached them before the two people it belonged to did — a couple coming up the slope with a picnic basket swinging between them, the man doing most of the talking with his free hand.

"Oh — sorry, didn't know anyone else knew about this spot." The woman lifted the basket slightly. "James's grandfather used to bring him here."

"We'll move." Azrael was already rising.

"Don't be ridiculous, there's plenty of orchard." James set the basket down between the trees. "I'm James. This is Claire."

"Tristan." Azrael said it without a beat of hesitation.

"Adrian."

The basket emptied slowly, the way afternoons do when no one's in a hurry. Claire talked with her whole body, hands narrating half of every sentence. James laughed easily and often, mostly at things that weren't especially funny.

Azrael allowed himself exactly enough participation to seem present. Lucifer said less and noticed more — the ring on James's hand, plain metal, a stone missing from its setting long enough that the empty channel had gone dull. He noted it and did nothing about it.

James stood up too fast, brushed grass off his knees that wasn't there, and got down on one knee in front of Claire before either stranger on the blanket could pretend not to be watching.

"I had a whole thing planned," he said. "Skipping most of it. Marry me?"

She said yes before he finished the question.

Azrael clapped, twice. Lucifer said nothing.

By the time the ring's missing stone came up again — Claire teasing James for never getting it fixed — the two strangers on the blanket had stopped being strangers to either of them.

"So." James refilled Claire's water glass before his own. "What are you two actually doing out here? Nobody hikes this far in for the view."

Lucifer considered several answers and discarded them; lying was not available to him.

"I'm looking for someone," he said. "I can't explain that without explaining what I am. Both, or neither."

Claire looked at James. James looked at the ring he'd just put back on her finger. Neither of them looked especially alarmed.

"What are you," James said, "specifically."

He told them. Plainly, the way he'd have reported a number to Sebastian.

Claire's water glass stopped halfway to her mouth. James set his down.

"You're—" He didn't finish it.

"Yes."

James's alarm didn't last long. He looked at Claire; his shoulders came down.

"Okay," he said. "Why here. Why us."

"You weren't the plan. You were convenient, and then you weren't just that."

He held out his hand for the ring. James hesitated, then let him take it.

It took less effort than closing a hand. The dull channel where the stone had been filled itself in, gold instead of the same worn metal, and the empty setting was simply not empty anymore. He handed it back.

"For the wedding," he said, since an explanation seemed to be expected and that one was true.

Claire made a sound that wasn't quite a word. James stared at his own hand for a second like it belonged to someone else.

"I need to find someone in this town," Lucifer said, before either of them recovered enough to ask a question he didn't want to answer twice. "A young woman. I don't have a name for her, or a face. Only that she's here."

"That's..." James rubbed the back of his neck. "That's not a lot to go on."

"If anyone would have noticed a new face around," Claire said, "it's our friend Elizabeth. She's out running errands most mornings this time of year — practically knows everyone in town by sight."

"Elizabeth." He said the name once, procedural, no different from any other.

"Elizabeth." Azrael repeated it, flat.

James picked up the picnic basket, considerably lighter now. "We'll walk you as far as the road. Easier to point from there."

They started down through the orchard together, four of them where there had been two, the trees thinning toward a fence, a road, and a town on the other side of it.
