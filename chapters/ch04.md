# Chapter 4 — Lucifer

The portal didn't set him down. It threw him.

He hit an apple tree shoulder-first, hard enough to shake fruit loose around him, and landed in the grass at its roots without anything dignified about it.

He got up, brushed a crushed apple off his sleeve, and looked past the branches at the sky beyond them. He pointed one finger straight up. "That was unnecessary."

There was no reply.

"Graceful." Azrael stood under the next tree over, arms crossed, having apparently watched the whole thing. "You're late."

"I didn't choose the timing." Lucifer brushed grass from his sleeve without looking at him.

"No." Azrael's eyes went once over him and came back without comment. "You look the part."

He looked down at himself — plain clothes, human-cut, human-colored, nothing about them would make a passerby look twice. He didn't ask who had decided that. He didn't care enough to.

Azrael hadn't followed the finger. "Ready?"

"For?"

"The part where I do my actual job and you don't get either of us noticed doing it." Azrael's arms stayed crossed. "You have no power here. None. Not until certain conditions are met."

"What conditions." Lucifer's eyes were already on the orchard past him, not on his face.

"I'm not telling you."

"Azrael."

"The moment I tell you, you'll find a path around it, and I'm not spending this visit undoing your loopholes." Something went tight at the corner of Azrael's jaw, there and gone, unremarked on. "You'll meet them when you meet them."

Lucifer didn't push it further. It cost too much effort for an answer he'd already decided not to need.

"And if I ask a human instead?"

"You'll get a human's answer, which is to say none." Azrael pushed off the tree. "You'll feel it when it changes. That's all you get."

Lucifer said nothing to that either.

"And the names?" He said it flatly, already turning to survey the tree line instead of Azrael.

"Adrian." Azrael's jaw was tight again, and stayed unmentioned. "Tristan. The moment there's a person in earshot, those are the only two words either of us answers to."

"Fine."

---

A woman's laugh reached them before the two people it belonged to did — a couple coming up the slope with a picnic basket swinging between them.

"Oh — sorry, didn't realize anyone else knew about this spot." She slowed, glancing at the man beside her.

"We'll move." Azrael was already rising.

"Don't be ridiculous, there's plenty of orchard." He set the basket down between the trees. "I'm James. This is Claire."

"His grandfather used to bring him here," Claire said. "He likes to call it sentimental. It's mostly that it's free."

"Tristan." Azrael said it without a beat of hesitation.

"Adrian." Lucifer copied and gave Azrael a glare.

The basket came apart across the blanket — bread, a jar of something dark, fruit that hadn't come from these trees. Claire talked with her whole body, hands narrating half of every sentence. James laughed easily and often, mostly at things that weren't especially funny, and made a point of including the two strangers in it, asking Azrael what he did and not accepting "a bit of everything" as an answer until he'd gotten a better one.

Azrael allowed himself exactly enough participation to seem present. Lucifer said less and noticed more — the ring on James's hand, plain metal, a stone missing from its setting long enough that the empty channel had gone dull. He noted it and did nothing about it.

An hour of that, maybe more, before James stood up too fast, brushed grass off his knees that wasn't there, and got down on one knee in front of Claire before either stranger on the blanket could pretend not to be watching.

"I had a whole thing planned," he said. "I'm skipping most of it. Claire, will you marry—"

She said yes before he finished the question.

Azrael clapped, twice. Lucifer said nothing.

By the time the ring's missing stone came up again — Claire teasing James for never getting it fixed — the two strangers on the blanket had stopped being strangers to either of them.

"So." James refilled Claire's water glass before his own. "What are you two actually doing out here? Nobody hikes this far in for the view."

Lucifer considered several answers and discarded them; lying was not available to him.

*Don't.* Azrael's voice arrived flat, no louder than a thought. *You don't get to take it back once it's said. Not with these two. Not with anyone after them.*

*They already suspect something.*

*Suspecting and knowing aren't the same thing.*

*Guessing wastes time I don't have.*

There was no reply.

"I'm looking for someone," he said. "I can't explain that without explaining what I am. Both, or neither."

Claire looked at James. James looked at the ring he'd just put back on her finger. Neither of them looked especially alarmed.

"What are you," James said, "specifically?"

"We're angels," he told them, plainly.

Nobody said anything for a moment. Claire's water glass stopped halfway to her mouth and stayed there. James let out a short laugh that didn't commit to being one.

"Right." He looked at Claire, then back. "Good one."

"I don't joke."

"You don't—" James got up, sat back down, got up again, apparently unable to settle on which his body wanted to do. "People don't just say that. You can't just say that and expect—"

"Expect what."

"I don't know. Something. A reaction that isn't this."

"James." Claire's voice came out steadier than her hands looked. "Look at him. Really look."

James looked. Whatever he found there put him back in his seat, mostly. His knee hadn't stopped moving.

Claire set her water glass down before she dropped it. "Okay. Okay, so — angels. Actual — with the—" She gestured vaguely above her own head, gave up on the sentence, and started a different one. "Why do you look like that. Shouldn't you look like something."

"This is what looks like nothing to notice."

"That's not an answer, that's a—" She pressed both hands flat against her knees to hold the rest of her still. "I need a minute too, actually. Ignore me."

"Prove it," James said, quieter now, apparently deciding one of them had to ask for something useful.

He held out his hand for the ring instead of answering. James hesitated, then let him take it.

It took less effort than closing a hand. The dull channel where the stone had been was filled in, the band gold instead of the same worn metal. He handed it back.

"For the wedding," he said, since an explanation seemed to be expected and that one was true.

Claire made a sound that wasn't quite a word. James turned the ring over twice before he found anything to say.

"Okay." He said it like setting down something heavy. "Okay. Why here. Why us."

"You weren't the plan. You were convenient, and then you weren't just that."

"Let's get this over with then. I need to find someone in this town," Lucifer said, before either of them recovered enough to ask a question he didn't want to answer twice. "A young woman. I don't have a name for her, or a face. Only that she's here."

"That's..." James rubbed the back of his neck. "That's not a lot to go on."

"If anyone would have noticed a new face around," Claire said, "it's our friend Elizabeth. She's out running errands most mornings this time of year — practically knows everyone in town by sight."

"Elizabeth." Lucifer said the name once, procedural, no different from any other.

"Elizabeth." Azrael repeated it, flat.

James picked up the picnic basket, considerably lighter now. "We'll walk you just outside of town. Easier to point from there."

They started down through the orchard together, four of them where there had been two, the trees thinning toward a fence, a road, and a town on the other side of it.
