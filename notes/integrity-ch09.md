The filler scene's Lucifer/Azrael voices are mostly on-target — Lucifer stays flat and diagnostic ("I noticed." "Which part." One soft spot: Azrael's lines lean more verbally droll than the style guide's specified mechanism for his pre-Calm annoyance. Not a violation, but a texture worth watching in future filler scenes so it doesn't drift toward Azrael becoming the "funny one" by default.

COMEDY MECHANICS:
This is not a comedy-genre project, but the filler scene is explicitly comedic and the task calls for an assessment of it, so this is scored rather than marked N/A.

What works: the comedy is genuinely escalating and specific rather than generically loud — mailbox, then fire hydrant, then across the street, then behind a parked car, each attempt adding a new specific detail rather than just repeating the same beat louder. The dog ("a dog two yards over quit barking at him and lay back down between rounds") is a clean, situational, one-off image that earns its laugh without commentary. Azrael's clipped, procedural exasperation ("I don't have a tape measure. I have you falling on me every time I ask you to take one more step") is character-based — it comes specifically from his evaluator/measurement instinct being undermined by an uncooperative variable, not generic straight-man sarcasm.

Two soft notes: First, the balance the task asked about — does "no banter between Lucifer and Azrael" actually hold — mostly does. Every exchange that could read as back-and-forth wit is one-sided: Azrael delivers the dry line, Lucifer answers flatly or disengages, never returns fire. That is the correct shape per the locked rule. Second, this scene deploys the sky-glance ("He glared up at the sky before he'd even gotten his other hand under him. Once. He said nothing about it") on an occasion that isn't clearly one of God's engineered ironies — it's a newly-discovered bond glitch, not an obviously "too convenient" coincidence in the way the orchard landing or Elizabeth's resemblance are. The style guide is explicit that the gesture "should always land as a specific, earned reaction to a specific too-convenient thing, never as a tic." Using it here for generic physical pain rather than a legible God-joke slightly dilutes the device's precision, especially now that its per-book cap has been lifted and its scarcity is the only thing still protecting it from becoming a tic.
