# Yarros-Influenced Style Experiment — Reference Notes (2026-10-21)

**Status: experimental, not canon.** This is a style the author is testing via a full rewrite of "Twenty Feet," refined over several passes in direct conversation with the Director (not routed through Writer or Creative Partner). It is NOT the current house style — `bible/style-guide.md` and `.claude/agents/soul.md` remain the locked, authoritative rules for the actual manuscript unless and until the author says otherwise. Nothing from this file overrides those without an explicit author ruling.

This file exists so the refined rules aren't lost between sessions, in case the author wants to develop this further or apply it somewhere later.

## What changed vs. the locked house style, and why, refined across multiple passes

**1. Figurative language (similes/metaphors/hedges) — permitted, unlike the main style guide's zero-tolerance ban. But two failure modes surfaced and got corrected in turn:**

- *Content repetition.* A first loosened pass over-used figurative language to the point of roughly one instance every 2-3 lines — too dense, cutting flow. Corrected by spacing comparisons out to roughly one every few paragraphs, used for specific textural or comic weight, not as a constant tic.
- *Architectural repetition.* Even once content/imagery was varied (different source domains each time — weather, a muscle, lightning, a splinter — not the same handful of feeling-words reused), the connective phrase itself repeated: "[X], the way [Y]" showed up 9+ times in one pass. A reader's ear catches the joint before the image. **Fix: vary the sentence architecture delivering the comparison** — some flat statement with no connector at all, some straight juxtaposition, occasional true "like" simile, metaphor without any connector, appositive. Never lean on one connector phrase as the default move.
- General diagnostic for telling a real simile from a hedge in disguise: a true simile imports a concrete, often odd image from an unrelated domain (nearly unlimited supply, so repetition is a choice, not a trap). A hedge dressed as a simile just restates a character's emotional/mental state in deniable form ("like it was nothing," "like she was done with it") — drawing from a narrow vocabulary of feeling-words, which is why it degrades into repetition fast even with good intentions.

**2. No gratuitous continuity callbacks.** Don't explain a present action by explicitly cross-referencing an earlier chapter's events or mechanics ("the same trick he used on the ballroom the first day," "the same thing he'd done to the tree in the orchard," naming a past power and explaining how this is/isn't that power). Just show the present action plainly. The reader's memory is better than a draft tends to assume, and a book worth finishing gets reread anyway. This cut both unneeded exposition and a chunk of the repetition problem at once, since callbacks were a major source of the "the way" construction.

**3. Restored/increased descriptive richness — this is NOT a minimalist style.** The world is meant to read as beautiful, almost otherworldly. Concrete sensory/world detail (room textures, materials, specific damage, specific finished spaces) should be fuller and more vivid than the locked house style's leaner default, not thinner. The early loosened-style drafts cut descriptive content for pace/brevity and that was a mistake — restraint on figurative-language *frequency* should not come at the cost of descriptive *substance*.

**4. Dialogue is looser — real banter/quips permitted,** including one-liners that exist partly for their own comic rhythm rather than purely to convey plot information (a step past the locked style's purely functional dialogue rule). Still has to stay inside each character's established voice — this loosens register, not characterization.

**5. A visible narratorial personality, used sparingly.** Free-indirect narration is allowed to carry wit/warmth slightly beyond the strict POV character's own immediate articulated thought — a departure from the locked style's fully disciplined close-third with zero authorial commentary. **Author's specific color for this narratorial warmth: it reads as God's own fond, mischievous watching-over-His-family affection — not solemn, not stern, delighted by His own jokes at Lucifer's and Azrael's expense.** This should surface rarely (once or twice in a full chapter), not as a running commentary track.

**6. Overwritten posture/action with an explanatory tail — permitted here** (banned outright in the locked style), used occasionally for comic or character-revealing color, not as a constant crutch.

**7. Still third person, close to Lucifer** — true first-person-present-tense Yarros narration was explicitly ruled out early in this experiment; POV structure itself did not change, only the prose's permissiveness within that structure.

**8. One unresolved tension, flagged not decided:** this experiment used italics for conversational emphasis mid-dialogue. The locked house style reserves italics exclusively for non-audible/telepathic/divine speech formatting. If this style is ever used for real manuscript pages, that conflict needs an explicit author ruling before being treated as settled either way.

## Process note
Developed over several iterative passes, each correcting a specific craft problem the author caught in the previous one (over-dense figurative language → architectural repetition of "the way" → unnecessary continuity callbacks). Each fix was narrow and specific, not a wholesale rewrite — worth preserving that incremental-correction approach if this gets picked up again later.
