- **Category:** Banned Construct — Overwritten Action
- **Location:** `Sam turned the burner down and looked over — the same unhurried pass he'd given Lucifer on the porch two nights before, longer now, because there was more worth checking with him not already halfway out the door.`
- **Rule violated:** § "No Overwritten Posture, Expression, or Action" — this is a near-exact structural match to the style guide's own "Wrong" example ("...because reading them was the function and the function did not care whether it was useful"). The "because" clause explains *why* the look lasted longer instead of trusting the longer look itself, already contrasted against the porch look two nights earlier, to communicate that.
- **Suggested fix direction:** Cut the "because" clause; the comparison to the earlier, shorter look already does the work of showing Sam's growing scrutiny.

- **Category:** Banned Construct — Overwritten Action
- **Location:** `She looked at him properly then, something working behind it that didn't resolve into a question. He hadn't meant to say it. It served no version of the assignment, and he had nothing to gain from having said it. He'd said it anyway.`
- **Rule violated:** § "No Overwritten Posture, Expression, or Action" — this is the chapter's most load-bearing beat (per this chapter's own unit-plan craft note: "this one costs him something small"), and it's the one place the prose explains that cost directly ("served no version of the assignment," "nothing to gain") instead of letting the bare action carry it. It mirrors the exact banned pattern in the style guide's own corrected example almost sentence-for-sentence.
- **Suggested fix direction:** Cut the explanatory sentence and let "He hadn't meant to say it. He'd said it anyway." stand alone — her reaction and the silence that follows already signal the cost without narration spelling out why it cost him anything.
