## After "Hell" (2026-09-11)

**Plot Points:**
- Sebastian gives Lucifer his routine sector report in the Hell throne room; Lucifer is visibly bored/lazily powerful (deflects a falling fruit from the Tree of Knowledge without looking), Sebastian is visibly on edge before any bad news arrives.
- A runner reports Eve's containment cell wall has been failing for an hour, undetected. Lucifer slows time across the whole realm and drops to sector one.
- A portal has opened in Eve's cell wall. Lucifer reaches her right as she's being pulled through — she reaches back toward him, not away — but the portal closes on empty air before he can catch her hand; the momentum throws him into the cell's inner wall.
- Evidence at the wall (worn claw marks, layered over each other) shows Eve had been clawing at it for millennia before it finally gave.
- Lucifer puts Lilith in charge of Hell with full authority in his absence, orders the cell resealed with brimstone, and tells Sebastian to reinforce every cell in sector one before the next watch change.
- Back in the throne room, Lucifer asks God for permission to go to Earth after Eve. Granted, on conditions: no revealing his divinity, every commandment still applies, and Azrael comes with him (not to help catch Eve — to make sure Lucifer doesn't "make a mess again"). Lucifer argues the terms and loses, as he apparently always does.
- The portal to Earth forms and pulls Lucifer through mid-protest — not a clean, composed crossing; he's dragged in with no grip, apple still in his mouth/fist. Chapter ends as the portal seals behind him.
- **Naming note (resolved 2026-09-11):** author confirmed **Lilith**, one L. Bible file renamed to `bible/lilith_character_profile.md` and all "Lillith" spellings corrected across the bible.

### Character Development

- **Lucifer:** First appearance. Baseline established as bored/imperious/lazily powerful in his own domain — casual, economical with effort (redirects a falling apple without looking up), quick to cold command the instant real danger lands. Eve's escape hit him hard enough to slam him bodily into a wall; he doesn't show that rattled state to Sebastian or Lilith, keeps it clipped and procedural in front of them, but the Eve moment (her eyes, her hand reaching for him, missing her by inches) is clearly a live wound, not a settled one — he doesn't examine it on the page, just moves past it into action. Chafes hard against restriction: argued at length with God over the no-divinity/Tristan-minder terms and lost, same as (per God's own line) he apparently always does. Leaving for Earth chapter's end carrying real anger at the leash he's been put on, alongside genuine urgency to recapture Eve. Holding a fruit from the Tree of Knowledge as he crosses over — didn't eat it, just carried it through.
- **Sebastian:** First appearance. Established as Lucifer's report-and-operations aide, normally composed/professional. Written visibly on edge for this whole chapter *before* the bad news arrives (throat-clearing, flinching, a lie about having "checked twice" that he hadn't) — so when the runner confirms Eve's cell is failing, it reads as a dread he already had, not a surprise. End of chapter: given a direct, urgent task (reinforce every cell in sector one by next watch change) and an implicit rebuke hanging over him about how the failing wall went undetected for an hour on his watch.
- **Lilith:** First appearance. Composed, unshaken, immediately pivots to operational questions ("how long was the wall compromised") rather than reacting emotionally. Left in charge of Hell with full authority in Lucifer's absence — a position of real trust, not just a formality.
- **Eve:** Not yet a POV character and stays off-page after this chapter per the project's own Act Three placement, but her single visible moment here — reaching *toward* Lucifer as she's pulled through the portal, not away from him — is written ambiguously on purpose (could be a plea, could be something colder) and is the one interpretive note the next writer to touch her should be careful not to flatten in either direction without being told to.

## After "Breakfast" (2026-09-11)

**Plot Points:**
- Elizabeth comes downstairs in a pale yellow floral sundress (small white flowers, thin straps) and joins Sam for breakfast — eggs and sausage.
- Sam notices a fresh cut above her left eyebrow (small, already scabbing). She deflects with a joke — "lost a fight with the bedside table this morning" — and downplays it as mostly just sore now, barely remembers it happening. Played as a light, believable bit, not written as suspicious in the moment. **Flag for continuity:** the "Morning" baseline entry above describes this same general head injury as an "alarm/fall incident" — "bedside table, this morning" fits that framing well (consistent with a stumble at the alarm) but isn't stated as confirmed fact on the page, since it's still Elizabeth's own explanation to Sam, not authorial narration. Future chapters should treat the exact cause as author's call to confirm or complicate, not settled by this line alone.
- They talk through Elizabeth's college prep: housing form done, waiting on roommate assignment (due end of month), registered for the earliest orientation session — **in four weeks** (author's edit; final revision, not the Writer draft's "three weeks" — use four going forward). She presents as fine/unbothered to Sam; narration confirms this isn't performance in this moment — she isn't privately panicking, she's genuinely keeping her own weight off him rather than hiding acute distress.
- A new postcard appears on the fridge, postmarked/pictured London (red phone booth image), added to a small existing collection already on the fridge under one magnet. **Confirmed on the page (author's edit, 2026-09-12):** message reads "Greetings from London kids!" — addressed to both Elizabeth and Sam ("kids"), and Elizabeth recognizes the handwriting as her mother's specifically. This isn't new lore — `bible/world-bible.md` already established the fridge postcard as coming from their traveling parents (previously from "some island," per the pre-rework record in "Dinner"); "Breakfast" is the next instance of that same recurring, minimal-effort correspondence, this time postmarked London, now with the actual card text on the page. Elizabeth's reaction reads as quietly wanting more than the card gives ("half hoping there was more to it than that") — ties directly to the locked family wound (`bible/elizabeth_character_profile.md` § "Family & Core Wound": parents chronically absent, "money stood in for presence").
- Elizabeth finishes eating before Sam, offers to hit the store: he asks for milk, eggs, non-seedy bread, and (separately) a chocolate croissant from the cafe, correctly assuming she's going there with "Soph" (Sophie) today — a running/established pattern between them. Elizabeth confirms she is in fact going to the cafe with Sophie today. She leaves, closing the door behind her.

### Character Development

- **Elizabeth:** Consistent with baseline — outwardly cheerful and easy with Sam, deflects the head injury with humor rather than concern, and keeps her college-prep anxiety (if any) off his plate entirely; when pressed directly ("you'd tell me if you were actually freaking out"), her reassurance reads as genuine in the moment, not a mask cracking. No indication yet that she's hiding anything larger than an ordinary teenager's mild pre-college nerves.
- **Sam:** First real on-page scene as caretaker. Confirmed warm/teasing per baseline — cooks breakfast, ribs her lightly and constantly, but also checks in directly and specifically (asks if she's nervous, asks if she'd tell him if something were wrong) rather than only joking past things. Observant of small physical details (spots the cut immediately). Predictable/reliable in routine — knows her cafe habit with Sophie well enough to shorthand it and to already call her "Soph."
