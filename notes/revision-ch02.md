## WHAT THE CHAPTER DOES NOW

1. **Ledger scene (unchanged in substance).** Sebastian's report, the broken forgiveness system, the dust flag. Lucifer's short contextual interior lines remain.
2. **The descent.** Lucifer already knows, before he moves, that the breach is Eve — a cold certainty, not a deduction. He tears straight down through the mountain's rock rather than taking the stairs.
3. **The breach, shown live.** He arrives as the centuries-worn door finally gives out and turns to ash. A portal tears open where it stood; Eve is already inside it. He lunges and misses her by the width of a held breath as it seals.
4. **The aftermath, compressed and urgent.** He shouts Lillith's name; she arrives instantly. Her regency orders are given rapid-fire, on the spot, in the wrecked corridor — full regency, the no-possession/immediate-report rule, no leave for anyone, all hands on the floor, permanent contact. He explicitly forbids her from analyzing the door or the breach — there is nothing left to examine and no time for it.
5. **The ascent.** Not the stairs — straight back up the shaft he tore.
6. **The petition to God, reframed.** He doesn't request permission to pursue; he demands to be sent immediately. God reveals Azrael already has his instructions — assigned before Lucifer even asked, reinforcing (for the reader, not for Lucifer) that God is a step ahead of him this whole chapter. Lucifer's friction with Azrael surfaces here for the first time (irritation at having to route through an intermediary), which is consistent with their established mutual resentment, not a new beat.
7. **The aliases.** Still delivered by God directly, in the same exchange.
8. **The ending.** God states plainly that Lucifer cannot send himself to Earth — only God can. A portal opens, matching the one that took Eve, and pulls Lucifer through against his own volition. It closes. Chapter ends there.

## DELIBERATE DEVIATIONS FROM THE ORIGINAL UNIT PLAN (author-directed, now the new canon)

`bible/unit-plans.md` Ch.2 has been rewritten to match all of these — this list is for the record of what changed and why:

1. **Emotional register.** The original Must-NOT was "any emotional reaction from Lucifer to Eve's name or escape — purely procedural at this stage." The author explicitly asked for urgency, a premonition that it's her, and a failed physical lunge to catch her. This is now allowed, with one boundary preserved: the reaction is written as a warden's failure and instinct, never as longing or personal feeling for her specifically. The only named emotional cost in the whole sequence is dread at having to report the breach to God — deliberately not about her.
2. **The breach mechanism.** Originally discovered after the fact (Lillith already on scene, door already lying in the corridor, forensic examination). Now witnessed live: door turns to ash, portal opens, Eve is pulled through, Lucifer just misses her. The "worn thin over centuries" backstory from earlier drafts still holds as the reason the door finally failed *now* — it's simply never re-examined on the page, since Lillith is expressly told not to analyze it.
3. **Lillith's role in this scene.** No forensic dialogue. She arrives, receives orders, confirms understanding. Her regency terms (the four rules) are preserved but compressed into the shouted orders rather than a separate calm negotiation scene later in the ninth corridor — that whole separate scene is cut.
4. **The petition to God.** Originally a formal request for leave that God grants with two conditions. Now Lucifer demands to go himself; God tells him Azrael already has instructions (assigned pre-emptively) and refuses to let Lucifer transport himself — that authority is God's alone. This is new, locked worldbuilding: **Lucifer cannot send himself to Earth.**
5. **The ending.** Originally a quiet solo departure with a bond-sensed farewell beat from Azrael. Now Lucifer is forcibly pulled through a portal by God, mirroring the one that took Eve. This also changes Ch.4's framing slightly — `bible/unit-plans.md` Ch.4 has been updated to note his landing is not a landing of his own choosing, though the actual physical outcome (arriving alone in the orchard, Azrael already waiting) is unchanged.
