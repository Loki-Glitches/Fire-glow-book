VOICE FIDELITY:
Lucifer's POV holds the Act One/early Act Two "clinical, diagnostic" register precisely — style-guide language like "notices things the way a system notes input" is realized almost literally: "Lucifer caught the shift in Sam's face — eyes narrowing a beat after the question left him. He eased back from the edge he hadn't meant to walk to." No warmth is asserted anywhere; what reads as care is entirely behavioral (finishing the bread "even strokes... nothing wasted" without being told where the tray was).

Sam is dry and watchful without tipping into hostility — "You again." / "Apparently." / "That checks out." — consistent with his profile (protective, catches the edge of things, doesn't chase). Elizabeth, seen only through Lucifer's eyes here, still reads as herself through action: deflecting Sam's question fast ("It's a family thing," Elizabeth said quickly, reaching for the bread) and downplaying her own state ("I'm good, Sam") — both are established Elizabeth behaviors (protecting Sam, minimizing to him), rendered correctly even without direct access to her interiority.

EMOTIONAL REGISTER:
The chapter's one real emotional escalation — Lucifer naming that Elizabeth minimizes things to Sam — arrives with exemplary restraint. It's delivered through interiority describing the *absence* of a reason to say it, not through any stated feeling: "It served no version of the assignment, and he had nothing to gain from having said it. He'd said it anyway." This is precisely how the style guide's Emotional Ladder wants a pre-Calm "bump" to surface — costly, small, unnamed, and immediately followed by Elizabeth's plain, ungilded response ("It's easier this way... He worries enough already") rather than a speech about her feelings. The critical test passes: the moment arrives while the scene is nominally just two people finishing dinner and going out to the porch, not while the prose is visibly building toward a turning point.

STRUCTURAL INTEGRITY:
Checked line-by-line against bible/unit-plans.md Ch.10. All Must Include items are MADE: Sam meets Lucifer skeptically (the "what do you actually do all day" exchange, cut off by Elizabeth's cover-protection); the meal shows Lucifer more interested in the family dynamic than in Sam ("he's more interested in the family dynamic and the absent parents than in Sam himself" — realized via the postcard beat and the "performance of fine" observation); Sam's skepticism registers and Lucifer backs off and restores cover; the porch scene surfaces, but doesn't force, the hiding-things-from-Sam observation. All Must NOT items are honored: no divinity hint from Elizabeth, no confrontation (tension stays at "temperature," per the craft notes' own instruction), no hard press on the porch, no emotion unlock.

One genuine concern: the chapter opens with a compressed time-summary — "Four evenings running now, he'd shown up when the light started going long — once for a return at the pharmacy, twice for nothing she could name beyond wanting the company, tonight for no stated reason at all." This is material invented beyond anything in the unit plan, and it functions as a brief montage: three unseen evenings are summarized in two sentences rather than dramatized, which sits uneasily against the style guide's explicit Act Two pacing rule — "The relationship moves in increments. A single unguarded question is a scene. A second look is a beat. Nothing should feel like a montage." It's short enough not to derail the chapter, and it does useful work establishing the Ch.9-mechanic's nightly recurrence (Azrael walking him back into range), but it is the one place in these two chapters where summary stands in for a scene the style guide would generally prefer to see played out, or at minimum referenced more glancingly.

THEMATIC FIDELITY:
This chapter is a strong, clean expression of the project's soul. Soul.md's explicit callout — "Sam cooks. It is a detail that carries weight — it is how he shows care" — is honored precisely: Sam serving without measuring, plate for Elizabeth first, bread pushed within everyone's reach before he sits. The postcard on the fridge ("corners soft from being taken down and put back more times than a postcard should survive... Neither of them mentioned it. Neither of them needed to.") is exactly the "one true detail beats ten beautiful sentences" instinct the soul document asks for — the absent parents are present in the scene without a word of exposition about them.

The chapter's best thematic stroke is dramatic irony that the craft notes call out directly and the prose executes without ever naming it: Lucifer recognizes that Elizabeth hides things from Sam while doing exactly the same thing to her about himself. Nothing in the text points this out — the reader has to notice it, which is the entire "invisible before undeniable" philosophy of this project's approach to character change.

WHAT'S WORKING:
1. "He weighed the sentence for anything that required arguing and found nothing. He went in." — clinical, precise, funny in the driest possible way, and entirely true to a being who is honest to a fault and has no small talk to spend.
2. The postcard detail is a model of the show-don't-tell rule from CLAUDE.md and the soul document's "specificity over grandeur" value — it does the work of an entire paragraph of backstory in one sentence.
3. The porch beat's restraint: "That's not the same as it being fine." / "No." She let the splinter go. "It's not." Two short lines carry the chapter's real emotional weight without either character explaining what just happened.
