FINDINGS
────────────────────────────────

- **Category:** Attribution
- **Location:** A recurring pattern of standalone dialogue tags with no attached physical action beat, across both sections of the chapter:
  - `"That wasn't the arrangement," Adrian said.` (Tristan/Adrian exchange, early in the chapter)
  - `"Built everything," she repeated. "Like — everything."`
  - `"Light," he said, without hesitating over the word the way she'd expected him to.`
  - `"Adam came after all that," Adrian said. "Long after..."`
  - `"She came later," he said, and stopped there.`
  - `"If you did something—" he started.`
  - `"Go again," Azrael told him. "Further back this time."`
  - `"You should've told me it did this," Lucifer said.`
- **Rule violated:** § Dialogue Rules — Attribution Hard Rule: "Do not use standalone 'he said / she said' tags unless absolutely necessary for clarity after a long passage. If attribution is needed, attach it to a physical action beat." None of these sit after a long passage or in a spot where the two-person alternation isn't already doing the identification work — several surrounding lines in these same exchanges (e.g., "Tristan's voice had gone flat..." / "Azrael paced off the distance...") already model the correct action-attached pattern this chapter should use consistently.
- **Suggested fix direction:** Attach each to a beat already available in the scene (Adrian's stance, Tristan's retreat, Azrael's pacing/posture while measuring) or cut the tag outright where voice/rhythm alone already makes the speaker clear, matching the technique the chapter already uses correctly elsewhere.

- **Category:** Banned Construct — Overwritten Action
- **Location:** `"I have somewhere to be." Adrian said it the same flat way he'd said it to Tristan an hour earlier.`
- **Rule violated:** § "No Overwritten Posture, Expression, or Action" — the clause explains the callback's significance (that this line deliberately echoes the earlier one) instead of letting the exact repetition — already self-evident four paragraphs after the first instance — speak for itself.
- **Suggested fix direction:** State the line and let the echo land on its own; a plain action beat on Sam's reaction can carry the moment without narrating the parallel.

- **Category:** Banned Construct — Punctuation (locked rule)
- **Location:** `"Is he actually going somewhere, or is that also a version of an answer."`
- **Rule violated:** § "Comma vs. Question Mark Before a Tag" (locked 2026-09-04) — "The clause is a complete question, and nothing more follows in that character's speech: always a question mark." This is a full, original interrogative sentence, not a short deadpan echo of something already said — it doesn't qualify for the separate Flat-Delivery Exception the way "What." or "Which part." do elsewhere in this same chapter, both of which are short clipped echoes and correctly kept as periods.
- **Suggested fix direction:** Close with a question mark: "...or is that also a version of an answer?"

- **Category:** Banned Construct — Overwritten Action (minor)
- **Location:** `"Reassigned." A short breath went through him that might have been the start of a laugh, if he'd let it get that far.`
- **Rule violated:** § "No Overwritten Posture, Expression, or Action" — the appended conditional clause ("if he'd let it get that far") qualifies/explains the beat's meaning rather than stating the plain physical fact and moving on.
- **Suggested fix direction:** State the beat plainly (e.g., "A short breath went through him — not quite a laugh.") and drop the extension.

No Scene Completeness or Word-Count Padding findings — the travel-bond range test in the filler scene repeats the fall-and-measure beat many times, but this reads as the comedic mechanic-demonstration the author specifically requested, not padding; every iteration adds a new piece of information (direction doesn't matter, distance is constant, a dog and a porch light react) rather than repeating the same beat. Props referenced in dialogue (the bulb, the door, the walk home) are all rendered on the page, not left as placeholders.
