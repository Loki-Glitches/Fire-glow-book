## 3. Locked Canon (2026-09-11 rework + since)

Full detail lives in the companion bible docs listed in § 1; this is the load-bearing summary.

- **Lucifer has power from the start of the book.** Not bond-gated. Constrained instead by standing orders: never self-disclose his own divinity (exception: if a human independently figures it out, that's allowed to stand), never reveal his mission, never use power where a human can witness it (passes it off as a magic trick if he must). — *`bible/lucifer_character_profile.md` § "Power & Secrecy"*
- **His baseline personality is volatile** — snide, sarcastic, angry, lashes out at small things — softening in small, informal, unnamed beats over the book, with real acceleration once he falls for Elizabeth.
- **He is the original angel** — God gave him his form; every other angel is patterned from his image. Not God's offspring — God's most devoted worshipper. His own gift was smaller-scale creation (art, music).
- **God is NOT Lucifer's father.** Their bond reads closer to siblings.
- **He paints privately from early in the book; sings later, only after she knows, only because she pushes him into it. Neither beat has happened on the page yet** as of "Errands."
- **Elizabeth genuinely hates him at first.** Constant arguing/friction is their default mode; he can't kill her, so there's heat but no real danger.
- **Azrael's usual death-duty is temporarily reassigned** to a different, unnamed angel (still not named — § 7). His job is to contain Lucifer. Present in every Lucifer scene until Elizabeth knows the truth. He and Lucifer can physically hurt each other badly but neither can die; both heal within 24 hours, consistent with "No Place Like Home"'s single-shove injury (Lucifer's back) and "Errands" tracking that injury's lingering limp.
- **Bond system: Travel Bond is the only tier that survives** (~20ft proximity mechanic, not a power gate). Power Bond and Full Anchor are cut entirely.
- **Names:** Lucifer → Adrian; Azrael → Tristan. Narration uses true names regardless of scene; only spoken self-introduction or human address uses the alias. Azrael privately calls Lucifer "Lucy"; God calls him "Luc."
- **Eve is the antagonist, absent from the page until Act Three.**

### New this pass (all 2026-10-02, `bible/lucifer_character_profile.md` §§ FORM & SOUL / POWER SCOPE, and `bible/gabriel_character_profile.md`)

- **FORM & SOUL.** Lucifer's **soul is uncreated** — it existed before God found him, is entirely his own, and is **permanently unalterable: beyond God's power to change or take, unconditionally.** No other angel shares this (their souls were made along with their forms). His **form**, by contrast, **was a gift from God** — a vessel to use, not a claim of authorship or ownership — and **because it was given, it can be taken back.** The form is also a deliberate limiter on his divine power (see POWER SCOPE). **Because his soul was never God's work, Lucifer has the greatest free will of any angel** — this is also the stated reason he, unlike other angels, is permitted to act physically on Earth regardless of his assigned task (ties directly to Gabriel, below).
- **Purity, restated under the new cosmology.** In his given form Lucifer **cannot sin, physically and literally** — not a temptation resisted, an actual impossibility. **Never write him straining against it, tempted by it, or choosing not to sin — there is no internal struggle to dramatize.** The underlying cosmology (sin becoming possible only in his full, outside-body/outside-time state, which he never enters) is explicitly **narratively inert, reference-only, not a plot lever** — flag to the author before any chapter goes near it.
- **POWER SCOPE.** Lucifer's full power is **"almost on the same level as God"** — he can do anything except create from nothing. **That full power exists only outside his body, outside time and space** — the state he was in before God found him. **Inside his given form (which is where he always is on the page), God has built in deliberate limits.** He's still enormously powerful within those limits (slowing time across Hell, the illusion, the orchard repair, the ring trick) but far below his full scope — **this is explicitly why Azrael can genuinely hurt him in a fight and why he can be caught off guard.** Power is still not knowledge: this change does not make him all-knowing (see the pre-existing "He Is Not All-Knowing" rule, unchanged and still in force).
- **General human-desire knowledge, not individual mind-reading (locked, ties to the above).** As ruler of Hell, Lucifer has institutional knowledge of worldly human desire in general — what souls wanted, that money drives behavior — which is why money, bribery, and devices like phones hold no mystery for him. **This never extends to reading what a specific individual wants or is thinking.** (An earlier draft of "Errands" had him reference a private detail about a specific clerk's daughter; this was cut during drafting specifically to keep this limit intact — see § 5.)
- **"Deals aren't sins."** Bribes and similar transactions are deals, not sins, and do not conflict with his purity. First demonstrated on-page in "Errands" (the $5,000 bribe to Kevin Marsh).
- **Gabriel (new character, `bible/gabriel_character_profile.md`).** The biblical archangel, one of Lucifer's angelic siblings, God's messenger. **He is the angel who typically handles physical arrangements in the human world on God's behalf** (records, identities, accounts, documents) — other angels' work on Earth is normally immaterial. Lucifer and Azrael are exceptions because of their special task; Lucifer is independently exempt regardless of task because of his free will (see FORM & SOUL, above). **First on-page effect, not yet a physical appearance:** in "Errands," Lucifer tries to open a bank account as "Adrian Black" and discovers one already exists, fully set up, empty, with no creation date — he immediately knows it was Gabriel. **Still open** (flagged in the bible file itself): whether Gabriel is the sibling currently covering Azrael's reassigned death-duty; whether Azrael knew about the account in advance; how the account could already carry "Black," a surname Lucifer chose that same day.
- **Corrected stale lines, same pass:** "God made him" → "God gave him his form as a gift" (his soul pre-existed; ORIGIN and LUCIFER & GOD sections both updated, old wording kept struck-through for record); "God's son in this era" → "God's closest companion in this era"; "present but powerless" → "present, with all of his power and nothing yet to use it on."
- **Two new named minor characters, both one-off, "Errands":** **Kevin Marsh** (phone-shop clerk, bribed to skip an ID check) and **Denise Harlow** (bank clerk at **First Link Banking** — bank's name corrected from an earlier draft's "First Century Bank"; synopsis updated to match).

## 4. Standing Character-Voice Rules (apply to every chapter, not just the one that introduced them)

These live in `notes/character-synopsis.md` under "Standing habits" specifically so Writer — who never reads the bible — still sees them. Treat this section as load-bearing for any new chapter brief.

1. **Elizabeth/Lucifer friction is mandatory, not chemistry.** His caustic baseline is aimed at her as much as anyone; friction/open arguing is their default mode from the very first exchange; she stays in his orbit because she reads him as broken, not because she likes him yet. No warmth/attraction registering in his interiority toward her; no comfortable silences between them specifically.
2. **Not every character reads people.** Only **Elizabeth** (knows when someone is being untruthful — see the correction immediately below) and **Lucifer/Azrael reading each other** (both directions, six thousand years of proximity) get any analytical read on others. Nobody else — Sophie, James, Claire, Sam, any extra — catches subtext or reads a tell.
   - **Correction, locked 2026-10-02 (`notes/creative-partner-session.md`, "Author round 3"): Elizabeth has no lie-detection ability at all.** The author explicitly objected to framing her questioning as a power. Reframed: she asks pointed questions and watches for inconsistencies — **an ordinary skeptical-questioning habit, a skill, not a perception power.** This wording fix was sent to six locations; **five are done** (`bible/story-bible.md`, `bible/style-guide.md`'s substantive framing, `notes/character-synopsis.md`, this file, and the chapter-level usage). **One is still outstanding: `.claude/agents/agent-generic-writer.md` line ~20 still describes it as "knows when someone is being untruthful," unchanged — this needs the same correction before Writer next drafts a scene involving her questioning anyone.** Director/author action item, not resolved by this synthesis pass.
3. **Lucifer eats apples constantly, as a habit** — ordinary apples, distinct from the (closed) Tree of Knowledge fruit thread. Bored, frustrated, or simply in the mood are all equally valid reasons. He cannot set one down once picked up — must eat or destroy it. **"Errands" is a deliberate, author-specified exception with no apple at all** — not a change to the standing habit, just this one chapter.
4. **No similes, no metaphors in narration** — zero exceptions in narration; dialogue is exempt. A deliberate floor, not permanent; no retroactive sweep planned until the end of the manuscript.
5. **No explicit physical description** of hair color or eye color, for any character, ever.
6. **Lucifer is not all-knowing.** Unaffected by the new POWER SCOPE cosmology (§ 3) — raw power is explicitly not knowledge. He does not get to state something as fact unless actually in a position to know it.
7. **Divine/telepathic speech formatting:** italics, no quotation marks, for God's voice and any telepathic bond traffic.
8. **The Sky-Glance:** Lucifer's silent upward glance at God's too-convenient timing — genuine annoyance, not shared amusement, Lucifer's POV only.
9. **Lucifer cannot sin or lie (restated 2026-10-02 under FORM & SOUL).** Not a temptation he resists — physically impossible inside his given form. Never dramatize him straining against it.
10. **Lucifer is always operating inside deliberate limits on his power while in his body (2026-10-02).** He's powerful but beatable in a fight — this is now the explicit, bible-locked reason Azrael can genuinely hurt him, not just an unexplained fact of their dynamic.
11. **Gabriel handles God's physical arrangements on Earth (2026-10-02)** — the explanatory mechanism any future "someone already took care of X before Lucifer could" beat should use.
12. **Lucifer knows general human desire (money, wants, how phones/institutions work) from ruling Hell — never a read on a specific individual (2026-10-02).** Keep any future scene using this grounded in institutional knowledge, not personal insight into one person's circumstances.
13. **Lucifer does not carry or own a phone (established "Errands").** Azrael carries the one phone the brothers currently have, registered under "Adrian Black" — see § 5 for the open question about why.

## 5. What Went Wrong Once and Got Corrected (worth knowing so it isn't repeated)

- **"Introductions Are In Order" had to be redrafted** because its first version broke the friction rule (§4.1) — warm chemistry-coded prose where cold/adversarial friction was required. The corrected version on disk is the worked example for the Lucifer/Elizabeth dynamic going forward.
- **"Errands" draft reassigned the Gabriel-realization to the wrong brother.** `notes/revision-errands.md` (CRITICAL) caught that an earlier draft had **Azrael** be the one to silently work out and name "Gabriel," with no stated way for him to possess that information (the POV is Lucifer's) — every authoritative record (`bible/gabriel_character_profile.md`, `bible/story-bible.md`, the planned synopsis entry) has **Lucifer** realize and curse Gabriel's name under his breath, with Azrael only picking up on it silently and saying nothing until they're outside. **This is corrected in the locked chapter and in `notes/character-synopsis.md`'s "After Errands" entry** — Lucifer whispers "Gabriel," Denise doesn't catch it, and only once outside does Azrael say "Oh, Gabriel," which Lucifer cuts off with "Don't."
- **"Errands" draft also had a two-phone arithmetic error** — Kevin's closing line ("Both of them are already activated") implied two phones/lines were set up, contradicting the scene's own explicit single-phone purchase ("New. Just for him though," nodding at Azrael; "$1,200... for the phone itself," singular). **Corrected to a single phone, Azrael-only**, matching the final synopsis entry. An earlier planning draft of the synopsis itself had assumed two separate lines (one per alias) and has since been updated to match.
- **"Errands" draft briefly had Lucifer leverage a private detail about Kevin's daughter to pressure the bribe.** Cut during drafting specifically because it would have violated the "general human desire only, never individual-specific" limit (§ 3) — the final version grounds the same beat in general knowledge of what money does to people instead. Worth remembering as a concrete example of the line between the two.
- **Still open, not fully resolved:** Revision flagged that the single phone ends up registered under **"Adrian Black"** (Lucifer's own alias) even though Azrael is the one who carries and uses it — Revision's suggested fix was to register it under "Tristan" instead, to match the actual user, unless there's an in-story reason for Lucifer's name to be on it. The locked synopsis entry keeps "Adrian Black" and frames it as "put down out of convenience," but **no line in the chapter itself explains why** — flagged here in case a future chapter needs to account for Lucifer's name being the one tied to the household's only phone number.
- **"No Place Like Home" has one unresolved CRITICAL finding still present in the chapter file as of this synthesis pass** (verified directly against `chapters/no-place-like-home.md`, lines ~187–189): Azrael says **"Still the same old Lucifer"** — his brother's actual true name, not the "Lucy" nickname — while the two are only "a short distance" from Emma Brock inside the same room, with no beat establishing she's out of earshot (the very next line has her "look up as they came back"). Every other instance of this exact secrecy risk in the manuscript (the "Luc… Adrian" self-catch in "Why Did It Have to Be Apples") is staged with an explicit acknowledgment and correction; this one has neither. **This has not been fixed or addressed anywhere in the downstream notes** — it needs author attention before this thread (the secrecy-around-Emma thread specifically) can be considered closed. See § 7.
- **"No Place Like Home" also had an unaddressed scope question on the brand-new illusion power** (Revision, VERIFY): the synopsis describes the illusion as cast "over the ballroom specifically," but the chapter's actual prose shows the projected vision extending to the staircase, front door, and "whole structure" — broader than the synopsis framing suggests. Not necessarily wrong (an interior vision including sightlines elsewhere is plausible) but flagged as worth a conscious author confirmation since this chapter is the template-setter for how the power works on the page going forward.
