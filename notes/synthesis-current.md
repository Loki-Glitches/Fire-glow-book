## 3. Locked Canon (2026-09-11 rework + since)

Full detail lives in the companion bible docs listed in § 1; this is the load-bearing summary.

- **Lucifer has power from the start of the book.** Not bond-gated. Constrained instead by standing orders: never self-disclose his own divinity (exception: if a human independently figures it out, that's allowed to stand), never reveal his mission, never use power where a human can witness it (passes it off as a magic trick if he must). — *`bible/lucifer_character_profile.md` § "Power & Secrecy"*
- **His baseline personality is volatile** — snide, sarcastic, angry, lashes out at small things — softening in small, informal, unnamed beats over the book, with real acceleration once he falls for Elizabeth.
- **He is the original angel** — God gave him his form; every other angel is patterned from his image. Not God's offspring — God's most devoted worshipper. His own gift was smaller-scale creation (art, music).
- **God is NOT Lucifer's father.** Their bond reads closer to siblings.
- **He paints privately from early in the book; sings later, only after she knows, only because she pushes him into it. Neither beat has happened on the page yet** as of "Twenty Feet."
- **Elizabeth genuinely hates him at first — a stated, crystallized fact since "Ambush"** ("I really don't like him," unchallenged by Sophie), reconfirmed rather than softened in "Debrief." Constant arguing/friction remains their default mode; he can't kill her, so there's heat but no real danger.
- **Azrael's usual death-duty is temporarily reassigned** to a different, unnamed angel (still not named — § 7). His job is to contain Lucifer. Present in every Lucifer scene until Elizabeth knows the truth. He and Lucifer can physically hurt each other badly but neither can die; both heal within 24 hours — "Twenty Feet" adds a new, real instance (Azrael's full-strength punch over the fruit reveal), on top of the standing back-injury thread from "No Place Like Home."
- **Bond system: Travel Bond is the only tier that survives** (proximity/tether mechanic, not a power gate). Power Bond and Full Anchor are cut entirely. **As of "Twenty Feet," the range is confirmed, on the page, as an exact 20 feet in every direction** (not approximate) — tested deliberately by Azrael, same number regardless of direction. The pull-to-anchor event is a clean, instant teleport (no visible slide/drag), and it genuinely, if mildly, hurts Lucifer each time — one of the few things that can. (`bible/bond_system_reference.md` § Travel Bond Range, revised 2026-10-21; first on-page demonstration in "Twenty Feet.")

Narration uses true names regardless of scene; only spoken self-introduction or human address uses the alias. **Azrael's private nickname for Lucifer, "Lucy," had its first on-page use in "Twenty Feet"** — private, affectionate-but-needling register, no humans present, consistent with the standing naming rule (never in front of humans, Azrael-only). God calls him "Luc."
- **Eve is the antagonist, absent from the page until Act Three.** **As of "Debrief," two narrow description exceptions now exist for her specifically** (added 2026-10-19, `bible/eve_character_profile.md`): she may be given explicit physical description (hair color: long, wavy black), and she's established to know multiple languages, surfacing on Earth as ordinary, mundane mix-ups — not the separate, climax-only divine language.

### 2026-10-02 additions (`bible/lucifer_character_profile.md` §§ FORM & SOUL / POWER SCOPE, and `bible/gabriel_character_profile.md`) — unchanged this pass, restated for completeness

- **FORM & SOUL.** Lucifer's **soul is uncreated** — permanently unalterable, beyond God's power to change or take, unconditionally. His **form** was a gift from God — a vessel, not a claim of ownership — and because it was given, it can be taken back. **Because his soul was never God's work, Lucifer has the greatest free will of any angel.**
- **Purity.** In his given form Lucifer **cannot sin, physically and literally.** Never write him straining against it. The underlying full-power-outside-the-body cosmology is explicitly **narratively inert, reference-only.**
- **POWER SCOPE.** Full power "almost on the same level as God" except creation from nothing — but that full power exists only outside his body/time/space. Inside his given form (always, on the page) he is **deliberately limited** — still enormously powerful, but beatable, which is explicitly why Azrael can hurt him in a fight. Power is still not knowledge.
- **Gabriel.** God's physical-arrangements-on-Earth angel. First effect (not physical appearance): the pre-built "Adrian Black" bank account in "Errands." Open questions unchanged — see § 7.
- **"Deals aren't sins."** Bribes/transactions are deals, not sins.

## 4. Standing Character-Voice Rules (apply to every chapter, not just the one that introduced them)

These live in `notes/character-synopsis.md` under "Standing habits" specifically so Writer — who never reads the bible — still sees them. Treat this section as load-bearing for any new chapter brief.

1. **Elizabeth/Lucifer friction is mandatory, not chemistry.** His caustic baseline is aimed at her as much as anyone; friction/open arguing is their default mode from the very first exchange; she stays in his orbit because she reads him as broken, not because she likes him yet. No warmth/attraction registering in his interiority toward her; no comfortable silences between them specifically. **As of "Ambush," reconfirmed in "Debrief," this friction has an explicit floor: she has stated outright that she really doesn't like him — treat this as the baseline going forward, not a feeling still being established.**
2. **Elizabeth has no lie-detection ability at all (correction fully closed as of "Ambush").** She asks pointed questions and watches for inconsistencies — an ordinary skeptical-questioning habit, a skill, not a perception power. All six locations, including `.claude/agents/agent-generic-writer.md`, carry the corrected wording.
3. **Not every character reads people.** Only **Elizabeth** (skeptical-questioning habit, not perceptiveness) and **Lucifer/Azrael reading each other** (both directions, six thousand years of proximity) get any analytical read on others. Nobody else — Sophie, James, Claire, Sam, Azrael's texted voice in "Debrief," any extra — catches subtext or reads a tell.
4. **Lucifer eats apples constantly, as a habit** — bored, frustrated, or simply in the mood are all equally valid reasons. He cannot set one down once picked up — must eat or destroy it. **FINAL as of "Twenty Feet" (2026-10-21/22): the ongoing habit genuinely IS Tree of Knowledge fruit, pulled fresh from the still-living, still-producing tree via a pocket-pull convention.** Tell: pocket-pull = real fruit; picked-up-from-wherever-it-already-was = ordinary apple. Both trigger the same compulsion. **His restraint around it is specifically about never giving a bite to a human** (his existing hard rule, dramatized consciously on the page for the first time in "Twenty Feet," not a new rule) — the separate finish-or-destroy compulsion is unaffected and continues regardless of which kind it is. "Errands" remains a deliberate, author-specified exception with no apple at all.
5. **No similes, no metaphors in narration — zero exceptions, EXCEPT as of 2026-10-22: Lucifer POV chapters now have a scoped exception** (see § 3's "2026-10-21/22 additions" and `bible/style-guide.md` § 2). Elizabeth's chapters remain under the original zero-tolerance ban, unchanged. Dialogue remains exempt regardless of POV.
6. **No explicit physical description** of hair color or eye color, for any character, ever — **except Eve, as of 2026-10-19, a narrow and deliberate exception scoped to her alone** (§ 3). No other character's description rules change.
7. **Lucifer is not all-knowing.** Unaffected by the POWER SCOPE cosmology — raw power is explicitly not knowledge.
8. **Divine/telepathic speech formatting:** italics, no quotation marks, for God's voice and any telepathic bond traffic — this formatting rule holds even inside the new Lucifer-POV prose permissions (italics are not available for ordinary dialogue emphasis, even there).
9. **The Sky-Glance:** Lucifer's silent upward glance at God's too-convenient timing — genuine annoyance, not shared amusement, Lucifer's POV only, no fixed numeric cap under the post-rework bible (the old pre-rework "2-3 uses total" rule no longer applies) — "rare and used precisely" is the only standing constraint. **Used again in "Twenty Feet"** (the exact 20-foot boundary reads to Lucifer as deliberate, not coincidental) — the first clean, wordless use since the rework; the orchard-landing outburst in "Why Did It Have to Be Apples" was a spoken line, not this gesture.
10. **Lucifer cannot sin or lie.** Not a temptation he resists — physically impossible inside his given form. Never dramatize him straining against it.
11. **Lucifer is always operating inside deliberate limits on his power while in his body.** He's powerful but beatable in a fight — the explicit reason Azrael can genuinely hurt him, reconfirmed hard in "Twenty Feet" (a full-strength punch, no restraint on Azrael's side).
12. **Gabriel handles God's physical arrangements on Earth** — the explanatory mechanism any future "someone already took care of X before Lucifer could" beat should use.
13. **Lucifer knows general human desire** (money, wants, how phones/institutions work) from ruling Hell — never a read on a specific individual.
14. **Lucifer does not carry his own phone, but physically operates the shared one.** Registered under "Adrian Black" — see § 5/§ 7 for the still-open naming question.
15. **Lucifer's subconscious eye-avoidance of Elizabeth** is an ongoing, occasionally-broken pattern, first broken on-page in "Ambush," now documented in the bible itself (§ 3). Neither he nor Elizabeth understands it; keep playing it as unresolved, not a single settled moment. Never describe her eyes if this recurs.
16. **New, "Twenty Feet": "Go big or go home" is a genuine, standing Lucifer trait** — excess is real and consistent whenever he builds/fixes/creates anything, even when something is meant to read as humble or quiet. A real, ongoing friction point with Azrael. Keep in mind for any future house-repair or creation scene.
17. **New, "Twenty Feet": the one-real-repair-per-day house rule** — paced deliberately, hidden from any local who might notice unnaturally fast work. Any future house-interior scene should track which rooms are and aren't fixed yet (currently: ballroom done; front door fixed as a prerequisite; everything else per "No Place Like Home"'s original tour still unrepaired).

## 9. Pipeline / Process Notes (current as of this rework — check `.claude/agents/agent-generic-director.md`'s Standing Rule before assuming any of this hasn't changed again)

- **Writer's context is deliberately tiny:** the author's short paragraph for the chapter, plus `notes/character-synopsis.md` — nothing else.
- **Revision is a single consolidated agent** doing continuity/voice-drift, soul/style fidelity, and fact-checking in one pass per chapter — findings only, never edits.
- **"Twenty Feet" is a notable process exception, worth remembering going forward:** it was not routed through the normal Writer → Revision pipeline for its prose register. The author worked a full stylistic rewrite directly with the Director (`notes/yarros-style-experiment.md`), iterating through several corrected passes (figurative-language density → architectural repetition of "the way" constructions → unnecessary continuity callbacks), and logged ongoing feedback separately in `notes/twenty-feet-revision-notes.md` as a live, in-progress document while working through the chapter. **That feedback log's own header still describes the chapter as "the weakest... not locked, not finalized" — this is now superseded; the chapter is locked.** Worth remembering: a notes file's own header/status line can go stale the same way a synopsis entry can (§ 9's "Ambush" lesson, below) — always check the actual current instruction (this turn's lock instruction, `bible/manuscript-order.md`'s own entry) before trusting an in-progress log's self-description.
- **"Ambush" remains a concrete demonstration of why the narrow-context trick needs active upkeep** — a synopsis entry can go stale the moment a chapter is hand-edited after the entry is first drafted; Revision checking the synopsis against the real chapter text (not just trusting it) is what caught that one.
- **No agent — Director included — rewrites or edits a chapter based on review findings.** Findings get reported; the author acts on them (or doesn't).
- **`chapters/morning.md` is permanently protected.**
- **The repo chapter file is not always the author's final prose** — style-only fixes may live only in the author's personal Doc.
- **Creative Partner** is active for ongoing chapter-planning brainstorming, but **"Twenty Feet" specifically bypassed it** for its prose-style work (see above) — the ballroom's three-attempt design content, however, WAS locked through the normal `notes/character-synopsis.md` Standing Habits route on 2026-10-21, ahead of the style experiment. Worth distinguishing: this chapter's *content* (what happens) came through the normal channel; its *prose register* did not.
- **This synthesis document itself went stale for one full cycle.** It had not been updated after "Debrief" (2026-10-19) before this pass began, despite the project's standing rule that Synthesis runs immediately on every chapter lock. This pass folds in both "Debrief" and "Twenty Feet" together as a catch-up. **Going forward, do not assume this file is current just because its header looks recent — cross-check its "Last updated after" line against `bible/manuscript-order.md`'s actual chapter list before relying on it**, the same caution this file already recommends applying to the character-synopsis file.
- **A new session should open by reading this file, then `bible/manuscript-order.md` for the next unwritten chapter's status.** As of this pass, there is no next-chapter paragraph to load yet (§ 2).
