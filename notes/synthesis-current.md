## 1. Orientation — What Kind of Project This Is Right Now

- **Concept:** Lucifer falls in love. He's sent to Earth to take Eve's escaped soul back to Hell. Angels try to stop him. Killing Eve will cost him Earth and the woman he's fallen for.

Not a shared future; not the first rework's redemption/joy arc.

Eve's path to Hell: ~800 years of mortal life, then death; because she remained faithful enough to God despite everything, her body went to Hell with her soul (an exception, not the norm).
- **Concept frozen at the author's direction — not up for debate or pushback.**

**What this explicitly replaces (now superseded — see § 3):** Lucifer having power from the start; the old secrecy-mandate framing (nothing to hide power-wise, since there's no power to hide for most of the book); Azrael's "contains but can't kill" role; the redemption-ending/joy-arc trajectory.

## 3. Locked Canon

### 3a. New, second-rework canon (2026-11-12) — authoritative over everything else

Full text: `bible/story-bible.md` § "SECOND MAJOR REWORK IN PROGRESS." Load-bearing summary:

- **Lucifer is mortal/powerless on Earth by default**, restrained by God; restraints break only at the climax. He fights well hand-to-hand regardless. He knows killing a human sends him to Hell — God told him this before the other angels even existed.
- **Lucifer's origin (reworked):** born outside time, like God; God told him what He was thinking before creating existence; Lucifer asked to experience existence, was given a true angelic form, which he shifted to have no flaws in his own eyes. He created the Tree of Knowledge of Good and Evil using divine power, without looking ahead to see it was incompatible with humans — he feels bad about this, not "learned a lesson." He was cast out and almost killed; he believes God sent him to Hell specifically because death was too great a price for what he did. **He hates humanity because of this** — a harder, less resolved framing than the first rework's "doesn't resent God, softens gradually" arc. (Whether he still "doesn't resent God" specifically is not restated either way in the new materials — don't assume either way.)
- **Lucifer believes Liz might love him for who he actually is.** She brings out the innocent, happy side of him from the Garden era.
- **Elizabeth (Liz):** hates him at first, falls in love over time. Watches him get attacked by angels repeatedly and "kill" them without hesitation. Nurses him after Azrael shatters his spine — she's the one who steps in during that fight, and her plea is what stops Azrael. **Learns he's the devil at the end of "Part 1"** (content/length of "Part 1" undefined).
- **Eve:** broke out of Hell, arrives on Earth after Lucifer. Lived ~800 years of mortal life, died, and — because she remained faithful enough to God despite everything — her body went to Hell with her soul (an exception, not the norm; this is what "took her body to Hell, so it never died" means). No remorse in her head, but her soul feels it — that's the weight that brought her to Hell; she reads this as "he still loves me, so he brought me to where he is." Still loves Lucifer but is angry he put her in this situation; blamed him after the Fall and told God to kill him. Hates Liz and tries to kill her to reunite with Lucifer.
- **Michael (NEW):** archangel, always jealous God seemed to love Lucifer more and that Lucifer had more power. **Does not change — no arc, no redemption.** Got revenge after Revelation by essentially expelling Lucifer from Heaven. Keeps attacking Lucifer; when he realizes he can't beat him, convinces Azrael to do it instead.
- **Azrael:** angel of death; originally the Garden's maintainer, loved that job. After Eve ate the fruit, death became possible; absorbing the pain of every human death took such power that God asked him — the strongest angel — to take the role. Went from a job he loved to eternal pain. Blames Lucifer for being too naive to create the tree. **Nearly kills Lucifer, shattering his spine; Liz's plea stops him and he disappears.** Tells Lucifer the truth about Adam at some point before Lucifer leaves.
- **Adam (NEW):** told God to deal with the consequences of guilt and souls, and asked that Lucifer be sent to Hell — this saved Lucifer's life. **Lucifer doesn't know this.**
- **World rules:** Lucifer can't come to Earth unless God sends him through a special portal. If Lucifer dies, he goes to Hell; killing a human also sends him to Hell. Angels can't die — their body dies, soul returns to Heaven, they come back with a new body. Angels can't kill humans — believed a literal inability — **what actually happens if one tries is left deliberately OPEN.** Only God can separate a soul from the body (exceptions exist, e.g. Mary). The author isn't discussing the supernatural elements further yet.
- **Plot spine:** Lucifer arrives, Eve isn't there yet. Angels attack repeatedly (Michael recurring); Lucifer "kills" them, they come back, he keeps humans out of the crossfire. Liz sees him as a man who kills without hesitating; they fall in love over time. Azrael arrives, nearly kills Lucifer; Liz stops him, nurses Lucifer, love takes hold. Eve arrives, hates Liz, injures Lucifer, takes Liz by force (knife to her throat), tells him where to meet her. **Climax:** Eve monologues, pushes Liz off a ledge; Lucifer breaks his restraints through love/fear of losing Liz, freezes her fall a foot from the ground, hunts Eve down, sets Liz safely down, kills Eve to take her back to Hell. **Ending:** Liz tells him she loves him through tears; his body fades; he's about to say it back but fully disappears — lost to Hell.
- **Themes:** guilt (Eve carries it as weight, Azrael as pain, Lucifer as feeling bad); being loved for who you are, not the flawless form; a mission whose completion costs the thing he wants most.
- **Decision log additions:** Lucifer is not the hero — he's sent to take Eve back. Angels are his siblings, respawn, and are the pressure through the middle; Michael is the recurring antagonist (no arc), Azrael the catalyst, Eve the climax. The ending is as above — Lucifer lost to Hell. Concept frozen — no more concept-level pushback.
