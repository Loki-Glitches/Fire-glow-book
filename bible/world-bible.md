## LOCATIONS

### Elizabeth and Sam's House ("Morning", "Routine", "Convergence", "Garden", "Dinner", "Roof")
- Elizabeth's bedroom: a nightstand by the bed, a full-length mirror on the closet door, a dresser.
- Kitchen: a stove — during a meal, two pots run at once (one at a rolling boil, one untouched until needed, "Dinner") — a counter with stools on the far side (**"Dinner": the counter is built to seat two, Elizabeth and Sam; extra stools are stored underneath and pulled out for a guest** — three crowded in for Lucifer's first dinner), a sink, a cutting board (used as a bread-prep station, "Dinner"). A postcard is stuck to the fridge with a lemon-shaped magnet (from Elizabeth and Sam's parents, traveling — "some island," per Sam) — **as of "Dinner", its corners have gone soft from being taken down and put back more times than a postcard should survive.** **"Breakfast" (post-rework, 2026-09-11):** a new one from the same parents arrives, postmarked London (red phone booth image), joining a small existing collection already crowding that corner of the fridge — same pattern, short/unsigned message, no elaboration given. Confirms this is an ongoing, recurring correspondence rather than a one-off detail.
- Porch: has an outdoor light that burned out and stayed dead for over a week as of "Routine" through "Sighting" (Elizabeth buys the replacement bulb in "Sighting", but it's still sitting unused on the porch step, in its cardboard sleeve, as of "Convergence" — she hasn't put it in yet). **Locked (2026-09-04): the bulb does not get installed for a long time.** This is a deliberate long-running thread, not an oversight — see `bible/story-bible.md` § "The Porch Light" for the payoff. No chapter before then should have Elizabeth, Sam, or anyone else casually fix it; it needs to still be dead when its moment comes. **Resolved (2026-09-05):** a "plastic sleeve" wording was flagged mid-session but no longer appears in either file as of the current text — `chapters/garden.md` reads "cardboard sleeve" and `chapters/dinner.md` reads just "sleeve" (no material stated, not a contradiction). This was almost certainly a stale read against an intermediate draft during a parallel review pass. "Cardboard sleeve" remains the standing canonical detail. The top step's edge has at least one loose splinter ("Dinner" — Elizabeth works it loose with a finger while sitting there), consistent with the porch's already-established worn, older-house character. **Confirmed still dead and unbulbed in "Roof"** ("The porch light was still dead. The bulb was still in its sleeve by her foot").
- **The porch planter (established "Roof"):** poured concrete, chipped at one lip, sits at a corner of the porch. Heavy enough that it normally takes two people and a dolly to move — which is why Sam has been meaning to relocate it for two summers and never has. Elizabeth estimates its weight aloud as "four hundred pounds of concrete" (her own guess in the moment, not a verified figure — treat as color, not a locked spec). Lucifer lifts it single-handed in "Roof" as his first on-page Power Bond strength demonstration (see SUPERNATURAL EFFECTS § "Power Bond Strength") and carries it a few feet over, out of the walking path. **As of the end of "Roof" it is still in that displaced spot** — the chapter's closing lines reference "the planter still sitting where he'd left it" as Lucifer heads out. A future chapter reusing this porch should show it in the moved position unless something in-story moves it back on the page.
- **The porch roof (established "Roof"):** accessed for the first time on-page here — Elizabeth had been up once before, at twelve ("Sam nearly had a heart attack about it"). The porch overhang blocks the sky from ground level, cutting off sunsets "right where it's getting good"; from the roof, nothing blocks it — the view opens over the yards and fence lines to the tree line, with the sunset's color deepening from orange toward red at the horizon. The shingles hold the day's heat into evening, warm enough to sit on directly through clothing (see RECURRING SENSORY MOTIFS § "Evening porch heat" — this extends the same motif upward). There is no ladder to the roof; access in "Roof" is by Power Bond–assisted boost/climb only (see SUPERNATURAL EFFECTS).
- Exterior ("Convergence"): a covered porch, two steps up from the front walk, in a stretch of the street where houses are better-spaced with older trees. Located roughly ten minutes' walk past the town green, on the far side of town from Main Street.
- **The street immediately outside the house ("Garden"):** a mailbox at the end of Elizabeth's front walk; a fire hydrant two houses down; a neighbor's fence with a gate (audible when it swings shut); a dog belonging to a neighbor two yards over, quick to bark at anything unusual on the street (settles once the unusual thing stops surprising it). Established while Lucifer and Azrael paced out the Travel Bond's ~20-foot range along this stretch — the mechanic itself is documented in `bible/bond_system_reference.md` § "Travel Bond Range," not here.
- **The sidewalk outside the house at night ("Rhythm"):** by full dark the color is gone from the sky except a last band of rust low over a neighboring roofline before it goes flat blue-grey — this is the specific sunset-fade beat for a night walk-up, distinct from the porch-roof sunset in "Roof" (which is a daytime-to-dusk sequence watched head-on; this one is glimpsed from a kitchen window and is already almost finished when Elizabeth notices it). Use for future evening arrivals at this house.

### The Town ("Routine", "Sighting", "Convergence")
Small town, everyone knows everyone. Summer heat is a running condition — triple digits, the kind that makes a parked car an oven, and heat-shimmer visible down Main Street.
- **The pharmacy:** smells like cough drops and the plastic of an unused blood-pressure cuff. Has a service counter. Staffed by an unnamed pharmacist who calls Elizabeth "hon" and knows Sam ("Sighting").
- **The hardware store:** small, usually near-empty. Has at least two aisles, pegs for hanging tools (hooks), and bins for bulkier goods (tarps, magnets). A teenage employee restocks shelves there.
- **The bakery:** warm, oven-adjacent heat, smells of butter and yeast. Menu hasn't changed in three years. Limited seating — one table reliably has shade. The door sticks and needs a shoulder to open.
- **Main Street ("Convergence"):** two blocks of storefronts before opening onto the town green. Includes a long-closed shoe repair (closed longer than it was ever open) and a school whose lot sits empty for the summer.
- **The bookstore on Fifth Street ("Rhythm", referenced only — not yet visited on-page):** established via Elizabeth's dialogue only, no physical description of the store itself yet. She and Sophie have had a small standing discount there since childhood — partly loyalty (they hung around for years without buying much), partly Sophie's knack for charming retail staff. First on-page mention of this street name; if a future chapter is actually set inside the store, it needs its own physical rendering (layout, smell, lighting) — nothing beyond the discount and the street name is locked yet.
- **The town green / park ("Convergence", "Garden", "Rhythm"):** center of town, past the two blocks of Main Street. Grass, a scatter of benches, a fountain that stopped working before any of the four leads arrived and was never fixed — cause unknown; Lucifer explicitly has no theory about it (2026-09-04: he is not all-knowing, and should never be written diagnosing something he has no way of knowing). A coffee shop sits just beyond the green — hand-lettered sign, door propped open in the heat, reputation as "the good coffee" locally. **A woman runs a painting class out of the park on weekends** (seeds Lucifer's park class, per style guide § 8). This is also the park referenced for "Park" and the wedding site referenced later in the story bible ("Wedding") — same location, keep consistent. "Garden" reuses the same dry fountain and benches without contradiction; kids with a ball and a jogger with a dog are the ambient extras present that afternoon.
  - **Entrance gate ("Rhythm"):** the park has a gate at its main entrance, close enough to Elizabeth's house to reach on foot in a couple of minutes ("two streets over").
  - **Lamps along the main path ("Rhythm"):** come on together at dusk, but not quite simultaneously — "the first one, then the next, then the whole line of them at once," catching the dark before it settles. Mundane town infrastructure, not a supernatural effect (the unit plan for "Rhythm" explicitly flags this as "atmospheric, not supernatural" — keep it that way in any future evening park scene; no glow, flicker, or effect beyond an ordinary streetlamp timer/sensor). The lamps thin out toward the park's far end, where the trees thicken — that far stretch stays dim even after the main-path lamps are lit.
  - **The fountain, reconfirmed ("Rhythm"):** referred to in-scene as "dry for the season" — this is Elizabeth's own passing gloss, not a new authorial fact about *why* it's dry. It does not contradict the established "stopped working, cause unknown, never fixed" detail from "Convergence"/"Garden": nobody in this town actually knows the real reason, so a local's casual assumption that it's just off "for the season" is consistent characterization, not a competing explanation. **Keep this distinction in future chapters** — other characters can voice their own guesses about the fountain, but the narration itself should never confirm a cause.
  - **The swing set ("Rhythm"):** established as a real, specific childhood object — Elizabeth and Sophie had "a whole system for the swings that made no sense to anyone but us." On the page in "Rhythm," Elizabeth "looked around for the swing set, half-expecting it to still be exactly where it had been" — the text does not confirm whether it's still there or where. **Open item, not yet locked:** the next park-set chapter ("Class," "Ready," or "Painting," per this chapter's own handoff) should resolve this one way or the other — either the swing set is still standing (and gets a physical placement, e.g. near the far tree line or by the entrance) or it's gone and that absence gets a beat. Don't let a future chapter silently assume either answer; make a call and lock it on the page.
- **The church ("Convergence"):** bell rings ten minutes fast on purpose, because the pastor likes being early.

### Hell — The Throne Room ("Breach")
Cold, always. At its center stands the Tree of Knowledge, gold to the root, branches holding ruby fruit that had gone untouched for an age until Lucifer eats one. Lucifer's throne sits with its back to the tree; a dais at its foot is where Sebastian reports from. Beyond the throne room's doors, down the mountain's flank, Hell extends in every direction — dark, close, lit only by leaking cell doors (suffering is the realm's sole light source, by design).

### Hell — The Corridors ("Breach")
The oldest cells in the realm are in the lowest corridor, under the mountain. Cell doors glow low and steady, dimming as Lucifer passes. Corridors are numbered (at least up to forty-two as of "Breach"). Brimstone dust was found pooling in the low corridor — powdered fine, about a handspan deep across ten yards — shortly before Eve's escape; the door there later turns to ash rather than breaking.

## SUPERNATURAL EFFECTS

*New category, seeded 2026-09-05 alongside the World Builder agent's expanded charter. Each entry is the established SENSORY rendering of an effect — what it looks/sounds/feels like on the page. The MECHANICAL rule (trigger, unlock conditions, constraints) lives in `bible/bond_system_reference.md` and is cross-referenced, not restated, here.*

### Forced Interrealm Transport ("Thrown") — "Breach", "Arrival"
God is the only one who can move Lucifer between realms (see `bible/bond_system_reference.md`). On the page this reads as violent and involuntary — "Arrival": Lucifer is thrown through a portal into the orchard and lands hitting an apple tree shoulder-first, with no control over the landing. Keep any future forced-transport moment in this same register: impact, no grace, no control — never a clean or dignified arrival.

### Travel Bond Involuntary Pull ("Thrown" to Azrael) — "Garden" filler scene
Distinct from the interrealm version above but deliberately using the same "thrown" vocabulary. Established rendering: no falling sensation on the first, surprise instance — "distance collapsing all at once," the surroundings going past faster than a single moment can hold, ending in a hard landing (palms and a knee taking the impact, later instances the same). Subsequent deliberate tests read as a binary event from the outside — present, then simply gone, "one foot still lifted mid-stride" — with the landing-impact detail preserved every time. Full mechanical rule: `bible/bond_system_reference.md` § "Travel Bond Range." Keep future instances of this specific effect consistent with: (1) it's abrupt with no visible transition, (2) it always ends in an impact/landing, not a smooth stop, (3) no supernatural glow, light, or sound effect has been established — it is felt and physically landed, not seen by an observer.

**Flag re: "Rhythm" (not added as a new instance — see world-notes-12.md):** at the end of "Rhythm," Lucifer is on-page one moment and simply gone the next while Elizabeth's back is turned for a couple of seconds at her own front door — "present, then simply gone" phrasing that reads very close to this effect's established observer-side grammar. Per `bible/bond_system_reference.md` § "Travel Bond Range," the pull-to-Azrael fallback should only trigger when *no* Travel-Bonded person is within ~20 feet of Lucifer — Elizabeth (Travel-Bonded since "Park") is standing at arm's length when it happens, which would mechanically block this exact fallback. The chapter itself never confirms a cause — Elizabeth rationalizes it as him "just moving fast" — so this has not been treated as a confirmed new instance of the effect and no catalog entry has been added for it. Flagged to the Director/Continuity Checker to confirm intent: either this is meant as deliberate, unexplained mystery (fine as written, but worth a conscious choice) or it's an accidental echo of a mechanic that shouldn't be able to fire here.

### Power Bond Strength — "Roof"
First shown on the page in "Roof", in two instances (lifting the porch planter; boosting Elizabeth to the roof, then following her up unassisted). Elizabeth's Power Bond formed back at the end of "Park", but per `bible/bond_system_reference.md` §§ "STAGE 3 — POWER BOND" and "BOND TIER AWARENESS," Lucifer had no way of knowing the ability was available until he actually tried it here — both uses in "Roof" should read as genuine private uncertainty resolved the instant they work, not a confident display. Established sensory rendering:
- **Effortless, with no visible strain.** No grunting, no bracing-and-heaving, no buildup of effort during the act itself. The only tell of anything unusual is a beat of hesitation *before* he commits to it ("only a fraction longer than the task should have needed. Nothing in his face said why") — the hesitation belongs to his private uncertainty, not to physical exertion.
- **Grounded, leverage-based verbs and similes only** — "crouched," "got his hands under the lip," "lifted it clean off," "came up... in a single, unhurried motion, no more effort than stepping over a fence," "dropped to the porch boards." Comparisons are drawn from ordinary ground-level physical acts, never from flight, floating, gliding, or soaring.
- **Fast and immediately underplayed.** The act itself takes a beat, not a scene; he moves on from it or minimizes it in dialogue ("It's not much") rather than lingering on or narrating the feat.
- **No accompanying visual or sound effect** — no glow, shimmer, wind, or noise marks the strength as supernatural. The only evidence is the result (a several-hundred-pound object relocated one-handed; a grown woman lifted to a roofline in one motion) and the other bonded character's reaction to it, not anything visible in the air or on his body.
- **HARD CONSTRAINT (locked with the "Roof" unit plan):** this ability must never read as flight. No floating, soaring, gliding, hovering, or airborne language — even in simile — for Power Bond strength scenes. Wings/flight are a separate, much later unlock ("Wings" "Wings"). "Roof"'s own text stays cleanly on the leverage/ground side of that line; hold every future strength scene to the same register.
- Cross-reference: mechanical unlock conditions, the two-condition disclosure requirement, and the Bond Tier Awareness rule governing his uncertainty going in are documented in `bible/bond_system_reference.md` §§ "STAGE 3 — POWER BOND" and "BOND TIER AWARENESS." This entry covers only the sensory rendering.

## RECURRING SENSORY MOTIFS

- **Hell:** cold, always. Ash-gray as a dominant color/texture (the ledger, crumbling doors). Suffering is the realm's only light source — cell doors leak light where souls are in treatment.
- **The town in summer:** oppressive heat, triple digits, "an oven with a door handle" for cars. Used consistently as ambient texture in "Routine" and "Sighting" — keep using it through the summer-set chapters rather than letting the weather go unmentioned.
- **The bakery:** oven-adjacent warmth specifically distinguished from the outdoor heat — it's a "worth it anyway" warmth, not an oppressive one.
- **Evening porch heat ("Dinner"):** a softer variant of the general summer-heat motif — the day's heat lingers on the porch after the sun is down, but reads as pleasant rather than oppressive once direct sun is gone. Distinct from the daytime "oven" texture; use this version specifically for evening porch scenes. **"Roof" extends this motif upward to the porch roof** — the shingles hold the day's heat into evening, warm enough to sit on directly through clothing; same pleasant, not-oppressive register as the porch below it.
- **Night settling ("Rhythm"):** a new, distinct beat from the daytime heat and the evening-porch-warmth motifs above — the sky goes from a last thin band of rust to flat blue-grey quickly once evening arrives, and streetlamps/park lamps visibly "catch the dark before it can really settle." Use this for any scene set after full dark rather than reusing the daytime heat-motif language.
