## LOCATIONS

### Elizabeth and Sam's House (Ch.1, Ch.3, Ch.6, Ch.9, Ch.10, Ch.11)
- Elizabeth's bedroom: a nightstand by the bed, a full-length mirror on the closet door, a dresser.
- Kitchen: a stove — during a meal, two pots run at once (one at a rolling boil, one untouched until needed, Ch.10) — a counter with stools on the far side (**Ch.10: the counter is built to seat two, Elizabeth and Sam; extra stools are stored underneath and pulled out for a guest** — three crowded in for Lucifer's first dinner), a sink, a cutting board (used as a bread-prep station, Ch.10). A postcard is stuck to the fridge with a lemon-shaped magnet (from Elizabeth and Sam's parents, traveling — "some island," per Sam) — **as of Ch.10, its corners have gone soft from being taken down and put back more times than a postcard should survive.**
- Porch: has an outdoor light that burned out and stayed dead for over a week as of Ch.3–5 (Elizabeth buys the replacement bulb in Ch.5, but it's still sitting unused on the porch step, in its cardboard sleeve, as of Ch.6 — she hasn't put it in yet). **Locked (2026-09-04): the bulb does not get installed for a long time.** This is a deliberate long-running thread, not an oversight — see `bible/story-bible.md` § "The Porch Light" for the payoff. No chapter before then should have Elizabeth, Sam, or anyone else casually fix it; it needs to still be dead when its moment comes. **Resolved (2026-09-05):** a "plastic sleeve" wording was flagged mid-session but no longer appears in either file as of the current text — `chapters/ch09.md` reads "cardboard sleeve" and `chapters/ch10.md` reads just "sleeve" (no material stated, not a contradiction). This was almost certainly a stale read against an intermediate draft during a parallel review pass. "Cardboard sleeve" remains the standing canonical detail. The top step's edge has at least one loose splinter (Ch.10 — Elizabeth works it loose with a finger while sitting there), consistent with the porch's already-established worn, older-house character. **Confirmed still dead and unbulbed in Ch.11** ("The porch light was still dead. The bulb was still in its sleeve by her foot").
- **The porch planter (established Ch.11):** poured concrete, chipped at one lip, sits at a corner of the porch. Heavy enough that it normally takes two people and a dolly to move — which is why Sam has been meaning to relocate it for two summers and never has. Elizabeth estimates its weight aloud as "four hundred pounds of concrete" (her own guess in the moment, not a verified figure — treat as color, not a locked spec). Lucifer lifts it single-handed in Ch.11 as his first on-page Power Bond strength demonstration (see SUPERNATURAL EFFECTS § "Power Bond Strength") and carries it a few feet over, out of the walking path. **As of the end of Ch.11 it is still in that displaced spot** — the chapter's closing lines reference "the planter still sitting where he'd left it" as Lucifer heads out. A future chapter reusing this porch should show it in the moved position unless something in-story moves it back on the page.
- **The porch roof (established Ch.11):** accessed for the first time on-page here — Elizabeth had been up once before, at twelve ("Sam nearly had a heart attack about it"). The porch overhang blocks the sky from ground level, cutting off sunsets "right where it's getting good"; from the roof, nothing blocks it — the view opens over the yards and fence lines to the tree line, with the sunset's color deepening from orange toward red at the horizon. The shingles hold the day's heat into evening, warm enough to sit on directly through clothing (see RECURRING SENSORY MOTIFS § "Evening porch heat" — this extends the same motif upward). There is no ladder to the roof; access in Ch.11 is by Power Bond–assisted boost/climb only (see SUPERNATURAL EFFECTS).
- Exterior (Ch.6): a covered porch, two steps up from the front walk, in a stretch of the street where houses are better-spaced with older trees. Located roughly ten minutes' walk past the town green, on the far side of town from Main Street.
- **The street immediately outside the house (Ch.9):** a mailbox at the end of Elizabeth's front walk; a fire hydrant two houses down; a neighbor's fence with a gate (audible when it swings shut); a dog belonging to a neighbor two yards over, quick to bark at anything unusual on the street (settles once the unusual thing stops surprising it). Established while Lucifer and Azrael paced out the Travel Bond's ~20-foot range along this stretch — the mechanic itself is documented in `bible/bond_system_reference.md` § "Travel Bond Range," not here.

### The Town (Ch.3, Ch.5, Ch.6)
Small town, everyone knows everyone. Summer heat is a running condition — triple digits, the kind that makes a parked car an oven, and heat-shimmer visible down Main Street.
- **The pharmacy:** smells like cough drops and the plastic of an unused blood-pressure cuff. Has a service counter. Staffed by an unnamed pharmacist who calls Elizabeth "hon" and knows Sam (Ch.5).
- **The hardware store:** small, usually near-empty. Has at least two aisles, pegs for hanging tools (hooks), and bins for bulkier goods (tarps, magnets). A teenage employee restocks shelves there.
- **The bakery:** warm, oven-adjacent heat, smells of butter and yeast. Menu hasn't changed in three years. Limited seating — one table reliably has shade. The door sticks and needs a shoulder to open.
- **Main Street (Ch.6):** two blocks of storefronts before opening onto the town green. Includes a long-closed shoe repair (closed longer than it was ever open) and a school whose lot sits empty for the summer.
- **The town green / park (Ch.6, Ch.9):** center of town, past the two blocks of Main Street. Grass, a scatter of benches, a fountain that stopped working before any of the four leads arrived and was never fixed — cause unknown; Lucifer explicitly has no theory about it (2026-09-04: he is not all-knowing, and should never be written diagnosing something he has no way of knowing). A coffee shop sits just beyond the green — hand-lettered sign, door propped open in the heat, reputation as "the good coffee" locally. **A woman runs a painting class out of the park on weekends** (seeds Lucifer's park class, per style guide § 8). This is also the park referenced for Ch.8 and the wedding site referenced later in the story bible (Ch.34) — same location, keep consistent. Ch.9 reuses the same dry fountain and benches without contradiction; kids with a ball and a jogger with a dog are the ambient extras present that afternoon.
- **The church (Ch.6):** bell rings ten minutes fast on purpose, because the pastor likes being early.

### The Orchard (Ch.4)
Apple trees old enough that "his grandfather used to bring him here" (James, of himself). A quiet, out-of-the-way spot — private enough that James and Claire treat it as a personal spot, surprised to find anyone else there. Slopes down toward a fence, a road, and the town beyond. This is also where Lucifer is thrown through the portal and lands (hits an apple tree shoulder-first).

### Hell — The Throne Room (Ch.2)
Cold, always. At its center stands the Tree of Knowledge, gold to the root, branches holding ruby fruit that had gone untouched for an age until Lucifer eats one. Lucifer's throne sits with its back to the tree; a dais at its foot is where Sebastian reports from. Beyond the throne room's doors, down the mountain's flank, Hell extends in every direction — dark, close, lit only by leaking cell doors (suffering is the realm's sole light source, by design).

### Hell — The Corridors (Ch.2)
The oldest cells in the realm are in the lowest corridor, under the mountain. Cell doors glow low and steady, dimming as Lucifer passes. Corridors are numbered (at least up to forty-two as of Ch.2). Brimstone dust was found pooling in the low corridor — powdered fine, about a handspan deep across ten yards — shortly before Eve's escape; the door there later turns to ash rather than breaking.

Hell's record book. Ash-gray leather cover, pages the same gray, "pressed flat." Three columns: souls received, souls in active treatment, souls released (column three is always empty). Lucifer marked it before leaving for Earth — three words seared into the cover: LILLITH HAS COMMAND.

A band with an inscription: *"Cursed by love's cruel decree, forced to bear the weight for an eternity."* Present on him throughout the book. Distinct from James's engagement ring below — do not conflate the two.

### James's Ring (Ch.4)
A plain metal band, worn, with a stone missing from its setting long enough that the empty channel had gone dull. This is the ring James proposes to Claire with. **Corrected 2026-09-05: Lucifer does not repair this ring in Ch.4** — the missing stone stays missing, a live loose end for a later chapter. The original "fills the channel, changes the band to gold" description was a transfiguration effect, which isn't available to anyone until the emotional ladder's Calm stage unlocks (Ch.11) — it had no business happening in Ch.4 regardless of bond tier.

### Lucifer's Second Ring (Ch.4)
A plain band, unremarkable next to his own cursed/inscribed ring, worn on his other hand with no story attached (as far as the text discloses — origin not established). After Power Bond forms with James and Claire (mission disclosed, on top of the divine-nature disclosure already made), James demands proof; Lucifer works this ring off and reshapes it smaller by hand — a superhuman-strength effect, not transfiguration — and gives it to James "for the wedding." Distinct from James's own damaged ring above; do not conflate the two.

### The Picnic Basket (Ch.4)
James and Claire's basket, brought to the orchard: bread, a jar of something dark, fruit that "hadn't come from these trees," and a thermos of cheap coffee that "had clearly seen better decades."

### Sam's Dish Towel (Ch.9, Ch.10)
Kept over one shoulder while he cooks or answers the door mid-cooking — on him at the front door in Ch.9, at the stove and through the whole meal in Ch.10. A small recurring visual anchor for "Sam mid-cooking"; keep it present and consistent in future kitchen/dinner scenes where he's the one making the meal. **Note (Ch.11):** Elizabeth is separately shown drying her hands on, and later draping over the porch rail, a dish towel of her own after doing the dishes — a different towel, her own incidental prop, not a contradiction of Sam's. Not significant enough on its own to warrant a separate catalog entry unless it recurs.

## SUPERNATURAL EFFECTS

*New category, seeded 2026-09-05 alongside the World Builder agent's expanded charter. Each entry is the established SENSORY rendering of an effect — what it looks/sounds/feels like on the page. The MECHANICAL rule (trigger, unlock conditions, constraints) lives in `bible/bond_system_reference.md` and is cross-referenced, not restated, here.*

### Forced Interrealm Transport ("Thrown") — Ch.2, Ch.4
God is the only one who can move Lucifer between realms (see `bible/bond_system_reference.md`). On the page this reads as violent and involuntary — Ch.4: Lucifer is thrown through a portal into the orchard and lands hitting an apple tree shoulder-first, with no control over the landing. Keep any future forced-transport moment in this same register: impact, no grace, no control — never a clean or dignified arrival.

### Travel Bond Involuntary Pull ("Thrown" to Azrael) — Ch.9 filler scene
Distinct from the interrealm version above but deliberately using the same "thrown" vocabulary. Established rendering: no falling sensation on the first, surprise instance — "distance collapsing all at once," the surroundings going past faster than a single moment can hold, ending in a hard landing (palms and a knee taking the impact, later instances the same). Subsequent deliberate tests read as a binary event from the outside — present, then simply gone, "one foot still lifted mid-stride" — with the landing-impact detail preserved every time. Full mechanical rule: `bible/bond_system_reference.md` § "Travel Bond Range." Keep future instances of this specific effect consistent with: (1) it's abrupt with no visible transition, (2) it always ends in an impact/landing, not a smooth stop, (3) no supernatural glow, light, or sound effect has been established — it is felt and physically landed, not seen by an observer.

### Power Bond Strength — Ch.11
First shown on the page in Ch.11, in two instances (lifting the porch planter; boosting Elizabeth to the roof, then following her up unassisted). Elizabeth's Power Bond formed back at the end of Ch.8, but per `bible/bond_system_reference.md` §§ "STAGE 3 — POWER BOND" and "BOND TIER AWARENESS," Lucifer had no way of knowing the ability was available until he actually tried it here — both uses in Ch.11 should read as genuine private uncertainty resolved the instant they work, not a confident display. Established sensory rendering:
- **Effortless, with no visible strain.** No grunting, no bracing-and-heaving, no buildup of effort during the act itself. The only tell of anything unusual is a beat of hesitation *before* he commits to it ("only a fraction longer than the task should have needed. Nothing in his face said why") — the hesitation belongs to his private uncertainty, not to physical exertion.
- **Grounded, leverage-based verbs and similes only** — "crouched," "got his hands under the lip," "lifted it clean off," "came up... in a single, unhurried motion, no more effort than stepping over a fence," "dropped to the porch boards." Comparisons are drawn from ordinary ground-level physical acts, never from flight, floating, gliding, or soaring.
- **Fast and immediately underplayed.** The act itself takes a beat, not a scene; he moves on from it or minimizes it in dialogue ("It's not much") rather than lingering on or narrating the feat.
- **No accompanying visual or sound effect** — no glow, shimmer, wind, or noise marks the strength as supernatural. The only evidence is the result (a several-hundred-pound object relocated one-handed; a grown woman lifted to a roofline in one motion) and the other bonded character's reaction to it, not anything visible in the air or on his body.
- **HARD CONSTRAINT (locked with the Ch.11 unit plan):** this ability must never read as flight. No floating, soaring, gliding, hovering, or airborne language — even in simile — for Power Bond strength scenes. Wings/flight are a separate, much later unlock (Ch.68 "Wings"). Ch.11's own text stays cleanly on the leverage/ground side of that line; hold every future strength scene to the same register.
- Cross-reference: mechanical unlock conditions, the two-condition disclosure requirement, and the Bond Tier Awareness rule governing his uncertainty going in are documented in `bible/bond_system_reference.md` §§ "STAGE 3 — POWER BOND" and "BOND TIER AWARENESS." This entry covers only the sensory rendering.

### The Sky-Glance — Ch.4, Ch.9 filler
A single upward look, once per instance, no words, no internal monologue explaining it. Exists only in Lucifer's own POV and is never noticed or shared by another character (locked exclusivity, see `bible/style-guide.md` § "The Sky-Glance"). No visual effect beyond the look itself — this is a character beat, not a special effect with any sensory footprint of its own. Listed here mainly so a future World Builder pass doesn't try to invent one.

### Telepathic / Divine Speech — Ch.2 onward
Formatting only, not a visual effect: *italics, without quotation marks*. No described sound, glow, or sensation accompanies it on the page as of Ch.2–10 — it simply arrives as heard-not-spoken. If a future chapter wants to give it a sensory quality (a pressure, a temperature change, anything), that would be a new locked decision, not an assumption — flag to the Director before adding one.

### Hell's Ambient Light — Ch.2
Suffering is the realm's only light source, by design — cell doors leak light where souls are in active treatment, dimming as Lucifer passes them. No other light source has been established in Hell. Keep any future Hell-set scene consistent with this — no torches, no ambient glow from the Tree of Knowledge or the throne room beyond what's already described.

### Transfiguration Outputs (per Emotional Ladder stage) — Calm unlocks Ch.11, sensory rendering still not shown
Mechanically defined in `bible/lucifer_character_profile.md` § "EMOTIONAL LADDER": Calm → restorative/stabilizing forms; Beauty/Desire → aesthetic/attractive forms; Generosity → material abundance; Wrath → weapons/destructive constructs. **Ch.11 unlocks Calm on the emotional ladder itself** (per `bible/unit-plans.md` Ch.11: shown only through the smile, the relaxed stillness, the absence of his usual containment — deliberately not named or explained on the page) **but does not show the associated transfiguration output.** No object is created or transfigured in Ch.11; the unlock is emotional/behavioral only. **The sensory grammar for "restorative/stabilizing forms" therefore still does not exist as of Ch.11** — treat this exactly as before: when a future chapter first shows Lucifer actually producing a transfiguration in this register, that chapter sets the template every later stage's output will be compared against. Flag to the Director rather than improvising it solo.

## RECURRING SENSORY MOTIFS

- **Hell:** cold, always. Ash-gray as a dominant color/texture (the ledger, crumbling doors). Suffering is the realm's only light source — cell doors leak light where souls are in treatment.
- **The town in summer:** oppressive heat, triple digits, "an oven with a door handle" for cars. Used consistently as ambient texture in Ch.3 and Ch.5 — keep using it through the summer-set chapters rather than letting the weather go unmentioned.
- **The bakery:** oven-adjacent warmth specifically distinguished from the outdoor heat — it's a "worth it anyway" warmth, not an oppressive one.
- **Evening porch heat (Ch.10):** a softer variant of the general summer-heat motif — the day's heat lingers on the porch after the sun is down, but reads as pleasant rather than oppressive once direct sun is gone. Distinct from the daytime "oven" texture; use this version specifically for evening porch scenes. **Ch.11 extends this motif upward to the porch roof** — the shingles hold the day's heat into evening, warm enough to sit on directly through clothing; same pleasant, not-oppressive register as the porch below it.
