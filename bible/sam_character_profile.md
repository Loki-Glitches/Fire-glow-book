# SAM — Character Profile

*Companion document to the Master Story Bible. Sam was a thin, background-support character under the old outline (see the superseded fragment in `bible/story-bible.md` § 9) — he is being expanded into a real character in his own right as of 2026-11-05 (author). This file is authoritative for anything about him going forward.*

---

## WANT / NEED / LIE

**Want:** Elizabeth to get everything she wants in life.
**Need:** To get his own life back on track.
**The lie:** Lucifer is not good for Liz.

---

## BACKSTORY & CORE WOUND

Shares the family abandonment wound with Elizabeth (see `bible/elizabeth_character_profile.md`) — their parents won the lottery and became chronically absent, funding the essentials but disappearing for months at a time. Money stood in for presence.

**Their parents left the summer going into Sam's third year of college.** He left school to come home and take over as Elizabeth's caretaker. It's been two years since. Elizabeth — doing fine now, and fully aware of what it cost him — is actively trying to convince him to go back and finish his degree. He's resisting. This is a running, normal-life plot thread independent of the Lucifer/Eve plot: not a single scene, but a recurring low-grade tension between them, since neither one is actually wrong (she genuinely is fine; he genuinely gave up something real and isn't ready to admit it mattered).

**He has effectively stepped into the father-figure role of this book** — not performed or stated as such, but functionally: the one steady adult presence in Elizabeth's life, the one who stayed when their parents didn't.

He and Sophie are in a real relationship; he's in love with her, and Elizabeth knows it. Sam and Sophie stay solid through everything this book puts them through — if anything, stronger by the end than at the start, regardless of what happens with Sam/Elizabeth or Sam/Lucifer specifically.

---

## FRIENDSHIP WITH JAMES AND CLAIRE

Sam has known James and Claire since high school. They've since graduated (college) without him — he was the one who stayed behind. **The author wants James and Claire to start coming by the house** specifically to rebuild Sam's social life, which atrophied during his two years of caretaking. This is a texture/normalcy beat, not a plot mechanism — it gives Sam something of his own happening on the page beyond his caretaker role and his suspicion of Lucifer.

---

## RELATIONSHIP WITH ELIZABETH

More one-on-one scenes between them are wanted going forward, not just scenes that happen to include both of them. He's suspicious of Lucifer/Adrian from early on — genuine, well-intentioned distrust based on not knowing him, not malice — and that suspicion sharpens specifically as Elizabeth starts warming to Lucifer (see her own profile's warming-arc note). Once she and Lucifer actually start seeing each other, Sam turns actively protective and tries to separate them, which leads to real, repeated fights between him and Elizabeth — not one blowup, a pattern.

**The painting is his turning point on Adrian specifically.** The exact chapter/placement of "the painting" is not yet decided (do not ask when or try to place it) — but whenever it lands, seeing it is what starts Sam seeing how good Adrian actually is for Elizabeth. This softens his read on Adrian-the-person. It does **not** survive contact with the Act Three reveal below — see RELATIONSHIP WITH LUCIFER.

---

## RELATIONSHIP WITH LUCIFER / ADRIAN

Before any softening, Sam starts actively engineering time with Adrian — inviting him over for dinners, lunches, just reasons to be in the same room — specifically to get to know him and figure out what he's hiding. **Sam has a knack: he's good at asking Lucifer the exact right question to get a real answer out of him naturally**, without Lucifer feeling cornered or deflecting the way he does with people pushing directly. This is a skill, not luck — worth showing him succeed at it more than once.

The painting (see above) softens Sam's read on Adrian as a person/partner for Elizabeth.

**Act Three, Eve's arrival — the rupture.** Sam senses something in town has gone wrong even before he has specifics. Separately, he learns the truth: Adrian is Lucifer. This is not the same kind of doubt as "is this guy good enough for my sister" — it's a totally different order of fear, and it overrides whatever ground the painting had won back. **Sam fully pushes Lucifer away.** This also drives a wedge between Sam and Elizabeth, since she doesn't reject Lucifer the way Sam does.

**This does not get resolved by the end of the book.** There is no later scene where Sam comes back around, makes peace with it, or gives his blessing (an earlier version of the outline had him eventually telling Azrael he'd made peace with the relationship — that beat is cut entirely, author-confirmed 2026-11-05; see `bible/tristan_azrael_character_profile.md`'s "Disapproval of Elizabeth" section for the matching cut on Azrael's side). **The book ends with Lucifer and Elizabeth together, but genuinely estranged from Sam** — he disapproves to the end and is powerless to stop the relationship, same as Azrael. This is a deliberate, bittersweet note the book closes on, not a loose thread to tie off in revision. Sam and Sophie's relationship is explicitly unaffected by this rupture — it holds, and strengthens, independent of what's happening with Sam/Elizabeth.
